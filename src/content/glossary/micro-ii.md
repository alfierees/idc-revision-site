## Theory of the firm & costs

### Theory of the Firm
How firms choose inputs and output to maximise profit. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Production Function
$f(l,k)$ — output obtained from labour and capital; its shape (concave $\sqrt{l}$, Cobb-Douglas, linear) fixes the isoquants, RTS and marginal cost. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Cost Function
$C(q)$ — minimum cost to produce $q$ units. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Marginal Physical Product
Extra output from one more unit of an input, other inputs fixed. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Marginal Cost
$C'(x)$ — cost of one more unit of output; a competitive firm produces where $p = MC$, so MC is the supply curve (rising if $C$ is convex, constant under constant returns to scale). → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Profit Maximisation
Firm chooses $q^*$ where $MR = MC$; SOC requires $\Pi'' < 0$. → [[PS_01-Profit Maximisation & Properties of the Profit Function|PS 01]]

### Optimal Profit Function
$\Pi^*(w,p)$ — profit at the optimal $q^*$; homogeneous degree 1, increasing in $p$, decreasing in $w$. → [[PS_01-Profit Maximisation & Properties of the Profit Function|PS 01]]

### Homogeneity of Degree 1
$\Pi^*(tw,tp) = t\Pi^*(w,p)$; only relative prices matter for production decisions. → [[PS_01-Profit Maximisation & Properties of the Profit Function|PS 01]]

### Envelope Theorem
$\partial\Pi^*/\partial p = q^*$ (Hotelling's Lemma); derivative of value function w.r.t. a parameter equals partial derivative of objective. → [[PS_01-Profit Maximisation & Properties of the Profit Function|PS 01]]

### Isoquant
Set of input bundles $(l,k)$ producing the same output. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### RTS
Rate of Technical Substitution: $MP_l / MP_k$ — slope of isoquant. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Diminishing RTS
RTS decreases as $l$ rises along isoquant; implies convex isoquants and preference for input diversity. → [[PS_02-Isoquants, RTS & Properties of the Profit Function|PS 02]]

### Convex Isoquant
Isoquant bows inward; midpoint of two bundles on same isoquant lies on a higher isoquant. → [[PS_02-Isoquants, RTS & Properties of the Profit Function|PS 02]]

### Cobb-Douglas Production Function
$f(l,k)=l^\omega k^\varepsilon$; RTS $= (\omega/\varepsilon)(k/l)$; diminishing RTS, convex isoquant. → [[PS_02-Isoquants, RTS & Properties of the Profit Function|PS 02]]

### Quasilinear Production Function
$f = g(l) + k$ with $g$ concave: $MP_k = 1$ and $RTS = g'(l)$, so labour is fixed at the tangency level $l^*$ and output beyond $g(l^*)$ comes from capital (marginal cost then equals $p_k$). → [[PP_02-Production Economy, Price Floor & Ceiling, Exchange True-False (2023 Moed B)|PP 02]]

### Constant Returns to Scale
Scaling all inputs by $t$ scales output by $t$ (e.g. Cobb-Douglas with exponents summing to 1); cost is linear in output, $MC = AC$, competitive profit is zero, and scaling a production economy's resources by $t$ scales its PPF by $t$. → [[PS_03-Cost Minimisation & Properties of the Cost Function|PS 03]]

### Perfect Substitutes
$f=al+bk$; linear isoquants; corner solution using cheaper $MP/\text{price}$; bang-bang switching. → [[PS_03-Cost Minimisation & Properties of the Cost Function|PS 03]]

### Isocost Line
Set of input bundles with the same total cost. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Cost Minimisation
Choosing $(l,k)$ to minimise $p_l l + p_k k$ subject to $f(l,k)=q_0$. → [[PS_03-Cost Minimisation & Properties of the Cost Function|PS 03]]

### Tangency Condition
Interior cost min: RTS $= MP_l/MP_k = p_l/p_k$ (isocost tangent to isoquant). → [[PS_03-Cost Minimisation & Properties of the Cost Function|PS 03]]

### Conditional Input Demand
Cost-minimising input quantities $l(q_0), k(q_0)$ as functions of prices and output. → [[PS_03-Cost Minimisation & Properties of the Cost Function|PS 03]]

### Corner Solution
Optimum on a boundary ($l=0$ or $k=0$); arises when tangency is infeasible or violates non-negativity. → [[PS_03-Cost Minimisation & Properties of the Cost Function|PS 03]]

## Partial equilibrium & welfare

### Partial Equilibrium
Single-market equilibrium with other prices fixed. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Quasilinear Utility
$U=v(x)+y$; no income effects on $x$, so surplus areas are money-valued and additive. → [[PS_04-Partial Equilibrium, Welfare & Price Ceilings|PS 04]]

### Consumer Surplus
Area between demand curve and price. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]
Also in Micro 3 (Year 2, Sem 2).

### Firm Surplus
Area between price and supply (marginal cost) curve. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Social Welfare
$W(x)=v(x)-C(x)$; total value minus cost, maximised at the competitive quantity; in general equilibrium the planner's social welfare function $W(U_1,U_2)$, increasing in each agent's utility (also called the social-welfare function). → [[PS_04-Partial Equilibrium, Welfare & Price Ceilings|PS 04]]

### Deadweight Loss
Welfare loss from a policy distorting the equilibrium quantity. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Marginal Cost Pricing
Competitive firms set price equal to marginal cost ($p = MC$); with constant $MC$ supply is a flat line and profit is zero. → [[PS_04-Partial Equilibrium, Welfare & Price Ceilings|PS 04]]

### Price Ceiling
Legal maximum price; binding if below $p^*$; creates a shortage with quantity traded = supply (also called a maximum price or price cap). → [[PS_04-Partial Equilibrium, Welfare & Price Ceilings|PS 04]]

### Price Floor
Legal minimum price (also called a minimum price or a fixed price above equilibrium); binding if above $p^*$, then demand is the short side and quantity traded is demand at the floor. → [[PS_04-Partial Equilibrium, Welfare & Price Ceilings|PS 04]]

### Short-Side Rule
Under a binding price control the quantity traded is the smaller of demand and supply (also called the short side of the market): supply under a binding ceiling, demand under a binding floor. → [[PS_04-Partial Equilibrium, Welfare & Price Ceilings|PS 04]]

### Efficient Rationing
Assumption that scarce units go to the consumers who value them most (top of the demand curve), so $CS = \int (P^D - p)\,dx$; random queuing would give lower welfare. → [[PS_04-Partial Equilibrium, Welfare & Price Ceilings|PS 04]]

### Price Subsidy
Per-unit payment to buyers (also consumer subsidy): consumers pay $p - \Delta$, shifting demand up by $\Delta$; pushes quantity above the competitive level so $MC$ exceeds marginal value on the extra units and social welfare falls. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Revealed Preference
Argument bounding a value function by evaluating a new optimum at old parameters. → [[PS_04-Partial Equilibrium, Welfare & Price Ceilings|PS 04]]

## Exchange economy & general equilibrium

### General Equilibrium
Simultaneous equilibrium across all markets. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Exchange Economy
Economy with no production; agents trade endowments. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Edgeworth Box
Graphical tool showing all feasible allocations for two agents and two goods. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### MRS
Marginal Rate of Substitution; $-dy/dx$ along an indifference curve; equalised across agents at an efficient allocation. → [[PS_05-Pareto Efficiency, Dominance & Social Welfare|PS 05]]

### Local Nonsatiation
Near any bundle there is a strictly preferred bundle; used to construct Pareto improvements. → [[PS_05-Pareto Efficiency, Dominance & Social Welfare|PS 05]]

### Pareto Efficiency
Allocation where no agent can be made better off without making another worse off. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Pareto Dominance
$A$ dominates $B$ if weakly better for all agents and strictly better for at least one. → [[PS_05-Pareto Efficiency, Dominance & Social Welfare|PS 05]]

### Contract Curve
Set of all Pareto efficient allocations in the Edgeworth box. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Utility Possibility Frontier
Pairs $(U_1,U_2)$ achievable along the contract curve (also called the utility frontier); the planner picks the tangency with the highest social-welfare indifference curve. → [[PP_04-Perfect Substitutes, Exchange Equilibrium, Minimum Price & Social Planner (2024 Moed A)|PP 04]]

### Social Planner
Hypothetical decision-maker maximising a social welfare function over feasible allocations (e.g. a Cobb-Douglas $U_1^a U_2^{1-a}$); compared with the market equilibrium, and able to implement its choice by redistributing endowments. → [[PS_06-Exchange Equilibrium, Walrasian Prices & the Social Planner|PS 06]]

### Walras' Law
If one market clears, all other markets also clear. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Excess Demand
$z$ = demand minus supply (endowment) of a good; by Walras' Law $p_x z_x + p_y z_y = 0$, so at non-equilibrium positive prices both goods have non-zero excess demand (a price above equilibrium gives excess supply). → [[PP_02-Production Economy, Price Floor & Ceiling, Exchange True-False (2023 Moed B)|PP 02]]

### Walrasian Equilibrium
Price ratio + allocation where each agent maximises utility on the budget line through their endowment and all markets clear; equivalently "competitive equilibrium". → [[PS_06-Exchange Equilibrium, Walrasian Prices & the Social Planner|PS 06]]

### Numeraire
Good whose price is fixed at 1 (e.g. $p_y = 1$) so that only relative prices matter; under quasilinear utility the linear good plays this role. → [[PS_09-Corner Solutions, Quasilinear Utility & Production Economy Prices|PS 09]]

### Price Indeterminacy
With constant-returns technologies and a corner solution (or a zero-endowment agent) a whole range of price vectors clears all markets and gives zero profit, so the price is not pinned down although the real allocation is. → [[PS_07-Core, Corner Endowments & Equilibrium vs the Social Planner|PS 07]]

### Core
Allocations that are both Pareto efficient and individually rational; the slice of the contract curve inside the lens of indifference curves through the endowment; every Walrasian equilibrium lies here. → [[PS_07-Core, Corner Endowments & Equilibrium vs the Social Planner|PS 07]]

### Individual Rationality
No agent ends up worse than at their own endowment ($U_i \ge U_i(\omega_i)$); holds at any equilibrium because the endowment is always affordable. → [[PS_07-Core, Corner Endowments & Equilibrium vs the Social Planner|PS 07]]

### First Welfare Theorem
Every competitive equilibrium allocation is Pareto efficient. → [[Lec_04-Theory of the Firm, Partial & General Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Second Welfare Theorem
Any Pareto efficient allocation is achievable via redistribution of endowments. → [[Lec_05-Production Economy & Externalities|Lec 05]]

### Lump-Sum Tax
Tax independent of the agent's choices, so it does not distort margins; the right tool for redistribution (Second Welfare Theorem) but useless for correcting an externality. → [[Lec_05-Production Economy & Externalities|Lec 05]]
Also in Micro 3 (Year 2, Sem 2).

## Production economy

### Production Economy
GE model with firms producing goods from inputs; efficiency requires RTS₁=RTS₂. → [[Lec_05-Production Economy & Externalities|Lec 05]]

### Efficiency Locus
Set of Pareto efficient input allocations; all points where firms' isoquants are tangent. → [[Lec_05-Production Economy & Externalities|Lec 05]]

### PPF
Production Possibility Frontier — all efficient $(x,y)$ output combinations. → [[Lec_05-Production Economy & Externalities|Lec 05]]

### RPT
Rate of Product Transformation — opportunity cost of $X$ in terms of $Y$ on the PPF. → [[Lec_05-Production Economy & Externalities|Lec 05]]

## Externalities

### Externalities
Effect of one agent's choices on another's utility/cost, not reflected in prices. → [[Lec_05-Production Economy & Externalities|Lec 05]]

### Market Failure
Situation where competitive equilibrium is not Pareto efficient. → [[Lec_05-Production Economy & Externalities|Lec 05]]
Also in Micro 3 (Year 2, Sem 2).

### Pigouvian Tax
Per-unit tax on a negative externality set equal to marginal external harm. → [[Lec_05-Production Economy & Externalities|Lec 05]]

### Marginal External Damage
Extra cost or harm that one unit of an agent's activity imposes on another at the margin; the Pigouvian tax equals it at the efficient allocation. → [[PS_10-Externalities in General Equilibrium & Public Goods|PS 10]]

### Tradable Permits
Property right over units of the externality (e.g. noise) traded at price $r$; the permit price plays the role of the Pigouvian tax and the initial split of permits changes who is paid, not the quantities (Coase logic). → [[PS_10-Externalities in General Equilibrium & Public Goods|PS 10]]

### Coase Theorem
With well-defined property rights and low transaction costs, markets achieve efficiency. → [[Lec_05-Production Economy & Externalities|Lec 05]]

## Public goods

### Public Goods
Good whose aggregate quantity affects every agent's utility (national security, a pool, a joint seminar paper); an externality hitting many agents at once, which causes under-provision and free riding. → [[Lec_06-Public Goods & Altruism|Lec 06]]

### Free Riding
An agent invests less (or nothing) because other agents' investment already gives her the benefit; the agent who wants the good more provides it all. → [[Lec_06-Public Goods & Altruism|Lec 06]]

### Under-Provision
The market provides a public good at too low a level: in Nash equilibrium $V_1' + V_2' > 1$ at the equilibrium total, so $g^e < g^*$ and the gap grows with the number of agents. → [[Lec_06-Public Goods & Altruism|Lec 06]]

### Nash Equilibrium
Choices where each agent's contribution is her best response to the others' and no one wants to deviate; models the free market for a public good. → [[Lec_06-Public Goods & Altruism|Lec 06]]
Also in Micro 3 (Year 2, Sem 2).

### Best Response
Agent's optimal choice given the others' choices; for a public good $BR_1(g_2) = \max(g_1^o - g_2,\,0)$, where $g_1^o$ solves $V_1' = 1$. → [[Lec_06-Public Goods & Altruism|Lec 06]]
Also in Micro 3 (Year 2, Sem 2).

### Marginal Social Benefit
Sum of all agents' marginal benefits from a public good; the planner provides it until $V_1' + V_2' =$ marginal cost (1). → [[Lec_06-Public Goods & Altruism|Lec 06]]

### Altruistic Utility
$V_1(g) + y_1 + \alpha\,(V_2(g) + y_2)$ with $\alpha \le 1$: the agent internalises part of the other's utility; raises provision, and $\alpha = 1$ restores the social optimum. → [[Lec_06-Public Goods & Altruism|Lec 06]]
