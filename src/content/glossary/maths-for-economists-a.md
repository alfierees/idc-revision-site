# Maths A Concepts

> One-stop glossary for Maths for Economists A (Year 1, Semester 1). Every concept link across the subject points to a heading in this file. ## Functions and Domain

### Function
A rule that gives each input exactly one output. Also in [[PS_01-Functions, Domain and Piecewise Graphs|PS 01]]. → [[Lec_01-Functions, Domain and Sigma Notation|Lec 01]]

### Domain
The set of inputs for which the function is defined, found by excluding division by zero, even roots of negatives and logarithms of non-positives. Also in [[PS_01-Functions, Domain and Piecewise Graphs|PS 01]]. → [[Lec_01-Functions, Domain and Sigma Notation|Lec 01]]

### Minimal Range
The set of outputs the function actually produces, as opposed to a larger set it could be said to map into. → [[Lec_01-Functions, Domain and Sigma Notation|Lec 01]]

### Function Notation
Writing $f(x)$ for the output of the rule $f$ at the input $x$. → [[Lec_01-Functions, Domain and Sigma Notation|Lec 01]]

### Vertical Line Test
A graph is the graph of a function exactly when no vertical line meets it more than once. → [[Lec_01-Functions, Domain and Sigma Notation|Lec 01]]

### Cube Root Domain
A cube root is defined for every real number, so it adds no restriction to the domain (unlike a square root). → [[PS_01-Functions, Domain and Piecewise Graphs|PS 01]]

## Function Types and Graphs

### Linear Function
A function of the form $y=ax+b$ whose graph is a straight line with constant slope. Also in [[Lec_02-Piecewise Functions and Business Applications|Lec 02]]. → [[Lec_01-Functions, Domain and Sigma Notation|Lec 01]]

### Slope
The steepness of a line, $m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$, used to find the equation of a linear function from two points. → [[Lec_02-Piecewise Functions and Business Applications|Lec 02]]

### Quadratic Function
A function $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$ whose graph is a parabola. → [[Lec_02-Piecewise Functions and Business Applications|Lec 02]]

### Parabola
The graph of a quadratic function, with its vertex at $x=-\frac{b}{2a}$. → [[Lec_02-Piecewise Functions and Business Applications|Lec 02]]

### Absolute Value Function
The function $|g|$, equal to $g$ where $g\ge 0$ and to $-g$ where $g<0$; it is a piecewise function that splits where $g=0$ and reflects the negative part of the graph above the x axis. Also in [[Lec_04-Limits and Continuity|Lec 04]]. → [[Lec_02-Piecewise Functions and Business Applications|Lec 02]]

### Piecewise Function
A function defined by different formulas on different intervals; the join points need separate checks for limits, continuity and differentiability. Also in [[Lec_02-Piecewise Functions and Business Applications|Lec 02]], [[Lec_04-Limits and Continuity|Lec 04]]. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_01-Functions, Domain and Sigma Notation|Lec 01]]

### Step Function
A piecewise-constant function that jumps at its breakpoints, such as a telephone charge billed per started minute. → [[Lec_02-Piecewise Functions and Business Applications|Lec 02]]

### Revenue Function
Revenue as price times quantity sold, written as a function of price or of quantity. Also in [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]]. → [[Lec_02-Piecewise Functions and Business Applications|Lec 02]]

### Cost Function
The production cost written as a function of quantity or price. → [[Lec_02-Piecewise Functions and Business Applications|Lec 02]]

### Profit Function
Revenue minus cost; its maximum is found where the marginal functions balance. → [[Lec_02-Piecewise Functions and Business Applications|Lec 02]]

### Break-Even
The price or quantity at which revenue equals cost, so profit is zero. → [[PS_02-Absolute Values, Business Functions and Exponential Equations|PS 02]]

## Transformations and Exponentials

### Composition of Functions
The function $f(g(x))$: apply $g$ first, then feed its output into $f$; the order matters. Also in [[Lec_08-Chain Rule, Exponential Derivatives and Integrals|Lec 08]]. → [[Lec_03-Composition, Transformations, Exponentials and Limits|Lec 03]]

### Vertical Translation
The graph of $y=f(x)+c$ is the graph of $f$ shifted up by $c$ units (down if $c$ is negative). → [[Lec_03-Composition, Transformations, Exponentials and Limits|Lec 03]]

### Horizontal Translation
The graph of $y=f(x+c)$ is the graph of $f$ shifted $c$ units to the left, and $f(x-c)$ shifts it $c$ units to the right. → [[Lec_03-Composition, Transformations, Exponentials and Limits|Lec 03]]

### Reflection of a Graph
The graph of $f(-x)$ flips $f$ across the y axis and the graph of $-f(x)$ flips it across the x axis. → [[Lec_03-Composition, Transformations, Exponentials and Limits|Lec 03]]

### Exponential Function
A function $y=a^x$ with $a>0$; it passes through $(0,1)$, grows for $a>1$ and decays for $0<a<1$, with the x axis as a horizontal asymptote. Also in [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]]. → [[Lec_03-Composition, Transformations, Exponentials and Limits|Lec 03]]

## Limits and Continuity

### Limit
The value $f(x)$ approaches as $x$ approaches a point, whatever the value at the point itself; it exists only if the left and right limits agree. Also in [[Lec_03-Composition, Transformations, Exponentials and Limits|Lec 03]]. → [[Lec_04-Limits and Continuity|Lec 04]]

### One-Sided Limit
The value $f(x)$ approaches as $x$ nears a point from the left only or from the right only. → [[Lec_04-Limits and Continuity|Lec 04]]

### Direct Substitution
Plugging the point into the formula to find a limit, valid where the function is continuous. → [[Lec_04-Limits and Continuity|Lec 04]]

### Conjugate
The same expression with the sign flipped on a root term; multiplying by it clears the root in a limit. → [[PS_04-Limits and Continuity|PS 04]]

### Indeterminate Form
A form such as $\frac{0}{0}$, $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$, $\infty-\infty$ or $1^{\infty}$ whose value is not fixed until the expression is rewritten. Also in [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]]. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_05-Infinite Limits|Lec 05]]

### Infinite Limit
A limit where $f(x)$ grows without bound as $x$ approaches a point, recorded as $+\infty$ or $-\infty$. → [[Lec_05-Infinite Limits|Lec 05]]

### Limit at Infinity
The value (or $\pm\infty$) that $f(x)$ approaches as $x\to\pm\infty$. → [[Lec_05-Infinite Limits|Lec 05]]

### Leading Term
The highest-degree term of a polynomial, which dominates the behaviour for large $|x|$. → [[Lec_05-Infinite Limits|Lec 05]]

### Continuity
A function is continuous at a point when $f(a)$ is defined, the limit exists and the limit equals $f(a)$. Also in [[Lec_12-Asymptotes, Function Investigation and Reading the Derivative|Lec 12]]. → [[Lec_04-Limits and Continuity|Lec 04]]

### Removable Discontinuity
A single hole where the limit exists but the function is undefined or has a different value, for example a cancelled common factor. Also in [[Lec_04-Limits and Continuity|Lec 04]], [[Lec_12-Asymptotes, Function Investigation and Reading the Derivative|Lec 12]]. → [[Lec_03-Composition, Transformations, Exponentials and Limits|Lec 03]]

### Jump Discontinuity
A break where the one-sided limits differ, so the function is not continuous and hence not differentiable there. → [[PS_07-Derivative Definition, Differentiability and Differentiation Rules|PS 07]]

### Sign of a Continuous Function
A continuous function with no zero on an interval keeps one sign there, so testing a single point per interval gives its sign. → [[Lec_09-Marginal Functions and Tangent Lines of Composites|Lec 09]]

## Logarithms and Euler Limits

### Euler Limit
A limit of the form $(1+f)^{1/f}$ with $f\to 0$, which equals $e$; related forms give $e^k$. → [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]]

### Euler's Number
The constant $e\approx 2.718$, the limit of $\left(1+\frac{1}{x}\right)^x$ as $x\to\infty$. → [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]]

### Logarithm
The inverse of an exponential function: $\log_a c$ is the power to which $a$ must be raised to give $c$. Also in [[PS_06-Exponential Limits, Euler Limits, Derivative Definition and Logarithms|PS 06]]. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]]

### Natural Logarithm
The logarithm with base $e$, written $\ln$, the inverse of $e^x$. → [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]]

### Logarithm Rules
The product, quotient and power identities for logarithms, together with change of base. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]]

### Change of Base
The identity $\log_a c=\frac{\log_b c}{\log_b a}$, which rewrites a logarithm in any convenient base. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]]

## The Derivative

### Derivative
The limit of the difference quotient, $f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$, which is the slope of the tangent at $x$. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Definition of the Derivative
The limit definition itself, $\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$, used to differentiate from first principles. Also in [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]], [[PS_07-Derivative Definition, Differentiability and Differentiation Rules|PS 07]]. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Difference Quotient
The expression $\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$, the slope of the secant line, whose limit as $h\to 0$ is the derivative. → [[PS_01-Functions, Domain and Piecewise Graphs|PS 01]]

### Tangent Line
The line touching a curve at a point, with slope $f'(a)$ and passing through $(a,f(a))$. Also in [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]], [[Lec_09-Marginal Functions and Tangent Lines of Composites|Lec 09]], [[Lec_12-Asymptotes, Function Investigation and Reading the Derivative|Lec 12]]. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Slope of a Tangent
The limit of the secant slopes, $\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$. → [[Lec_06-Exponential Limits, Tangent Slopes and Logarithms|Lec 06]]

### Differentiability
The derivative exists as a finite number, so the one-sided slopes exist and agree; failures are jumps, corners, cusps and vertical tangents. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Vertical Tangent
A point where both one-sided slopes are infinite with the same sign, so the vertical line through the point is tangent. Also in [[Lec_10-Increase and Decrease Intervals and Relative Extrema|Lec 10]]. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Cusp
A point where the one-sided slopes are opposite infinities, so there is no tangent line. Also in [[Lec_10-Increase and Decrease Intervals and Relative Extrema|Lec 10]]. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Corner Point
A point where the function is continuous but the two one-sided slopes are different finite numbers, so there is no tangent line. → [[PS_07-Derivative Definition, Differentiability and Differentiation Rules|PS 07]]

## Differentiation Rules

### Constant Rule
The derivative of a constant is 0. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Constant Multiple Rule
$(cf)'=c f'$. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Power Rule
$(x^a)'=a x^{a-1}$. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Product Rule
$(fg)'=f'g+fg'$. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Quotient Rule
$\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)'=\frac{f'g-fg'}{g^2}$. → [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]

### Chain Rule
The derivative of a composition: differentiate the outer function, then multiply by the derivative of the inner one. Also in [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]]. → [[Lec_08-Chain Rule, Exponential Derivatives and Integrals|Lec 08]]

### Derivative of a Composed Power Function
For $y=[f(x)]^n$, $y'=n[f(x)]^{n-1}\cdot f'(x)$. → [[Lec_08-Chain Rule, Exponential Derivatives and Integrals|Lec 08]]

### Derivative of an Exponential Function
$(e^f)'=f'e^f$ and $(a^f)'=f'a^f\ln a$. → [[Lec_08-Chain Rule, Exponential Derivatives and Integrals|Lec 08]]

### Derivative of a Logarithmic Function
$(\ln f)'=\frac{f'}{f}$ and $(\log_a f)'=\frac{f'}{f\ln a}$. → [[Lec_08-Chain Rule, Exponential Derivatives and Integrals|Lec 08]]

## Marginal Analysis

### Marginal Function
The derivative $f'(x)$, which estimates the change in $f$ when $x$ rises by one unit. Also in [[PS_09-Piecewise Differentiability, Marginal Functions & Inequalities|PS 09]], [[PS_10-Increase and Decrease Intervals, Extrema & Marginal Analysis|PS 10]]. → [[Lec_09-Marginal Functions and Tangent Lines of Composites|Lec 09]]

### Marginal Cost
$C'(x)$, the approximate extra cost of producing one more unit. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. → [[Lec_09-Marginal Functions and Tangent Lines of Composites|Lec 09]]

### Marginal Revenue
$R'(x)$, the approximate extra revenue from selling one more unit. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. → [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]]

## Monotonicity and Extrema

### Increasing and Decreasing Intervals
Intervals where $f'$ is positive (the function rises) or negative (the function falls). → [[Lec_10-Increase and Decrease Intervals and Relative Extrema|Lec 10]]

### Critical Point
A point in the domain where $f$ is continuous and $f'$ is 0 or undefined. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_10-Increase and Decrease Intervals and Relative Extrema|Lec 10]]

### Sign Table
A table of the sign of $f'$ (or $f''$) on each interval between the split points, used to read off monotonicity or concavity. Also in [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]]. → [[Lec_10-Increase and Decrease Intervals and Relative Extrema|Lec 10]]

### Relative Extremum
A local maximum or minimum, found at a critical point where $f'$ changes sign (plus to minus is a maximum, minus to plus is a minimum). Also in [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]], [[Lec_12-Asymptotes, Function Investigation and Reading the Derivative|Lec 12]]. → [[Lec_10-Increase and Decrease Intervals and Relative Extrema|Lec 10]]

## Concavity and Inflection Points

### Concavity
The function is concave up where $f''>0$ (slope increasing) and concave down where $f''<0$. Also in [[Lec_12-Asymptotes, Function Investigation and Reading the Derivative|Lec 12]]. → [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]]

### Concavity Intervals
Intervals on which $f''$ has constant sign, found with a sign table. → [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]]

### Inflection Point
A point where $f$ is continuous and $f''$ changes sign, so the concavity switches. Also in [[Lec_12-Asymptotes, Function Investigation and Reading the Derivative|Lec 12]]. → [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]]

### Second Derivative Test
At a point with $f'(c)=0$: $f''(c)>0$ gives a minimum, $f''(c)<0$ gives a maximum and $f''(c)=0$ is inconclusive. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]]

## Asymptotes and Investigation

### Rational Function
A fraction of two polynomials; undefined where the denominator is zero, giving a vertical asymptote or a hole. → [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]]

### Vertical Asymptote
The line $x=a$ where $f$ is undefined and tends to $\pm\infty$ as $x$ approaches $a$. Also in [[Lec_05-Infinite Limits|Lec 05]], [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]]. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_12-Asymptotes, Function Investigation and Reading the Derivative|Lec 12]]

### Horizontal Asymptote
The line $y=L$ that $f$ approaches as $x\to\pm\infty$. Also in [[Lec_11-Concavity, Inflection Points and Asymptotes|Lec 11]]. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_12-Asymptotes, Function Investigation and Reading the Derivative|Lec 12]]

### Oblique Asymptote
A slanted line $y=mx+b$ that $f$ approaches as $x\to\pm\infty$, found by polynomial division. → [[Lec_12-Asymptotes, Function Investigation and Reading the Derivative|Lec 12]]

### Function Investigation
The full routine for sketching a function: domain, asymptotes, intercepts, sign tables for $f'$ and $f''$, then the graph. Also in [[PS_12-Function Investigation, Asymptotes & Reading the Derivative Graph|PS 12]]. → [[Lec_12-Asymptotes, Function Investigation and Reading the Derivative|Lec 12]]

## Integrals and Sigma Notation

### Indefinite Integral
The family of antiderivatives $F(x)+C$ of a function $f$. → [[Lec_08-Chain Rule, Exponential Derivatives and Integrals|Lec 08]]

### Definite Integral
$\int_a^b f\,dx=F(b)-F(a)$, the signed area between the graph and the x axis, counting area below the axis as negative. → [[Lec_08-Chain Rule, Exponential Derivatives and Integrals|Lec 08]]

### Integration Rules
The sum rule, the constant multiple rule and the linear-inside rule $\int f(ax+b)\,dx=\frac{F(ax+b)}{a}+C$ (the reverse chain rule). Also in [[PS_08-Chain Rule, Exponential and Log Derivatives and Integrals|PS 08]]. → [[Lec_08-Chain Rule, Exponential Derivatives and Integrals|Lec 08]]

### Sigma Notation
Compact notation $\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i$ for a sum over an index. Also in [[PS_13-Sigma Notation as a Sum|PS 13]]. → [[Lec_01-Functions, Domain and Sigma Notation|Lec 01]]

### Summation Rules
Sums split over addition, constants pull out of a sum, and the sum of a constant $c$ over $n$ terms is $n\cdot c$. Also in [[PS_13-Sigma Notation as a Sum|PS 13]]. Seen in: [[Maths for Economists B]]. → [[Lec_01-Functions, Domain and Sigma Notation|Lec 01]]
