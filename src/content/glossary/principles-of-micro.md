## Core Ideas of Economics

### Scarcity
Resources are limited while wants are unlimited, so society must choose how to use them. Also in [[PS_01-The 10 Principles of Economics|PS 01]]. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Economics
The study of how society manages its scarce resources. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Microeconomics
The study of how households and firms make decisions and interact in specific markets. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Macroeconomics
The study of economy-wide phenomena such as inflation, unemployment and growth. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Tradeoff
Getting one thing means giving up another, so every choice has a cost in what is forgone. Also in [[PS_01-The 10 Principles of Economics|PS 01]]. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Efficiency
Society gets the most it can from its scarce resources. Also in [[PS_01-The 10 Principles of Economics|PS 01]]. Seen in: [[Intro Econometrics]]. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Equality
The benefits of those resources are distributed uniformly among society's members; it often trades off against efficiency. Also in [[PS_01-The 10 Principles of Economics|PS 01]]. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Opportunity Cost
Whatever must be given up to obtain an item: the value of the best forgone alternative. Also in [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]], [[PS_01-The 10 Principles of Economics|PS 01]], [[PS_02-Interdependence and Trade|PS 02]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. Seen in: [[Financial Management]], [[Microeconomics]]. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Rational Decision Maker
Someone who systematically and purposefully does the best they can to achieve their objectives, given the available opportunities. Also in [[PS_01-The 10 Principles of Economics|PS 01]]. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Marginal Change
A small incremental adjustment to an existing plan of action; rational people compare marginal benefits with marginal costs. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Marginal Benefit
The extra benefit from one more unit of an activity; a decision is worth taking while marginal benefit exceeds marginal cost. Also in [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]]. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Incentive
Something that induces a person to act, such as the prospect of a reward or a punishment; people respond to incentives. Also in [[PS_01-The 10 Principles of Economics|PS 01]]. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Market
A group of buyers and sellers of a particular good or service. Also in [[PS_01-The 10 Principles of Economics|PS 01]]. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Invisible Hand
Adam Smith's idea that self-interested buyers and sellers in a market are guided, as if by an invisible hand, towards outcomes that benefit society. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Market Failure
A situation in which a market left alone fails to allocate resources efficiently, for example because of an externality or market power. Also in [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]. Seen in: [[Microeconomics]], [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

### Market Power
The ability of a single buyer or seller to influence market prices substantially. → [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]]

## Gains from Trade and the PPF

### Economic Model
A simplified representation of reality that keeps the essential features and leaves out the rest. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

### Factors of Production
The inputs used to produce goods and services, such as labour, land, capital and entrepreneurship. Seen in: [[Macro-Economics (Intro)]]. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

### Production Possibility Frontier
A graph of the combinations of two goods an economy can produce with its given resources and technology; points on it are efficient, points inside it are not, points outside are unattainable. Also in [[PS_02-Interdependence and Trade|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

### Bow-Shaped PPF
A production possibility frontier that bends outwards because opportunity cost rises as more of one good is produced. Also in [[PS_02-Interdependence and Trade|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

### Economic Growth
An expansion of production possibilities that shifts the PPF outwards, for example through more resources or better technology. Also in [[PS_02-Interdependence and Trade|PS 02]]. Seen in: [[Macro-Economics (Intro)]]. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

### Absolute Advantage
The ability to produce a good using fewer inputs, or more output from the same inputs, than another producer. Also in [[PS_02-Interdependence and Trade|PS 02]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

### Comparative Advantage
The ability to produce a good at a lower opportunity cost than another producer; it is the basis for gains from trade. Also in [[PS_02-Interdependence and Trade|PS 02]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

### Specialisation
Each producer concentrates on the good in which they have a comparative advantage. Also in [[PS_02-Interdependence and Trade|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

### Gains from Trade
The extra consumption available when producers specialise and trade; both sides can end up better off. Also in [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]], [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]], [[PS_01-The 10 Principles of Economics|PS 01]], [[PS_02-Interdependence and Trade|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

### Consumption Possibility Frontier
The combinations of goods a country can consume once it specialises and trades at a given price ratio; it lies outside its PPF. Also in [[PS_02-Interdependence and Trade|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

### Terms of Trade
The rate at which one good is exchanged for another; for both sides to gain it must lie between their two opportunity costs. Also in [[PS_02-Interdependence and Trade|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-PPF and Gains from Trade|Lec 02]]

## Perfect Competition and Demand

### Perfect Competition
A market with many small buyers and sellers, identical goods and free entry and exit, so no single participant affects the price; also called a competitive market. Also in [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]], [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. Seen in: [[Microeconomics]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Demand Function
An equation giving the quantity demanded as a function of price (and other determinants). → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Demand Schedule
A table showing the quantity demanded at each price. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Demand Curve
A graph of the relationship between price and quantity demanded, other things equal; it slopes downward. Also in [[PS_03-Demand|PS 03]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Law of Demand
Other things equal, when the price of a good rises the quantity demanded falls. Also in [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]], [[PS_03-Demand|PS 03]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Substitution Effect
When a good's price rises, buyers switch to cheaper alternatives, so they buy less of it. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics I]], [[Macro-Economics]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Income Effect
A higher price lowers buyers' purchasing power, so they can afford less of the good. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics I]], [[Macro-Economics]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Diminishing Marginal Utility
Each extra unit of a good adds less satisfaction than the one before, which is why buyers' willingness to pay falls with quantity. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Quantity Demanded
The amount of a good buyers are willing and able to purchase at a given price. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Market Demand
The sum of all individual demands, found by adding quantities at each price (a horizontal sum). → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Change in Quantity Demanded
A movement along the demand curve, caused only by a change in the good's own price. Also in [[PS_03-Demand|PS 03]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Change in Demand
A shift of the whole demand curve, caused by something other than the good's own price such as income, tastes, expectations, number of buyers or related prices; also called a shift of the demand curve. Also in [[PS_03-Demand|PS 03]], [[Lec_07-Comparative Statics|Lec 07]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]], [[PS_07-Comparative Statics|PS 07]], [[PS_11-Taxes|PS 11]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Normal Good
A good whose demand rises when income rises. Also in [[PS_03-Demand|PS 03]], [[PS_08-Elasticity|PS 08]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Inferior Good
A good whose demand falls when income rises. Also in [[PS_03-Demand|PS 03]], [[PS_08-Elasticity|PS 08]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Substitutes
Goods used in place of one another: a rise in the price of one raises the demand for the other. Also in [[PS_03-Demand|PS 03]], [[PS_08-Elasticity|PS 08]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]], [[Lec_07-Comparative Statics|Lec 07]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Complements
Goods used together: a rise in the price of one lowers the demand for the other. Also in [[PS_03-Demand|PS 03]], [[PS_08-Elasticity|PS 08]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand|Lec 03]]

### Giffen Good
A rare good whose quantity demanded rises when its price rises, because it is so inferior that the income effect outweighs the substitution effect. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Veblen Good
A status good whose quantity demanded can rise with its price because a high price signals prestige. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

## Supply, Equilibrium and Comparative Statics

### Supply
The amount of a good sellers are willing and able to offer for sale at each price. Also in [[PS_04-Supply|PS 04]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Law of Supply
Other things equal, when the price of a good rises the quantity supplied rises. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Supply Curve
A graph of the relationship between price and quantity supplied, other things equal; it slopes upward. Also in [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]], [[PS_04-Supply|PS 04]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Market Supply
The sum of all individual sellers' supplies, found by adding quantities at each price. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Change in Quantity Supplied
A movement along the supply curve, caused only by a change in the good's own price. Also in [[PS_04-Supply|PS 04]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Change in Supply
A shift of the whole supply curve, caused by something other than the good's own price such as input prices, technology or expectations; also called a shift of the supply curve. Also in [[PS_04-Supply|PS 04]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]], [[Lec_07-Comparative Statics|Lec 07]], [[PS_07-Comparative Statics|PS 07]], [[PS_11-Taxes|PS 11]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Determinants of Supply
The non-price factors the lecture lists as shifting the supply curve: input prices, technology and expectations. Also in [[PS_04-Supply|PS 04]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Price Taker
A buyer or seller too small to influence the market price, so it takes the price as given. Also in [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]], [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]]. Seen in: [[Microeconomics]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Surplus
A market surplus (excess supply): at a price above equilibrium, quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded and the price tends to fall. Not the same as consumer, producer or total surplus. Also in [[Lec_07-Comparative Statics|Lec 07]], [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]], [[PS_10-Price Controls|PS 10]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Shortage
A market shortage (excess demand): at a price below equilibrium, quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied and the price tends to rise. Also in [[Lec_07-Comparative Statics|Lec 07]], [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]], [[PS_04-Supply|PS 04]], [[PS_10-Price Controls|PS 10]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Market Equilibrium
The point where supply and demand meet, so quantity supplied equals quantity demanded and there is no pressure on the price to change. Also in [[Lec_07-Comparative Statics|Lec 07]], [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]], [[PS_04-Supply|PS 04]], [[PS_07-Comparative Statics|PS 07]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Equilibrium Price
The price at which quantity supplied equals quantity demanded; it clears the market. Also in [[PS_04-Supply|PS 04]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Equilibrium Quantity
The quantity bought and sold at the equilibrium price. Also in [[PS_04-Supply|PS 04]]. → [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]]

### Comparative Statics
Comparing two equilibria, before and after a change in an outside factor, to see what happens to price and quantity. Also in [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]], [[PS_07-Comparative Statics|PS 07]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. Seen in: [[Microeconomics]]. → [[Lec_07-Comparative Statics|Lec 07]]

### Movement Along a Curve
A change in quantity demanded or quantity supplied caused by a change in the good's own price; the curve itself does not move. Also in [[PS_07-Comparative Statics|PS 07]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_07-Comparative Statics|Lec 07]]

### Simultaneous Shifts
When both the demand and supply curves shift at once, the change in price or in quantity is certain but the other is ambiguous unless the sizes of the shifts are known. Also in [[PS_07-Comparative Statics|PS 07]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_07-Comparative Statics|Lec 07]]

## Costs of Production

### Profit
Total revenue minus total cost. → [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]]

### Explicit Costs
Input costs that require a direct outlay of money, such as wages and rent paid. Also in [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]]. → [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]]

### Implicit Costs
Input costs that do not require an outlay of money, such as the owner's forgone wages or the forgone return on invested capital. Also in [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]]

### Economic Profit
Total revenue minus all costs, explicit and implicit. Also in [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_07-Onion Market Tax Ceiling and Mixed MCQ (2024 Moed A)|PP 07]]. → [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]]

### Accounting Profit
Total revenue minus explicit costs only, so it is larger than economic profit. Also in [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]]

### Production Function
The relationship between the quantity of inputs used and the quantity of output produced. Seen in: [[Macro-Economics (Intro)]], [[Macro-Economics]], [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]]

### Marginal Product of Labour
The extra output from one more worker: the change in output divided by the change in workers. Also called marginal product (of any input). Also in [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PP_06-Carrot Market Tax and Mixed Topics (2023 Moed B)|PP 06]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_14-Production Costs Worksheet|Lec 14]]

### Diminishing Marginal Product
Each extra unit of an input adds less output than the one before, so output rises at a falling rate. Also in [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]], [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_06-Carrot Market Tax and Mixed Topics (2023 Moed B)|PP 06]]. → [[Lec_14-Production Costs Worksheet|Lec 14]]

### Fixed Costs
Costs that do not vary with the quantity produced. Also in [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]], [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]], [[PP_06-Carrot Market Tax and Mixed Topics (2023 Moed B)|PP 06]]. → [[Lec_14-Production Costs Worksheet|Lec 14]]

### Variable Costs
Costs that vary with the quantity produced. Also in [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]], [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]], [[PP_06-Carrot Market Tax and Mixed Topics (2023 Moed B)|PP 06]]. → [[Lec_14-Production Costs Worksheet|Lec 14]]

### Total Cost
Fixed cost plus variable cost. Also in [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]]. → [[Lec_14-Production Costs Worksheet|Lec 14]]

### Average Fixed Cost
Fixed cost divided by quantity; it falls as output rises. Also in [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]], [[PP_05-Apples Tax Shifts and Mixed Multiple Choice (2023 Moed A)|PP 05]]. → [[Lec_14-Production Costs Worksheet|Lec 14]]

### Average Variable Cost
Variable cost divided by quantity. Also in [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]], [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_14-Production Costs Worksheet|Lec 14]]

### Average Total Cost
Total cost divided by quantity, equal to average fixed cost plus average variable cost; U-shaped. Also in [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]], [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]], [[PP_05-Apples Tax Shifts and Mixed Multiple Choice (2023 Moed A)|PP 05]]. → [[Lec_14-Production Costs Worksheet|Lec 14]]

### Marginal Cost
The change in total cost from producing one more unit; it crosses average total cost at ATC's minimum, and a rational decision maker weighs it against marginal benefit. Also in [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]], [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]], [[PS_05-Costs of Production|PS 05]], [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]], [[PP_03-Mixed Multiple Choice (2022 Moed A)|PP 03]]. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]]

### Economies of Scale
Long-run average total cost falls as the quantity of output rises. → [[Lec_05-Supply and Costs of Production|Lec 05]]

## Firms in Competitive Markets

### Total Revenue
Price times quantity sold: what a firm receives from selling its output, or what buyers spend. Also in [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]], [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]], [[PS_08-Elasticity|PS 08]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]]

### Average Revenue
Total revenue divided by quantity; for a price taker it equals the price. → [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]]

### Marginal Revenue
The change in total revenue from selling one more unit; for a price taker it equals the price. Also in [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]]

### Marginal Profit
The change in profit from producing one more unit: marginal revenue minus marginal cost. → [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]]

### Profit Maximisation
A competitive firm produces where marginal revenue (price) equals marginal cost, as long as price covers its relevant average cost. Also in [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]]

### Shutdown Point
The minimum of average variable cost; if price falls below it, the firm shuts down in the short run. Also in [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]]. → [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]]

### Sunk Cost
A cost that has already been committed and cannot be recovered, so it should not affect current decisions. Also in [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]]. Seen in: [[Microeconomics]]. → [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]]

### Short-Run Supply Curve
The firm's marginal cost curve above the minimum of average variable cost. Also in [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]]. → [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]]

### Long-Run Supply Curve
With free entry and exit, firms enter or leave until economic profit is zero; the firm supplies along its marginal cost curve above minimum average total cost. Also in [[PS_06-Firms in the Competitive Market|PS 06]]. → [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets|Lec 06]]

## Elasticity

### Elasticity
A measure of how much the quantity demanded or supplied responds to a change in one of its determinants. Also in [[PS_08-Elasticity|PS 08]]. Seen in: [[Microeconomics]], [[Intro Econometrics]]. → [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]]

### Price Elasticity of Demand
The percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price; demand is elastic if its size is above one and inelastic if below one. Also in [[Lec_11-Taxes|Lec 11]], [[PS_08-Elasticity|PS 08]], [[PS_10-Price Controls|PS 10]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]]

### Midpoint Method
Computes percentage changes using the average of the starting and ending values, so the elasticity is the same in either direction. Also in [[PS_08-Elasticity|PS 08]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]]

### Income Elasticity of Demand
The percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in income; positive for normal goods and negative for inferior goods. Also in [[PS_08-Elasticity|PS 08]]. → [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]]

### Cross-Price Elasticity of Demand
The percentage change in the quantity demanded of one good divided by the percentage change in the price of another; positive for substitutes and negative for complements. Also in [[PS_08-Elasticity|PS 08]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]]

### Price Elasticity of Supply
The percentage change in quantity supplied divided by the percentage change in price. Also in [[Lec_11-Taxes|Lec 11]]. → [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]]

## Welfare: Consumer and Producer Surplus

### Willingness to Pay
The maximum amount a buyer will pay for a good; it measures the value the buyer places on it. Also in [[PS_09-Welfare|PS 09]]. → [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]]

### Consumer Surplus
Willingness to pay minus the price paid: the area under the demand curve and above the price. Also in [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]], [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]], [[PS_09-Welfare|PS 09]], [[PS_10-Price Controls|PS 10]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. Seen in: [[Microeconomics]], [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]]

### Producer Surplus
The price received minus the seller's cost: the area above the supply curve and below the price. Also in [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]], [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]], [[PS_09-Welfare|PS 09]], [[PS_10-Price Controls|PS 10]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]]

### Total Surplus
Consumer surplus plus producer surplus: the total gains from trade in a market. Also in [[PS_09-Welfare|PS 09]]. → [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]]

### Efficient Production
Goods are produced by the sellers with the lowest cost. → [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]]

### Efficient Allocation
Goods are consumed by the buyers who value them most. → [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]]

### Efficient Quantity
The quantity that maximises total surplus, where marginal benefit equals marginal cost; the market equilibrium quantity in a competitive market. → [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]]

### Market Efficiency
A competitive equilibrium maximises total surplus, with the lowest-cost sellers selling to the highest-value buyers at the efficient quantity. Also in [[PS_09-Welfare|PS 09]]. → [[Lec_09-Welfare Consumer and Producer Surplus|Lec 09]]

## Price Controls

### Price Control
A government limit on the price at which a good may be sold. Also in [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]]. → [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]]

### Price Ceiling
A legal maximum on the price of a good; binding only when set below the equilibrium price. Also in [[PS_10-Price Controls|PS 10]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]]

### Price Floor
A legal minimum on the price of a good; binding only when set above the equilibrium price. Also in [[PS_10-Price Controls|PS 10]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]]

### Binding Constraint
A price control that actually changes the market outcome because it is set on the wrong side of the equilibrium price. Also in [[PS_10-Price Controls|PS 10]]. → [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]]

### Rent Control
A price ceiling on rents; when binding it creates a shortage of housing. → [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]]

### Minimum Wage
A price floor on labour; when binding it creates a surplus of labour, that is unemployment. → [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]]

### Deadweight Loss
The fall in total surplus that results from a market distortion such as a tax, price control or tariff. Also in [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_10-Price Controls|Lec 10]]

## Taxes

### Per-Unit Tax
A fixed amount of tax charged on each unit sold, which drives a wedge between the price buyers pay and the price sellers receive. Also in [[PS_11-Taxes|PS 11]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]], [[Lec_08-Elasticity|Lec 08]]. Seen in: [[Microeconomics]]. → [[Lec_11-Taxes|Lec 11]]

### Tax Incidence
How the burden of a tax is shared between buyers and sellers; it does not depend on who legally pays, and falls more on the less elastic side. Also in [[PS_11-Taxes|PS 11]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. Seen in: [[Microeconomics]]. → [[Lec_11-Taxes|Lec 11]]

### Tax Wedge
The difference between the price buyers pay and the price sellers receive, equal to the per-unit tax. Also in [[PS_11-Taxes|PS 11]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_11-Taxes|Lec 11]]

### Subsidy
A per-unit payment from the government, the opposite of a tax; it raises the quantity above the efficient level and creates a deadweight loss. Also in [[PS_11-Taxes|PS 11]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_11-Taxes|Lec 11]]

### Tax Revenue
The tax per unit times the quantity sold after the tax. Also in [[PS_11-Taxes|PS 11]]. → [[Lec_11-Taxes|Lec 11]]

### Deadweight Loss of a Tax
The deadweight loss caused by a tax: surplus lost on units no longer traded; it grows with the size of the tax and with the elasticities of supply and demand. Also in [[PS_11-Taxes|PS 11]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_11-Taxes|Lec 11]]

### Laffer Curve
The relationship between the tax rate and tax revenue: revenue rises at first, reaches a peak and then falls as high rates shrink the quantity traded. → [[Lec_11-Taxes|Lec 11]]

## Externalities

### Externality
The uncompensated impact of one person's actions on the well-being of a bystander. Also in [[Lec_01-Ten Principles of Economics|Lec 01]], [[PS_12-Externalities|PS 12]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Negative Externality
An externality that imposes a cost on bystanders, so the market produces more than the social optimum. Also in [[PS_12-Externalities|PS 12]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_03-Mixed Multiple Choice (2022 Moed A)|PP 03]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Positive Externality
An externality that gives a benefit to bystanders, so the market produces less than the social optimum. Also in [[PS_12-Externalities|PS 12]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_07-Onion Market Tax Ceiling and Mixed MCQ (2024 Moed A)|PP 07]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Marginal Private Cost
The cost of one more unit borne by the producer; it is the supply curve. Also in [[PS_12-Externalities|PS 12]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Marginal Social Cost
Marginal private cost plus the external cost to others. Also in [[PS_12-Externalities|PS 12]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Marginal Private Benefit
The benefit of one more unit to the buyer; it is the demand curve. Also in [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Marginal Social Benefit
Marginal private benefit plus the external benefit to others. Also in [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Socially Optimal Quantity
The quantity where marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost. Also in [[PS_12-Externalities|PS 12]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Internalizing the Externality
Altering incentives so that people take account of the external effects of their actions. Also in [[PS_12-Externalities|PS 12]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Pigovian Tax
A tax set equal to the marginal external cost, so a negative externality is internalised. Also in [[PS_12-Externalities|PS 12]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Corrective Subsidy
A subsidy set equal to the marginal external benefit, so a positive externality is internalised. Also in [[PS_12-Externalities|PS 12]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Command-and-Control Policy
A regulation that sets a required behaviour or limit directly, such as a pollution cap per firm. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Tradable Pollution Permits
Allowances to pollute that firms can buy and sell, so the total is capped and cuts go to the firms where they cost least. Also in [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Coase Theorem
If private parties can bargain at no cost over the allocation of resources, they can solve an externality themselves, whatever the initial allocation of rights. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

### Transaction Costs
The costs parties incur in agreeing to and following through on a bargain; when they are high, private bargaining can fail. → [[Lec_12-Externalities|Lec 12]]

## Public Goods and International Trade

### Excludability
A good is excludable if people can be prevented from using it, for example by charging a price. Also in [[PS_13-Public Goods and International Trade|PS 13]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Rivalry
A good is rival if one person's use diminishes another's use. Also in [[PS_13-Public Goods and International Trade|PS 13]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Private Goods
Goods that are both excludable and rival. Also in [[PS_13-Public Goods and International Trade|PS 13]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Club Goods
Goods that are excludable but not rival. Also in [[PS_13-Public Goods and International Trade|PS 13]], [[PP_01-Markets Surplus Subsidy and Tax (2021 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Public Goods
Goods that are neither excludable nor rival, so the market tends to under-provide them. Also in [[PS_13-Public Goods and International Trade|PS 13]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Common Resources
Goods that are rival but not excludable. Also in [[PS_13-Public Goods and International Trade|PS 13]], [[PP_02-Markets, Taxes, Elasticity and Costs MCQ (2021 Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Free-Rider Problem
People enjoy a good without paying for it, which is why private markets under-provide public goods. Also in [[PS_13-Public Goods and International Trade|PS 13]]. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Tragedy of the Commons
A common resource is overused because each user ignores the cost their use imposes on others. Also in [[PS_13-Public Goods and International Trade|PS 13]]. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### World Price
The price of a good prevailing in the world market; a small open economy takes it as given. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Exporting Country
A country whose domestic price without trade is below the world price, so it sells abroad; producers gain and consumers lose. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Importing Country
A country whose domestic price without trade is above the world price, so it buys from abroad; consumers gain and producers lose. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Tariff
A tax on imported goods; it raises the domestic price, shrinks imports and creates a deadweight loss. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]

### Import Quota
A limit on the quantity of a good that may be imported; its effects resemble a tariff, except that licence holders rather than the government may collect the gap. → [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]]
