# Maths B Concepts

> Glossary for Maths for Economists B (Year 1, Semester 2). Each entry: a one-line definition and the note where it is taught. Registry: [[_Wiki-Link Registry]]. Hub: [[Maths for Economists B]].


## Global extrema and optimisation

### Global Extremum
The highest or lowest value of f over the whole domain. → [[Lec_01-Global Extrema, Optimization and Summation|Lec 01]]

### Critical Point
A point where f′ = 0 or f′ does not exist (for two variables, where both partial derivatives are zero); the candidates for extrema. Also in [[Lec_07-Partial Derivatives in Applications and Local Extrema|Lec 07]]. → [[Lec_01-Global Extrema, Optimization and Summation|Lec 01]]

### Closed Interval Rule
A continuous function on a closed interval always has a global max and min, found among the critical points and the endpoints. → [[Lec_01-Global Extrema, Optimization and Summation|Lec 01]]

### Optimization Problem
Use the constraint to eliminate a variable, differentiate, then justify that the critical point is global. → [[Lec_01-Global Extrema, Optimization and Summation|Lec 01]]

### Summation Rules
Sums split over addition, constants pull out, and the sum of a constant c over n terms is nc. → [[Lec_01-Global Extrema, Optimization and Summation|Lec 01]]

### Cusp
A sharp point on a graph where the derivative does not exist, such as x^(2/3) at 0. Also in [[PP_01-Tangent Slope, Marginal Profit, Parametric System & Cube-Root Extrema (2009 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[PS_01-Global Extrema and Optimisation|PS 01]]

### Piecewise (Compound) Function
A function written in separate pieces, such as an absolute value; check each piece and the join points. → [[PS_01-Global Extrema and Optimisation|PS 01]]


## Logarithms, inverses and limits

### Logarithm
log_a b = n exactly when aⁿ = b; log without a base is read as base 10 in this course. → [[Lec_02-Logarithmic Functions and Inverse Functions|Lec 02]]

### Logarithm Rules
Product, quotient, power and inverse rules for logs. → [[Lec_02-Logarithmic Functions and Inverse Functions|Lec 02]]

### Change of Base
log_a c = log_b c / log_b a. → [[Lec_02-Logarithmic Functions and Inverse Functions|Lec 02]]

### Inverse Function
The function with the roles of x and y swapped; its graph is the reflection in the line y = x. → [[Lec_02-Logarithmic Functions and Inverse Functions|Lec 02]]

### L'Hopital's Rule
For a 0/0 or ∞/∞ limit, differentiate numerator and denominator separately and take the limit again. Also in [[Lec_03-Exponential and Logarithmic Limits and Asymptotes|Lec 03]]. → [[Lec_02-Logarithmic Functions and Inverse Functions|Lec 02]]

### Indeterminate Form
A limit form (0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞, 0⁰) that does not fix the answer; rewrite it as a fraction or as exp(…·ln …) and apply L'Hopital. Also in [[PP_33-Two-Price Profit, Tangent Lines, Limits & Extrema (2025 Moed B)|PP 33]]. → [[Lec_03-Exponential and Logarithmic Limits and Asymptotes|Lec 03]]

### Horizontal Asymptote
A line y = L that the graph approaches as x → ±∞ (a finite limit at infinity). → [[Lec_03-Exponential and Logarithmic Limits and Asymptotes|Lec 03]]

### Vertical Asymptote
A line x = a near which f → ±∞. → [[Lec_03-Exponential and Logarithmic Limits and Asymptotes|Lec 03]]

### Derivative of Exponential and Logarithmic Functions
(aˣ)′ = aˣ ln a and (log_a x)′ = 1/(x ln a). → [[Lec_03-Exponential and Logarithmic Limits and Asymptotes|Lec 03]]


## Implicit differentiation and surfaces

### Implicit Differentiation
Differentiate F(x, y) = 0 treating y as a function of x, then solve for y′. Also in [[Lec_06-Partial Derivatives and the Differential|Lec 06]]. → [[Lec_04-Implicit Differentiation and Two-Variable Functions|Lec 04]]

### Tangent Line
The line touching a curve at a point with slope y′ there. → [[Lec_04-Implicit Differentiation and Two-Variable Functions|Lec 04]]

### Logarithmic Differentiation
For y = f^g, take ln of both sides before differentiating. → [[Lec_04-Implicit Differentiation and Two-Variable Functions|Lec 04]]

### Two-Variable Function
A rule z = f(x, y) giving one output for each pair of inputs; its graph is a surface. → [[Lec_04-Implicit Differentiation and Two-Variable Functions|Lec 04]]

### Coordinate Plane
One of the planes z = c, x = c or y = c used to slice a surface. → [[Lec_04-Implicit Differentiation and Two-Variable Functions|Lec 04]]

### Cross Section of a Surface
The curve you get by cutting a surface with a plane such as y = c. → [[Lec_04-Implicit Differentiation and Two-Variable Functions|Lec 04]]


## Two-variable functions and contours

### Domain of a Two-Variable Function
The set of points (x, y) where the formula is defined. → [[Lec_05-Two Variable Functions|Lec 05]]

### Contour Line
The set of points where f(x, y) = c, drawn in the (x, y) plane. → [[Lec_05-Two Variable Functions|Lec 05]]

### Level Curve
The curve cut from a surface by the plane z = c; another name for a contour line. Also in [[Lec_05-Two Variable Functions|Lec 05]]. → [[PP_29-Profit Maximisation, Parametric Linear System, Implicit Slope & x² ln x (2023 Moed B)|PP 29]]

### Indifference Curve
A contour line of a utility function: bundles giving the same utility. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics I]]. → [[Lec_05-Two Variable Functions|Lec 05]]


## Partial derivatives and the differential

### Partial Derivative
The derivative in one variable with the other variables held constant. Also in [[Lec_06-Partial Derivatives and the Differential|Lec 06]]. → [[Lec_05-Two Variable Functions|Lec 05]]

### Total Differential
dz = f′ₓ dx + f′ᵧ dy, a linear estimate of the change in z; also written as a differential estimate dP = Pₓ dx + Pᵧ dy. Also in [[PP_32-Import Profit, Level Curves, Homogeneity Sums & x to the x (2025 Moed A)|PP 32]]. → [[Lec_06-Partial Derivatives and the Differential|Lec 06]]

### Second Partial Derivative
A partial derivative of a partial derivative, such as fxx, fyy and the mixed fxy. → [[Lec_06-Partial Derivatives and the Differential|Lec 06]]


## Local extrema and critical points

### Marginal Profit
The extra profit from one more unit; the partial derivative of profit with respect to that product. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. → [[Lec_07-Partial Derivatives in Applications and Local Extrema|Lec 07]]

### Isoprofit Curve
A contour line of the profit function, P = constant, with slope −Pₓ/Pᵧ. → [[Lec_07-Partial Derivatives in Applications and Local Extrema|Lec 07]]

### Local Extremum
The highest or lowest value of f in a neighbourhood of a point. → [[Lec_07-Partial Derivatives in Applications and Local Extrema|Lec 07]]

### Saddle Point
A critical point that is neither a local max nor a local min. Also in [[Lec_08-Second Derivative Test and Lagrange Multipliers|Lec 08]]. → [[Lec_07-Partial Derivatives in Applications and Local Extrema|Lec 07]]

### Second Derivative Test
With D = fxx·fyy − fxy² at a critical point: D > 0 gives a max or min by the sign of fxx, D < 0 a saddle, D = 0 is inconclusive. → [[Lec_08-Second Derivative Test and Lagrange Multipliers|Lec 08]]

### Double Root
A quadratic whose discriminant is zero, so it touches the axis at one point. → [[PP_29-Profit Maximisation, Parametric Linear System, Implicit Slope & x² ln x (2023 Moed B)|PP 29]]

### Laplace Check
The test fxx + fyy = 0 for a function of two variables. → [[PP_27-Fabric Profit, Parametric System, Log Surface & x e to the minus x (2022 Moed B)|PP 27]]


## Constrained optimisation

### Constrained Optimisation
Maximising or minimising f subject to a constraint g = c. Also in [[Lec_08-Second Derivative Test and Lagrange Multipliers|Lec 08]]. → [[Lec_09-Constrained Optimisation and Lagrange Multipliers|Lec 09]]

### Lagrangian
L = f − λ(g − c), the function whose critical points solve the constrained problem. → [[Lec_09-Constrained Optimisation and Lagrange Multipliers|Lec 09]]

### Lagrange Multiplier
The λ that scales ∇f to ∇g at the optimum; it measures the shadow price of the constraint. → [[Lec_09-Constrained Optimisation and Lagrange Multipliers|Lec 09]]

### Lagrange Multiplier Method
Set every partial derivative of the Lagrangian, including the one in λ, to zero and solve. Also in [[Lec_08-Second Derivative Test and Lagrange Multipliers|Lec 08]]. → [[Lec_09-Constrained Optimisation and Lagrange Multipliers|Lec 09]]

### First-Order Conditions
The conditions that all partial derivatives equal zero. → [[Lec_09-Constrained Optimisation and Lagrange Multipliers|Lec 09]]

### Shadow Price
The rate at which the optimal value changes per unit of constraint relaxation; equal to λ. → [[PS_09-Lagrange Multipliers|PS 09]]


## Linear systems and Gauss-Jordan

### System of Linear Equations
Simultaneous linear equations, with no, one or infinitely many solutions. → [[Lec_10-Linear Systems and Gauss-Jordan Elimination|Lec 10]]

### Elementary Row Operations
Add a multiple of one row to another, scale a row by a non-zero number, or swap two rows. → [[Lec_10-Linear Systems and Gauss-Jordan Elimination|Lec 10]]

### Augmented Matrix
The coefficient matrix with the constants as an extra column. → [[Lec_10-Linear Systems and Gauss-Jordan Elimination|Lec 10]]

### Gauss-Jordan Elimination
Pivot each variable to 1 and cancel it from every other row. → [[Lec_10-Linear Systems and Gauss-Jordan Elimination|Lec 10]]

### Leading Entry
The first non-zero entry in a row. → [[Lec_10-Linear Systems and Gauss-Jordan Elimination|Lec 10]]

### Dependent Variable
A variable whose column has a leading entry; solved in terms of the others. → [[Lec_10-Linear Systems and Gauss-Jordan Elimination|Lec 10]]

### Independent Variable
A free variable whose column has no leading entry; it can take any value. → [[Lec_10-Linear Systems and Gauss-Jordan Elimination|Lec 10]]


## Echelon form and homogeneous systems

### Reduced Echelon Form
Each leading entry is 1 and alone in its column, leading entries step down to the right, and zero rows come last. → [[Lec_11-Reduced Echelon Form and Homogeneous Systems|Lec 11]]

### Homogeneous System
A system with all constants zero, so it always has the zero solution. → [[Lec_11-Reduced Echelon Form and Homogeneous Systems|Lec 11]]

### Zero Solution
The solution with every variable equal to 0. → [[Lec_11-Reduced Echelon Form and Homogeneous Systems|Lec 11]]

### Redundant Equation
An equation that reduces to 0 = 0 and adds no information. → [[PP_29-Profit Maximisation, Parametric Linear System, Implicit Slope & x² ln x (2023 Moed B)|PP 29]]


## Systems with parameters

### Parametric System
A linear system containing a letter; ask for which values of the letter the number of solutions changes. → [[Lec_12-Systems with Parameters|Lec 12]]

### Parameter Case Split
Reduce without dividing by the parameter, then treat each value that makes a pivot zero as its own case. → [[PS_12-Systems with Parameters|PS 12]]
