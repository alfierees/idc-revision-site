# Legal and Tax Concepts

> Glossary for Legal and Tax Aspects (Year 2, Semester 1). One entry per concept: a one-line definition and the note that teaches it. Link tracker: [[_Wiki-Link Registry]]. Subject hub: [[Legal and Tax Aspects]].

## Foundations: the startup lifecycle & the Israeli legal system

### Startup Lifecycle
The stages of a venture from idea and pre-incorporation through funding rounds to exit, each raising different legal questions. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Founders' Agreement
Contract among co-founders fixing equity split, roles, vesting, IP ownership and exit mechanics. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Vesting
Earning the right to equity or options gradually over time, so a departing founder or employee keeps only the vested part. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].
Seen in: [[Accounting]]

### Articles of Association
The company's constitutional document (Companies Law s.15/18/19) and a contract between the company and shareholders and among shareholders. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Term Sheet
Non-binding summary of the main terms of an investment, drafted before the definitive investment agreement. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Liquidation Preference
Investor right to be paid back first (often a multiple of the investment) before others share exit proceeds. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].
Seen in: [[Accounting]]

### Anti-Dilution
Down-round investor protection that adjusts the conversion price when new shares are issued at a lower price (full ratchet, broad-based weighted average). Taught in [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Lec 08]].
Seen in: [[Accounting]]

### Due Diligence
Investigation of a company's legal, financial and IP position that an investor or acquirer runs before closing. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Warranties & Indemnities
Seller or company statements of fact in a deal, backed by a promise to compensate the buyer if they prove false. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Bootstrapping
Funding a venture from founders' own money and early revenue rather than outside investors. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Seed Round
Early outside funding used to build an MVP and validate the market. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Venture Capital
Professional funds that invest in high-growth startups in return for equity. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### IPO
Initial public offering: first sale of a company's shares to the public on a stock exchange. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].
Seen in: [[Accounting]]

### Soft Law vs Hard Law
Hard law is binding legislation and case law; soft law is guidance and standards without direct legal force. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Jurisprudence
The theory and philosophy of law and of how courts reason. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Israeli Legal System
Mixed system built on Ottoman, British Mandate and Continental influences, with common-law-style precedent and codified statutes. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Common Law
System in which judge-made precedent is a primary source of law, as in England and the US. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Continental Law
Civil-law tradition built on comprehensive codes rather than precedent, as in France and Germany. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Mixed Jurisdiction
A legal system combining common-law and civil-law features, as Israel does. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Good Faith
Duty to act fairly and honestly in contracts: negotiation (s.12), performance (s.39) and gap-filling. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Codification
Collecting the law into systematic statutes or codes. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Separation of Powers
Division of government into legislature, executive and judiciary so that none controls all state power. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Rule of Law
Principle that everyone, including the state, is bound by publicly known, clear, non-retroactive law applied by independent courts. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Precedent
A prior court decision that later courts follow (stare decisis). Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Ratio Decidendi
The binding legal reasoning of a judgment, which creates precedent. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

### Obiter Dictum
Remarks in a judgment not needed for the decision; persuasive but not binding. Taught in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 01]].

## Legal entrepreneurship: agreements, funding & compliance

### Legal Entrepreneurship
The course frame: a venture's legal universe rests on three pillars, business structure, contracts and regulation. Also called: Three Legal Pillars. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Five Core Legal Risks
No founders' agreement, improper corporate structure, no NDAs, neglecting privacy, improper open-source use. Also called: Legal Risk. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Corporate Structure
How a venture's legal entities, ownership and control are organised. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Corporation
A legal entity with its own legal personality, separate from its owners. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Subsidiaries
Companies controlled by a parent company, used to ring-fence risk or hold operations or IP in another jurisdiction. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Contracts
A venture's web of agreements (founders, investors, customers, suppliers, employees); the practical, startup-level view of contract law. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Contract Law
The branch of law governing formation, content, interpretation and enforcement of agreements. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Voting Rights
Shareholders' power to vote at general meetings, which can differ by share class. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### NDA
Non-disclosure agreement protecting confidential information shared between parties. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Investment Agreement
Definitive contract in which an investor buys shares, setting price, rights, protections and conditions. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Service Agreement
Contract under which one party provides services to another for a fee. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### SLA
Service level agreement: measurable commitments on quality, availability and remedies within a service contract. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Non-Compete
Clause restricting a person from competing after leaving, enforceable only if reasonable and not contrary to public policy. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Licence Agreement
Permission to use IP or software on defined terms without transferring ownership. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### EULA
End-user licence agreement: licence terms an end user accepts to use software. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### IP Licensing
Granting rights to use intellectual property while the owner keeps title. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Ownership
Who legally holds title to assets such as IP; for startups, usually fixed by assignment clauses, not by who created it. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Open Source
Software released under licences that allow use and modification, sometimes with conditions such as copyleft. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### ESOP
Employee stock option plan: programme granting staff options over company shares as incentive. Also known as: Employee Stock Option Plan. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Angel Investors
Wealthy individuals who invest their own money in early-stage startups. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Fundraising
Raising capital through angels, VC rounds or an IPO, with legal terms at each stage. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Exit Strategy
Planned route for owners to realise value, such as acquisition or IPO. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Valuation
An estimate of a company's worth, used to price investment rounds and equity. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Cloud Computing
Delivery of computing as a service, in the IaaS, PaaS and SaaS models, with distinct legal responsibilities. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Regulatory Compliance
Meeting the laws and regulations that apply to a business and its product. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Employment Law
Rules on hiring, working conditions, termination and employee rights. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Antitrust Law
Competition law that prohibits cartels and abuse of market power. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Privacy Policy
Public notice telling users what personal data is collected and how it is used. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

### Terms of Service
The contract governing a user's use of a product or website. Taught in [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 02]].

## Company law & corporate structures

### Types of Legal Entities
The forms a business can take: sole proprietor, partnership, association, cooperative society, statutory corporation and company. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Sole Proprietor
Individual running a business in their own name with unlimited personal liability. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Partnership
Business run by partners who share profits and bear personal liability. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Association (Amuta)
Non-profit entity whose profits may not be distributed to members. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Cooperative Society
Member-owned entity run on democratic one-member-one-vote lines (see the Pri HaEmek case). Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Statutory Corporation
Entity created by its own statute, such as a public body. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Certificate of Incorporation
Registrar's document confirming a company has been formed. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Limited Liability
Shareholders risk only what they invested; company debts are not theirs. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Corporate Governance
The system by which a company is directed and controlled, including its organs and checks. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Board of Directors
Organ that sets policy and supervises management (Companies Law s.57 and s.92 on powers and delegation). Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Minority Rights
Protections for minority shareholders against majority abuse. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Derivative Suit
Claim a shareholder brings on the company's behalf against those who harmed it. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Private vs Public Company
Private companies restrict share transfers and have few holders; public companies offer shares to the public and face stricter disclosure. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Share
Unit of ownership in a company, carrying rights such as voting and dividends (Companies Law s.188/190). Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Dividend
Distribution of company profits to shareholders. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Bond
Debt security in which the issuer owes holders fixed repayments and interest. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Option (Security)
Right, but not the obligation, to buy shares at a fixed strike price. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Agency Problem
Conflict between those who own (principals) and those who manage or control (agents), or between shareholders and creditors (s.192(a)). Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Duty of Loyalty
Officer's duty to act in the company's interest and avoid conflicts of interest. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Duty of Care
Officer's duty to act with reasonable skill and diligence. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Separate Legal Personality
A company is a legal person distinct from its shareholders (Companies Law s.4). Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Piercing the Corporate Veil
Exceptional court power to disregard separate personality and hold shareholders personally liable for abuse (s.6). Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Organ Theory
Attributes the mind and conduct of a senior officer (an organ) to the corporation itself. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Salomon Case
1897 English case (Salomon v Salomon & Co) establishing that a company is a distinct legal entity even if one shareholder runs it. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Tzuk Or Case
CA 407/89: Justice Barak rejects the idea that a corporation is artificial; it is a real bearer of rights and duties that acts only through people. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Modiin Case
Supreme Court case holding that organ theory can impose criminal liability on a company, but only where justified (for example tax offences). Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Subordination Doctrine
Delaying repayment of a shareholder's loan until external creditors are paid in full (s.6(c)); gentler than piercing the veil. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Thin Capitalization
Funding a company with minimal equity and mostly shareholder loans, shifting risk to creditors. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Northern Chicks Case
CA 4263/04: thin capitalization plus improper management led to subordination of shareholder loans behind other creditors. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Pri HaEmek Case
Majority of a cooperative's general meeting could not impose the cooperative's debts on members; that needs unanimous consent. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Israeli Companies Law
Companies Law 1999, the statute governing Israeli companies. Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

### Securities
Instruments a company issues to fund itself: shares (ownership), bonds (debt) and options (a right to buy). Taught in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 03]].

## Contract law

### Contract
Legally binding agreement formed by offer and acceptance, intention to be bound, capacity, legality and certainty (no consideration needed in Israel). Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Offer and Acceptance
The formation mechanism: one party's definite proposal and the other's matching acceptance (Contracts Law s.1/2/5). Also known as: offer and acceptance. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Intention to Create Legal Relations
Parties must mean to be legally bound; tested objectively and practically (Levin v Levin). Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Legal Capacity
Ability to contract: minors and incapacitated persons lack it. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Consideration
Common-law requirement that each side give value; Israeli law dropped it. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Pacta Sunt Servanda
Agreements must be kept. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Obligatory Transaction
Contract that faces the future and carries risk, with performance and payment happening later (investment, employment, SaaS, vesting). Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Real Transaction
Immediate exchange of goods for payment on the spot, with no future risk. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Freedom of Contract
Parties may set the content of their contract (s.24), limited by public policy; today weak and eroded. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Public Policy
Basic social values that void contracts contrary to them (s.30). Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Cogent Provisions
Mandatory legal rules a contract may not override. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Market Overt Rule
Sale Law s.34: a good-faith buyer acquires title if four conditions are met, including a sale in the seller's ordinary course of business. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Contract Interpretation
Determining what the parties meant, balancing language against circumstances (s.25). Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Apropim Doctrine
Barak's pendulum model of interpretation moving between language and circumstances; softened by Vegetable Growers and the 2011 s.25 amendment. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Section 25 Contracts Law
Interpretation section; 2011 amendment reads language and circumstances together, with clear explicit language governing. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Serious Intention (Gmirot Da'at)
Mental element of a contract, tested objectively but bending for signers with impaired capacity (Barashi). Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Definiteness (Mesuyamut)
Contract terms must be specific enough to enforce, though courts fill gaps. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Completion Mechanisms
Tools for filling gaps: agreed mechanism, dispositive law, optimal performance, custom, reasonableness. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Writing Requirement
Some contracts, notably land sales (Land Law s.8), must be in writing, softened by good faith in Kalmer v Gai. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Memorandum of Understanding
Interim agreement whose binding force depends on the parties' intent and definiteness (Rabinai). Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Special Contract Laws
Statutes for particular contracts (consumer, standard contracts, insurance, gifts, sale, banking) that protect the weaker party: the cry of fairness. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Israeli Contracts Law
Contracts (General Part) Law 1973, the main statute on formation, interpretation and remedies. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Achi Tekuma Case
CA 3158/20: a concealed illegal deal is void; illegality of purpose cannot be cured by hiding it. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Egged v Mashiach Case
Indirect restraint on freedom of occupation is treated as direct and may be void as contrary to public policy. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Rekant Case
The Barak-Zamir debate on whether equality overrides freedom of contract. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Zim v Maziar Case
Public policy voids exemption clauses over life and safety; freedom of contract stands below sanctity of life. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Lagil Trampoline Case
A liability-waiver sign was not part of the contract and could not waive sanctity of life. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Levin v Levin Case
A clause saying an agreement has no legal effect does not stop it binding if substance shows genuine intent; also a clause barring court access is void. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Knaan Case
A flea-market buyer of a stolen painting could not keep it because the sale was outside the seller's ordinary course of business (market overt failed). Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Apropim Case
Ministry of Housing apartment-purchase case in which Barak set out the pendulum interpretation model (see Apropim Doctrine). Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Vegetable Growers Association Case
Rivlin's partial pushback: language is the starting point of interpretation and cannot bear a meaning it cannot hold. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Peretz Bonei HaNegev Case
CA 1932/90: seller who claimed he was illiterate and misunderstood the deal; the objective test bends for impaired understanding. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Barashi Case
CA 3601/96: gift contract signed by a mentally unstable elderly man; objective test insufficient where capacity is impaired. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Hof HaTchelet Case
CA 133/89: sale of "one of" the plots; court completes missing terms unless the gap shows no consent. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Dor Energy Case
Eroded real-estate essentials to delivery date, price and type of transaction; courts complete the rest to save the contract. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Kalmer v Gai Case
Good faith can override the writing requirement in exceptional cases (reliance and fault of the withdrawing party). Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

### Rabinai Case
CA 77/158: a memorandum with all essential terms is a binding contract; substance beats the MOU label. Taught in [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 04]].

## Intellectual property

### Property Right
Right in a thing enforceable against everyone (in rem), stronger than a contractual right. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Movables
Tangible items that can be moved, as distinct from land. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Real Estate
Land and fixtures attached to it. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Absolute Rights
Rights good against the whole world, such as ownership. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Limited Rights
Rights over another's property that are narrower than ownership. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Intellectual Property Rights
Legal rights over creations of the mind: copyright, patents, trade secrets, trademarks and designs. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Copyright
Automatic protection for original expression, with no registration needed. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Patent
Time-limited (20-year) monopoly on a novel, inventive, industrially applicable invention, granted on registration. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Trade Secret
Confidential commercial information protected for as long as secrecy lasts, with no protection against independent discovery. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Trademark
Distinctive sign identifying a business's goods or services. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Design (IP)
Protection for the visual appearance of an industrial product. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Novelty
Patent requirement that the invention is not already known. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Inventive Step
Patent requirement that the invention is not obvious to a skilled person. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Non-Triviality
Patent requirement that the invention goes beyond the obvious, used with inventive step. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Technological Applicability
Patent requirement that the invention has industrial or technical use. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Patent Claims
The part of a patent that defines the scope of protection. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Israel Patent Authority
Israeli body that examines and registers patents, trademarks and designs. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Patents Law 1967
Israeli statute governing patents. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Maintenance Fees
Periodic fees needed to keep a patent in force. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Distinctiveness
Trademark requirement that a sign identifies one trader's goods. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Trademarks Ordinance 1972
Israeli law governing trademark registration. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Designs Law
Israeli statute protecting registered industrial designs. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### AI and Copyright
Open questions on authorship of AI output and use of protected works as training data. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Fair Use
US doctrine allowing limited use of protected works without permission; central to AI training disputes. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Andersen v Stability AI Case
US case on whether training image generators on protected works infringes copyright. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### Thaler v Vidal Case
US ruling that only a natural person can be a patent inventor, leaving room for AI-assisted human inventions. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

### New York Times AI Case
Newspaper's claim that AI training on its articles infringes copyright. Taught in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 05]].

## Employee stock options & Section 102 tax

### Exercise Price
Price the holder pays per share when exercising an option; also called strike price. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Strike Price
Fixed price at which an option holder may buy shares, set at grant. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Vesting Schedule
Timetable on which options or shares vest, for example monthly over four years. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Vesting Cliff
Initial period (often one year) before any equity vests. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Expiration Date (Options)
Date, typically around 10 years from grant, after which an option lapses. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Cause (Termination)
Dismissal for cause, after which even vested options are forfeited. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Section 102
Tax Ordinance section giving employees and officers (not controlling shareholders) favourable option taxation through a trustee. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].
Seen in: [[Accounting]]

### Section 3(i)
Tax Ordinance section taxing options of consultants and controlling shareholders at exercise as ordinary income. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Israeli Tax Ordinance
Income Tax Ordinance, the main Israeli tax statute. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Capital Gains Tax
Tax on profit from selling an asset; 25% on the Section 102 capital gain track. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Marginal Tax Rate
Rate on the next shekel of income, up to about 59% in Israel. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Trustee
Third party that holds Section 102 options and shares for the employee. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Holding Period
Minimum 24 months the trustee must hold options or shares to qualify for capital gain treatment. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Capital Gain Track
Section 102 track taxing gains at 25% after the holding period. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Ordinary Income Track
Section 102 track taxing gains as salary, up to about 59%. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Non-Trustee Track
Section 102 route without a trustee, taxed at exercise. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### Israel Tax Authority
Israeli tax authority that receives Section 102 filings and approves plans. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

### 409A Valuation
US independent valuation setting the fair market value of common stock for option pricing. Taught in [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 06]].

## Digital regulation & data protection

### Legal Product
The idea that law is built into product design, so each interface and data flow is a legal decision. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### EU Digital Single Market
EU programme of digital rules in three generations: GDPR, then DSA and DMA, then the AI Act. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Digital Services Act
EU law on platform transparency and content responsibility. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Digital Markets Act
EU law restricting gatekeeper platforms to keep digital markets competitive. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### EU AI Act
EU risk-based regulation of AI with duties for providers and deployers. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Brussels Effect
EU rules becoming the global standard because firms apply them everywhere. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### GDPR
General Data Protection Regulation: EU privacy law with extraterritorial reach (Art. 3) over all entities handling EU residents' data. Also known as: General Data Protection Regulation. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Personally Identifiable Information
Any information relating to an identifiable person, including IP addresses and cookie IDs. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Privacy by Design
Building data protection into a product from the start. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Data Controller
Entity that decides the purposes and means of processing personal data. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Data Processor
Entity that processes personal data on a controller's behalf. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Data Protection Officer
Role overseeing GDPR compliance inside an organisation. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Data Subject
The individual whose personal data is processed. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Data Subject Rights
Eight rights, including access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection and protection from automated decisions. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Consent (GDPR)
One of six lawful bases; must be freely given, specific, informed and as easy to withdraw as to give. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Legitimate Interest
Lawful basis balancing the controller's interest against the individual's rights. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Purpose Limitation
Article 5 principle: use data only for the purposes collected for. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Data Minimization
Article 5 principle: collect only the data needed. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Right to be Forgotten
Right to have data erased or de-listed, born in the Google Spain case. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Data Portability
Right to receive personal data in a reusable format and move it. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Data Subject Access Request
Request by an individual to see the personal data a controller holds. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Article 22 GDPR
Right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with significant effects (see SCHUFA). Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### DPIA
Data protection impact assessment, required under Art. 35 for high-risk processing. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### RoPA
Record of processing activities required by Art. 30. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Adequacy Decision
EU finding under Art. 45 that a country gives adequate protection, allowing free transfers. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Standard Contractual Clauses
EU-approved contract terms enabling transfers to non-adequate countries. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Binding Corporate Rules
Internal group rules approved for intra-group international transfers. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Amendment 13
Amendment 13 to Israel's Privacy Protection Law, tied to keeping Israel's EU adequacy status. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Extraterritoriality
Law applying beyond the territory of the legislating state, as GDPR does under Art. 3. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### CJEU
Court of Justice of the European Union, final interpreter of EU law. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Google Spain Case
2014 CJEU ruling creating the right to be forgotten; search engines are controllers. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### SCHUFA Case
CJEU ruling of 7 Dec 2023 that automated credit scoring can fall under Art. 22. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Meta Ruling
CJEU ruling of 4 Jul 2023 on whether terms of use can serve as the basis for consent or contract. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Schrems I Case
2015 CJEU ruling invalidating the EU-US Safe Harbor. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Schrems II Case
2020 CJEU ruling invalidating Privacy Shield and requiring case-by-case checks of SCCs. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Safe Harbor
EU-US transfer framework (2000) struck down in Schrems I. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Privacy Shield
Safe Harbor's 2016 replacement, struck down in Schrems II. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Data Privacy Framework
EU-US transfer framework adopted after Schrems II. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Snowden Affair
2013 leaks showing US firms gave the NSA access to user data (PRISM), which undermined trust in US data protection. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### ePrivacy Directive
EU rules on cookies and electronic communications requiring consent to place cookies. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Cookies
Small files placed on a device for tracking and sessions; regulated by ePrivacy and treated as PII under GDPR. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### LGPD
Brazil's general data protection law. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### PIPL
China's Personal Information Protection Law. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### APPI
Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### DPDPA 2023
India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Supervisory Authority
National body enforcing GDPR. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### EDPB
European Data Protection Board coordinating national authorities. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

### Privacy
Why data-protection law shifted the burden from individuals to the companies holding their data, leading to Privacy by Design. Taught in [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 07]].

## Cap tables, valuation & dilution

### Cap Table
Table managing a company's share capital; ownership percentage is a holder's shares divided by total outstanding. Taught in [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Lec 08]].
Seen in: [[Accounting]]

### Dilution
Fall in existing holders' ownership percentage when new shares are issued. Taught in [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Lec 08]].

### Fully Diluted Share Capital
Issued shares plus all potential shares from options (granted and ungranted), warrants and convertible notes. Taught in [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Lec 08]].

### Pre-Money Valuation
Agreed company value before the new investment. Taught in [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Lec 08]].
Seen in: [[Accounting]]

### Post-Money Valuation
Pre-money valuation plus the new investment. Taught in [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Lec 08]].
Seen in: [[Accounting]]

### Price Per Share
Pre-money valuation divided by pre-round fully diluted shares. Taught in [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Lec 08]].

### Option Pool
Block of shares reserved for employee options, often created pre-money as an investment condition. Taught in [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Lec 08]].
