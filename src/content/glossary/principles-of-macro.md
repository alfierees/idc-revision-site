# Intro Macro Concepts

> Searchable concept index for [[Macro-Economics (Intro)]]. Each entry points to the note that teaches it.

## Measuring GDP & prices

### Gross Domestic Product
Total market value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a period; a geographic measure, written Y. Also in [Ex 04](/subjects/principles-of-macro/problem-sets/exercise-4). Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]

### Nominal GDP
GDP measured at the prices of the year it is produced, so it rises with both quantities and prices. → [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]

### Real GDP
GDP measured at constant (base-year) prices, RGDP = GDP / (1 + inflator); strips out inflation so only quantities change. Also in [Ex 04](/subjects/principles-of-macro/problem-sets/exercise-4). → [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]

### GDP Deflator
Price index implied by GDP, nominal GDP divided by real GDP; its change is the GDP inflator. Also in [Ex 04](/subjects/principles-of-macro/problem-sets/exercise-4). Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]

### Growth Rate
Relative change of any variable, g = F(t+s)/F(t) - 1; the average per-period rate uses the s-th root. Also in [Ex 04](/subjects/principles-of-macro/problem-sets/exercise-4). → [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]

### GDP per Capita
GDP divided by population; a first proxy for average material living standards that ignores distribution. Also in [Ex 04](/subjects/principles-of-macro/problem-sets/exercise-4), [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]. → [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]

### Consumer Price Index
Index of the cost of a fixed basket of goods bought by households, used to measure consumer inflation. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]

### Producer Price Index
Index of the prices producers receive for their output, a producer-side measure of inflation. → [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]

### Inflation
Rate of change of the general price level over a period, pi = p(t+s)/p(t) - 1. Also in [Ex 04](/subjects/principles-of-macro/problem-sets/exercise-4). Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]

### Gross National Product
Value of final output produced by a country's residents wherever they produce it, GNP = GDP − NIFF, where NIFF is net payments to foreign factors. Also in [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]

## The three approaches & national accounts

### Gross Value Added
A firm's sales minus the cost of the intermediate goods it buys; the extra value created at that stage. → [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]

### Value-Added Approach
Measures GDP as the sum of value added across all firms, which avoids double-counting intermediate goods. → [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]

### Income Approach
Measures GDP as the total income earned from production: wages, rent, interest and profits (plus depreciation and taxes). → [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]

### Expenditure Approach
Measures GDP as total spending on final goods, Y = C + I + G + NX. Also in [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]. → [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]

### Uses and Resources
The identity listing where output goes (consumption, investment, government, exports) against where supply comes from (GDP and imports). → [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]

### Private Consumption
Households' spending on final goods and services, the C in the expenditure identity. → [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]

### Net Exports
Exports minus imports, NX = EX - IM; the trade balance and the economy's net investment abroad. Also in [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]], [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]. Seen in: [Macro-Economics II](/subjects/macro-ii). → [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]

### Factors of Production
The inputs used to produce output, mainly labor and capital, whose owners earn the income side of GDP. Also in [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]. → [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]

### Depreciation
Loss of capital value over time through wear and tear; subtracting it from GDP gives net domestic product. Also in [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]

### Aggregate Demand
Total planned spending on the economy's output, Y = C + I + G + NX. Seen in: [Macro-Economics II](/subjects/macro-ii). → [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]

## Savings, investment & GNP

### Savings Identity
Total national saving equals investment plus net exports, S = I + NX; a definitional identity from the income and expenditure views. Also in [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]. → [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]

### Disposable Income
Income left after taxes, Yd = Y - T; what households can spend or save. Also in [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]. → [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]

### Private Savings
Household saving: disposable income not consumed, Sp = Yd - C. Also in [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]. → [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]

### Business Savings
Retained profit after taxes and dividends, Sb = Profit - Taxes - Dividends. Also in [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]. → [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]

### Government Savings
The budget balance, Sg = T - G; positive in surplus, negative in deficit. Also in [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]. → [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]

### Investment
Firms' spending that adds to the physical capital stock (machines, buildings, inventories), not the purchase of financial assets. Also in [[Rec_02-The Three Approaches to Measuring GDP|Rec 02]]. → [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]

### Net Domestic Product
GDP minus depreciation: the output left after replacing worn-out capital. → [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]

### Net Income from Foreign Factors
Income foreign factors earn in the domestic economy minus income residents earn abroad (NIFF); the bridge from GDP to GNP, since GNP = GDP − NIFF. Also in [[Rec_01-Nominal & Real GDP, Price Indices & GNP|Rec 01]]. → [[Rec_03-Capital Accumulation, the Savings Identity & GNP vs GDP|Rec 03]]

## Production & the Cobb-Douglas function

### Production Function
The relation Y = F(K, L, A) mapping capital, labor and productivity into output. Also in [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]. Seen in: [Macro-Economics II](/subjects/macro-ii), [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]

### Cobb-Douglas Production Function
Y = A K^alpha L^(1-alpha); a production function with constant returns to scale and constant factor shares. Also in [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]], [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]. Seen in: [Macro-Economics II](/subjects/macro-ii), [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]

### Total Factor Productivity
The general productivity multiplier A in the production function; better technology or institutions raise output from the same inputs. Also in [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]

### Marginal Product of Labor
Extra output from one more unit of labor, holding capital fixed; it falls as labor rises. Also in [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]

### Marginal Product of Capital
Extra output from one more unit of capital, holding labor fixed; it falls as capital rises. Also in [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]

### Output Elasticity
Percentage change in output for a 1% change in one input; alpha is the output elasticity of capital. → [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]

### Returns to Scale
How output responds when all inputs are scaled by the same factor: increasing, constant or decreasing. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]

### Classical Model
Long-run model with full employment and competitive markets, in which output is set by supply-side factors. Also in [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]. → [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]

### Aggregate Supply
Total output firms are willing to produce; in the classical model it is set by factors of production and technology at full employment. Also in [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]. Seen in: [Macro-Economics II](/subjects/macro-ii). → [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]

## Factor markets & growth accounting

### Labor Market
Competitive market where firms demand and households supply labor, setting the real wage. → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Labor Demand
Firms hire labor until the real wage equals its marginal product, so labor demand follows the MPL curve. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Labor Supply
The quantity of labor households offer at each real wage. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Capital Market
Competitive market where firms rent capital until its marginal product equals the real rental rate. → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Nominal Wage
The wage W in money terms. → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Real Wage
The wage deflated by prices, W/P; what a worker's pay buys in goods. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Real Rental Rate
The price of renting a unit of capital in goods, R/P, equal to the MPK in equilibrium. → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Factor Payments
Total income paid to a factor, its price times its quantity (for example W times L). → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Factor Income Shares
The fractions of GDP paid to each factor; under Cobb-Douglas capital gets alpha and labor gets 1 - alpha. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Euler's Theorem
With constant returns to scale, paying each factor its marginal product exactly exhausts total output. → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Growth Accounting
Decomposition of output growth into contributions from capital, labor and productivity, weighted by factor shares. Also in [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]. → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Solow Residual
The part of output growth that input growth cannot explain, attributed to productivity (TFP) growth. Also in [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics). → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

### Economic Growth
Sustained increase in an economy's output per person over time. Also in [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]. → [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]]

## Long-run growth: the Solow model

### Solow Model
Growth model in which capital per worker changes by savings per worker minus effective depreciation, dk = s A k^alpha - (n + delta) k. Seen in: [Macro-Economics II](/subjects/macro-ii). → [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]

### Steady State
The point where net investment per worker is zero, so capital per worker stops changing. Seen in: [Macro-Economics II](/subjects/macro-ii). → [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]

### Effective Depreciation Rate
n + delta: the rate at which capital per worker must be replaced or spread over a growing workforce. → [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]

### Golden-Rule Savings Rate
The savings rate that maximises long-run consumption per worker; it equals alpha under Cobb-Douglas. Seen in: [Macro-Economics II](/subjects/macro-ii). → [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]

## Open economy accounts

### Export Surplus
Exports exceed imports (NX > 0): the country saves more than it invests and lends the difference abroad. → [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]

### Import Surplus
Imports exceed exports (NX < 0): the country invests more than it saves and borrows the gap from abroad. → [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]]
