# Innovation Concepts

> Searchable concept index for Entrepreneurship. Each entry points to the note that teaches it.

## Key terms

### Intrapreneurship
Acting like an entrepreneur within an established company; creating a new business or venture within an organization (MIT Sloan). → [[Lec_01-Introduction to Innovation|Lec 01]]

### Creativity
The production of novel and useful ideas (Sternberg & Lubart 2007; Csikszentmihalyi 2009). → [[Lec_01-Introduction to Innovation|Lec 01]]

### Innovation
The successful implementation of creative ideas within an organization (Amabile et al. 1996). → [[Lec_01-Introduction to Innovation|Lec 01]]

### Invention
Something newly created that may never be used or benefit anyone; not the same as innovation (Wilson 2016). → [[Lec_01-Introduction to Innovation|Lec 01]]

### Organization
A collection of people pursuing defined objectives through division of work and alignment of tasks (Business Jargons). → [[Lec_01-Introduction to Innovation|Lec 01]]

## Four types of innovation

### Four Types of Innovation
Framework with two axes: inward–outward and operational–strategic. → [[Lec_01-Introduction to Innovation|Lec 01]]

### Product Innovation
Outward, operational: changing, improving or renewing products and/or services, or creating new ones. → [[Lec_01-Introduction to Innovation|Lec 01]]

### Process Innovation
Inward, operational: often efficiency- and quality-driven improvement of how the organization works day to day. → [[Lec_01-Introduction to Innovation|Lec 01]]

### Business Model Innovation
Outward, strategic: changing how the organization creates and captures value, often through new revenue models. → [[Lec_01-Introduction to Innovation|Lec 01]]

### Management Innovation
Inward, strategic: innovating how the organization is organized, managed and led; often implies decentralization. → [[Lec_01-Introduction to Innovation|Lec 01]]

## Strategy concepts

### Business Strategy
The strategic initiatives a company pursues to create value and gain competitive advantage (HBS). → [[Lec_03-Innovation Strategy|Lec 03]]

### Business Model
How a business produces, offers and captures value. → [[Lec_03-Innovation Strategy|Lec 03]]

### Competitive Advantage
A durable edge over rivals; the goal that strategy exists to produce. → [[Lec_03-Innovation Strategy|Lec 03]]

### Blue Ocean Strategy
Creating uncontested new market space where demand is created, not fought over. → [[Lec_03-Innovation Strategy|Lec 03]]

### Red Ocean
Known, existing industries with defined boundaries and head-to-head competition. → [[Lec_03-Innovation Strategy|Lec 03]]

### Porter's Five Forces
Porter (1979) framework: new entrants, supplier power, buyer power, substitutes, rivalry — diagnoses industry attractiveness/profitability. → [[Lec_03-Innovation Strategy|Lec 03]]

## Culture concepts

### Organizational Culture
Shared values, beliefs & behaviours shaping how members think, act and interact (The Business Dictionary). → [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation|Lec 06]]

### OCAI Framework
Cameron & Quinn's Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument; four culture types (clan / adhocracy / market / hierarchy). → [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation|Lec 06]]

### Clan Culture
Family-like, loyalty/tradition; supportive & collaborative (Lego). → [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation|Lec 06]]

### Adhocracy Culture
Dynamic, entrepreneurial, risk-taking; most conducive to radical innovation (Nvidia). → [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation|Lec 06]]

### Market Culture
Results-oriented, competitive; drives incremental, market-facing innovation (GE). → [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation|Lec 06]]

### Hierarchy Culture
Formalised, procedure-governed; efficiency-focused, limits radical innovation (McKinsey). → [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation|Lec 06]]

## Barriers & innovation types

### Barriers to Innovation
Factors that prevent/hinder innovative activity; internal vs. external (Sandberg & Aarikka-Stenroos 2014). → [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation|Lec 06]]

### Radical Innovation
Fundamentally new products/services/processes with major market impact; faces the steepest barriers. → [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation|Lec 06]]

### Discovery Competences
Ability to identify & articulate innovation opportunities (incubation → acceleration → commercialisation pipeline). → [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation|Lec 06]]

## Barrier taxonomy (Sandberg & Aarikka-Stenroos 2014)

### External Barriers to Innovation
Obstacles outside the firm's boundary: resistance from specific actors + a restrictive macro environment. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

### Internal Barriers to Innovation
Obstacles inside the firm: restrictive mindset, lack of competences, insufficient resources, unsupportive structure. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

## External — specific actors

### Customer Resistance
Customers reluctant to adopt due to changing needs / lack of experience with the new technology. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

### Unsupportive Government
Restrictive regulations, laws, and standards imposed by the state. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

### Shortage of External Finance
Conservatism of financial institutions toward innovative firms, limiting funding. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

### Rivalry
Competitors' advancements pushing competing technologies (echoes Porter's rivalry force). → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

## External — macro environment

### Undeveloped Network & Ecosystem
Resistance/inertia within the stakeholder network that innovation depends on. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

### Technological Turbulence
Rapid, unpredictable tech change that makes committing to one technology risky. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

### Inappropriate Infrastructure
Inadequate facilities/services that impede implementation. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

### Restrictive Local Culture
Cultural/religious beliefs that lead to resistance against adoption. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

## Internal — competence pipeline

### Incubation Competences
Ability to develop opportunities into viable business proposals. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

### Acceleration & Commercialisation Competences
Ability to scale a new innovation to a self-standing level. → [[Reading-Barriers to Innovation|Barriers reading]]

## Google case study

Google/Alphabet as a deliberately engineered adhocracy innovation culture. → [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation|Lec 06]]

### Googliness

## Leadership concepts

### Leadership
An influence relationship among leaders and followers who intend real change reflecting shared purposes (Rost 1993). → [[Lec_07-Innovation Leadership|Lec 07]]

### Followership
The follower role; everyone is a follower at times, most of us more often than we lead. → [[Lec_07-Innovation Leadership|Lec 07]]

### Organizational Vision
A clear, inspiring portrayal of what a firm aspires to achieve in the future (the horizon). → [[Lec_07-Innovation Leadership|Lec 07]]

### Transformational Leadership
A soft leadership style inspiring followers past self-interest toward a greater vision; leads change in vision, strategy & culture (Grădinaru et al. 2020). → [[Lec_07-Innovation Leadership|Lec 07]]

### Leadership vs Management
Leadership = vision/horizon/"right things"; management = resources/schedules/"things right." → [[Lec_07-Innovation Leadership|Lec 07]]

## Creativity theory

### Componential Model of Creativity
Amabile & Pratt (2016) dynamic model: creativity = domain skills × creativity-relevant processes × task motivation, mirrored at the organisational level and linked by dynamic feedback loops. → [[Lec_08-Problem Solving|Lec 08]]

### Intrinsic Motivation
Drive from interest, enjoyment and meaning in the task itself; sustains creative persistence. → [[Lec_08-Problem Solving|Lec 08]]

### Extrinsic Motivation
Drive from outside factors (pay, deadlines, rewards); can support or crowd out creativity. → [[Lec_08-Problem Solving|Lec 08]]

### Meaningful Work
For individuals and organisations, work must feel significant to sustain intrinsic motivation and creative persistence. → [[Lec_08-Problem Solving|Lec 08]]

### Psychological Safety
A climate where people can take risks and share unconventional ideas without fear of ridicule; precondition for divergent thinking and learning from failure. → [[Lec_08-Problem Solving|Lec 08]]

### Progress Principle
Creativity is fuelled by perceived progress in meaningful work; small wins compound intrinsic motivation. → [[Lec_08-Problem Solving|Lec 08]]

## Ideation techniques

### Brainstorming
Osborn (1950s, BBDO) CPS technique; four rules: defer judgment, quantity over quality, wild ideas, build on others. → [[Lec_08-Problem Solving|Lec 08]]

### Lateral Thinking
De Bono's approach of breaking from linear/vertical thinking to reach non-obvious ideas via discontinuous jumps. → [[Lec_08-Problem Solving|Lec 08]]

### Six Thinking Hats
De Bono's parallel-thinking tool: white (facts), red (emotions), black (critique), yellow (positives), green (creativity), blue (meta/process). → [[Lec_08-Problem Solving|Lec 08]]

## Design Thinking core

### Design Thinking
User-centered innovation method starting from user needs (IDEO — David Kelley & Tim Brown; Stanford d.school); five stages: Empathy → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test. → [[Lec_09-Practical Innovation 1|Lec 09]]

### IDEO
Global design consultancy that pioneered and popularised design thinking (Kelley = founder, Brown = CEO). → [[Lec_09-Practical Innovation 1|Lec 09]]

### Desirability-Feasibility-Viability
The three lenses a good solution must satisfy: desirable (people), feasible (technology), viable (business). → [[Lec_09-Practical Innovation 1|Lec 09]]

### Double Diamond
UK Design Council process model: problem diamond (Discover → Define) + solution diamond (Develop → Deliver), each alternating divergent and convergent thinking. → [[Lec_09-Practical Innovation 1|Lec 09]]

## Discover & Define tools

### Empathy
Seeing the world through the user's eyes; the load-bearing first stage; its absence causes product failure. → [[Lec_09-Practical Innovation 1|Lec 09]]

### Empathy Map
Tool turning behavioural observation into actionable insight via quadrants (Say / Think / Do / Feel). → [[Lec_09-Practical Innovation 1|Lec 09]]

### Persona
Data-grounded fictional representation of a target customer; gives a segment a face and name and a shared team reference. → [[Lec_09-Practical Innovation 1|Lec 09]]

## Discover tools

### User Interviews
Listening-first discovery: define your learning goal, pick representative users, ask open-ended questions, record everything; primary goal is deep understanding of user experiences and needs. → [[Lec_10-Practical Innovation 2|Lec 10]]

### Customer Journey Map
Visual representation of the steps, interactions, and emotions a customer experiences across touchpoints, before/during/after engagement; mark one high-impact moment. → [[Lec_10-Practical Innovation 2|Lec 10]]

### Touchpoint
Any moment of interaction between a customer and a brand, product, or service — online, in person, or via communication channels — before, during, or after a purchase. → [[Lec_10-Practical Innovation 2|Lec 10]]

## Define tools

### Design Question
The "How might we…" sentence that closes the first diamond: user + need + problem, broad enough to invite ideas, narrow enough to act on; must be framed *before* brainstorming. → [[Lec_10-Practical Innovation 2|Lec 10]]

## Prototyping concepts

### Rapid Prototyping
Building the simplest, least costly, quickest model of a solution to iterate and gather feedback efficiently; cheapness buys the willingness to discard. → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

### Prototype
An early model or sample of a product created to test and evaluate concepts, design, functionality and usability; a tool for thinking, not a final product. → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

### Plan-Build-Test
The three-step prototyping loop: decide what to learn (Plan), build a low-fidelity model of the critical feature (Build), test with real users and refine (Test). → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

### Fidelity
How closely a prototype resembles/behaves like the finished product; rises across wireframe → mockup → interactive prototype; match fidelity to the learning goal. → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

### Wireframe
A digital sketch of the UI layout without detailed design elements (skeleton/structure only). → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

### Mockup
A static representation of the product with visual detail but no interactive functionality (looks real, doesn't respond). → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

### Interactive Prototype
A clickable digital prototype simulating the user experience (Figma / InVision / Adobe XD). → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

### User Testing
Testing the prototype with (≥ two) real users, observing behaviour rather than opinions, to gather insights and fixes. → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

### Iteration
The repeated test → learn → refine cycle; where the real design emerges (the "fix" in Develop's "test and fix"). → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

### Product Validation
Confirming via user testing that the refined solution actually meets user needs; the goal of Deliver. → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

### GTM Canvas
Go-to-Market Canvas; one of the three required assignment maps (with Empathy Map and Persona), introduced in the following class. → [[Lec_11-Practical Innovation 3|Lec 11]]

## Structure types

### Organisational Structure
Arrangement of roles, responsibilities & relationships that coordinates activity toward goals. → [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure|Lec 02]]

### Hierarchical Structure
Tall pyramid; centralised, top-down authority (CEO → layers → base staff). → [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure|Lec 02]]

### Functional Structure
Departments grouped by expertise, led by functional heads; deep skill but siloed. → [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure|Lec 02]]

### Flat Structure
Few/no management layers; decentralised, fast decisions; common in startups. → [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure|Lec 02]]

### Matrix Structure
Dual reporting to a functional **and** a project manager; cross-functional but "two bosses." → [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure|Lec 02]]

## Innovation design

### Organizational Innovation
The ecosystem of strategy, leadership, structure & culture that makes a firm innovative. → [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure|Lec 02]]

### Open Innovation
Sourcing/sharing innovation across the organisation's boundary (partners, ecosystem). → [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure|Lec 02]]

### Centralized Innovation Design
Innovation strategy & resources concentrated in a central department (exemplar: Apple). → [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure|Lec 02]]

### Decentralized Innovation Design
Each business unit owns its own innovation (exemplar: Johnson & Johnson). → [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure|Lec 02]]

### Hybrid Innovation Design
Central corporate strategy connected to each BU's innovation effort (exemplar: Procter & Gamble). → [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure|Lec 02]]
