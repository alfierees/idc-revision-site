## Academic texts and literature

### Academic Text
A text meeting five criteria: academic author, formal language, credible references, research-based content and academic structure. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Non-Academic Source
A source outside academic publishing, such as a professional database, research center, primary record, website or media outlet, whose credibility depends on its evidence. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Literature Review
A synthesis of existing research that forms the knowledge platform of a study, justifies it and leads to its question and hypotheses. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Gap in the Literature
An area where little or no research has been conducted. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Primary Source
Original research articles or documents. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Secondary Source
A summary, review or analysis of primary sources. → [[Lec_02-APA Citing and Referencing|Lec 02]]

### Theoretical Framework
The structure that supports the theory underlying the research. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Systematic Review
A methodical summary of all relevant studies on a specific topic. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Citation Network
A web of interconnected sources showing how studies reference each other. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Annotated Bibliography
A list of sources with summaries and evaluations. → [[Lec_02-APA Citing and Referencing|Lec 02]]

### Abstract
A concise summary of a research paper's content. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

## Citing and referencing

### APA Style
The American Psychological Association rules for citing and documenting sources, used here in the seventh edition. → [[Lec_02-APA Citing and Referencing|Lec 02]]

### Plagiarism
Presenting someone else's idea as your own. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### In-Text Citation
Credit given within the paper, usually the author's surname and the year in parentheses. → [[Lec_02-APA Citing and Referencing|Lec 02]]

### Reference List
The list at the end of a paper containing every source cited in the text, and only those. → [[Lec_02-APA Citing and Referencing|Lec 02]]

### Et Al
Latin shorthand for 'and others', used in the text after the first author of a work with three or more authors. → [[Lec_02-APA Citing and Referencing|Lec 02]]

### DOI
A digital object identifier: a unique, persistent link to a published work. → [[Lec_02-APA Citing and Referencing|Lec 02]]

## Generative AI in research

### PAIR Framework
A four-step approach to generative AI: Problem formulation, AI tool selection, Interaction and Reflection. → [[Lec_03-Generative AI Skills and the PAIR Framework|Lec 03]]

### Fabricated Citation
A citation or reference invented by an AI tool that does not correspond to a real source. → [[Lec_03-Generative AI Skills and the PAIR Framework|Lec 03]]

## Research problems and hypotheses

### Research Problem
A clearly formulated, empirically testable issue expressing a relationship between two or more variables. → [[Lec_04-Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses|Lec 04]]

### Research Question
A specific question the study aims to address, usually about the relationship between variables. → [[Lec_04-Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses|Lec 04]]

### Hypothesis
A testable statement, made before collecting data, predicting the existence, direction or size of a relationship between variables. → [[Lec_04-Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses|Lec 04]]

### Research Objective
The purpose or goal of the study. → [[Lec_04-Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses|Lec 04]]

### Operational Definition
A clear description of how a concept will be measured or identified. → [[Lec_04-Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses|Lec 04]]

### Scope
The boundaries of a study, defining what is included and excluded. → [[Lec_04-Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses|Lec 04]]

### Assumptions
Fundamental premises accepted as true without direct proof. → [[Lec_04-Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses|Lec 04]]

### Exploratory Research
Research that gets to know a phenomenon and helps formulate problems or hypotheses for later studies. → [[Lec_04-Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses|Lec 04]]

### Descriptive Research
Research that accurately describes characteristics, frequencies or the strength of relationships, without showing cause. → [[Lec_04-Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses|Lec 04]]

### Causal Research
Research that tests whether one variable causes another, using an experimental design. → [[Lec_04-Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses|Lec 04]]

### Explanatory Research
Research that addresses the 'why' or 'how' of a relationship. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

## Research design

### Research Design
The overall strategy for conducting a study, covering sample, tools, procedure and analysis. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Quantitative Research
Research that identifies and numerically describes variables to establish the relationship between them. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Qualitative Research
Research into processes not readily measured in quantity, amount or frequency. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Mixed Methods
A research approach combining quantitative and qualitative methods. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

### Experiment
A method that manipulates one or more independent variables and measures the effect on the dependent variable. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Experimental Group
The group in an experiment whose independent variable is manipulated. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Control Group
The group in an experiment whose independent variable is not manipulated. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Longitudinal Design
Repeated data collection from a single group over months or years. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Cross-Sectional Design
The same questionnaire given to a number of groups. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Pilot Study
A preliminary test of research methods or a questionnaire on a small representative group. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Bias
Systematic error that can affect the validity of research findings. → [[Lec_09-Course Summary|Lec 09]]

### Ethical Considerations
Principles ensuring research respects participants' rights, including confidentiality and informed consent. → [[Lec_01-Academic Texts and the Research Process|Lec 01]]

## Variables

### Independent Variable
The presumed cause, which the researcher manipulates in an experiment. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Dependent Variable
The effect being measured or observed, whose value depends on the independent variable. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Control Variable
A variable kept constant so it does not influence the dependent variable. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Mediator
A variable caused by the independent variable that influences the dependent variable, explaining the process linking them. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Moderator
A variable that changes the strength or direction of the relationship between two variables. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Third Variable Problem
When another variable affects both measured variables, making them appear causally related. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Directionality Problem
When two variables correlate but it is impossible to conclude which one causes the other. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

## Correlation and statistical analysis

### Correlation
A measure of the relationship between two variables that change together. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Causation
A cause-and-effect relationship in which changes in one variable bring about changes in another. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Positive Correlation
A relationship in which two variables increase or decrease together. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Negative Correlation
A relationship in which one variable increases as the other decreases. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Correlation Coefficient
A number giving the strength of a correlation, with its sign giving the direction. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### P-Value
The probability of a result at least this extreme if there were truly no relationship. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Statistical Significance
A judgement that a result is unlikely to be due to chance, typically when the p-value is below 0.05. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Descriptive Statistics
Techniques for summarizing data, such as the mean, median and mode. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Inferential Statistics
Techniques for drawing conclusions about a population from a sample. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Regression Analysis
A statistical technique for examining relationships between variables. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Outliers
Data points that differ significantly from other observations. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Vanity Metric
A metric that feels good but does not change how you act. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Actionable Metric
A metric that changes behavior by helping you choose a course of action. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

## Measurement and data quality

### Quantitative Data
Information that can be measured or counted and represented numerically. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Qualitative Data
Non-numerical information describing qualities, such as interviews, observations or texts. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Discrete Data
Quantitative data that is counted rather than measured. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Continuous Data
Quantitative data that can be placed on a measurement scale. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Nominal Scale
A level of measurement whose values are only labels, with no order. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Ordinal Scale
A level of measurement that ranks values without establishing equal distances. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Interval Scale
A level of measurement with equal distances and an arbitrary zero, so values can fall below zero. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Ratio Scale
A level of measurement with equal distances and an absolute zero. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Likert Scale
A rating scale of ordered responses, such as very unsatisfied to very satisfied. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Instrument
A tool used for data collection, such as a questionnaire, scale or test. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Reliability
The consistency of a measurement instrument. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Validity
The extent to which an instrument measures what it claims to measure. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### External Validity
The extent to which results generalize beyond the sample. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Internal Validity
The extent to which the independent variable can accurately be said to produce the observed effect. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Construct Validity
How well a measure captures a construct, an idea made up of related behaviors. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Content Validity
Whether experts or target-population members judge that a measure captures a hard-to-measure construct. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Test-Retest Reliability
The consistency of outcomes over time. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Internal Consistency
The consistency of results across different items on the same scale. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Inter-Rater Reliability
Agreement between different observers, which helps prevent personal bias. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

## Sampling

### Population
The entire group of individuals or entities being studied. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Sample
A subset of the population chosen for the study. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Sampling Method
A technique for selecting a sample, such as random, stratified or convenience. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Random Sampling
Sampling in which each unit in the population has an equal chance of selection. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Systematic Sampling
A random probability sampling method named in the course alongside simple sampling. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Non-Probability Sampling
Sampling in which the researcher or other subjective means select the sample. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Convenience Sampling
A non-probability method that samples whoever is easiest to reach. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Snowball Sampling
A method in which the first participants recommend further participants. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Advertising Sampling
Recruiting participants through notices in public places, forums, chats or websites. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Generalizability
The extent to which findings can be applied to broader populations or contexts. → [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation|Lec 05]]

### Response Rate
The percentage of participants who complete a survey or provide data. → [[Lec_09-Course Summary|Lec 09]]

## Surveys and questionnaires

### Survey
A research method for collecting and analyzing data from a sample to learn about a group. → [[Lec_06-Quantitative Data Collection|Lec 06]]

### Questionnaire
A set of questions collecting participants' views, preferences, experiences, intentions and mental states. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Open-Ended Question
A question answered in free form, yielding qualitative data. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Dichotomous Question
A closed question with two answers, usually yes or no. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Multiple-Choice Question
A question in which the participant selects one answer from a given list. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Dummy Question
A question used to disguise the purpose of a study. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Scaling Question
A question based on the measurement scales, typically a Likert item. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Biased Question
A question whose wording pushes the respondent toward an answer or contains an assumption. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

## Qualitative methods

### Interview
A qualitative method that collects in-depth data by asking participants questions. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Structured Interview
An interview with a set list of mostly closed questions asked in order. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Semi-Structured Interview
An interview with prepared questions that the interviewer may change, reorder or add to. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Unstructured Interview
An interview with general themes but no pre-determined questions. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Focus Group
A group interview used to collect qualitative insights. → [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design|Lec 07]]

### Observation
Collecting data by directly monitoring subjects in their surroundings. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Document Analysis
Examining verified documents, such as governmental or historical records, for information and insights. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Case Study
An intensive study of a subject to gain comprehensive understanding. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Gatekeeper
An authority figure who can facilitate permission to reach participants. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Memoing
Keeping a journal of thoughts, reflections and observations during an interview study. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

## Qualitative analysis

### Coding
The process of categorizing qualitative data into themes and sub-themes. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Content Analysis
Systematically identifying patterns in text by quantifying repeated words, phrases, topics or ideas. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Thematic Analysis
Identifying and analyzing patterns in qualitative data. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Deductive Analysis
A top-down approach that applies categories from a prior theory to the data. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Inductive Analysis
A bottom-up approach that builds categories and themes from the data. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Open Coding
A form of inductive analysis that labels the data to build initial codes. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### In Vivo Coding
Coding that uses participants' own words as the codes. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Constant Comparative Analysis
A form of inductive analysis named in the course alongside open and in vivo coding. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]

### Triangulation
Using multiple methods or sources to validate findings. → [[Lec_08-Qualitative Data and Interviews|Lec 08]]
