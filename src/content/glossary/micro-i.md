## Budget constraints

### Budget Constraint
The set of bundles a consumer can afford: total spending at the given prices cannot exceed income. Also in [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]]. → [[Lec_01-Budget Constraint|Lec 01]]

### Budget Line
The boundary of the budget constraint: the bundles that cost exactly the consumer's whole income; its slope is minus the price ratio. Also in [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]]. → [[Lec_01-Budget Constraint|Lec 01]]

### Opportunity Set
All the bundles a consumer can choose from under a constraint; for a budget constraint it is the budget line plus everything under it. → [[Lec_01-Budget Constraint|Lec 01]]

### Opportunity Cost
What must be given up to get one more unit of a good; on the budget line it is the price ratio, the units of one good forgone per unit of the other. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. → [[Lec_01-Budget Constraint|Lec 01]]

### Real Income
The bundles a consumer's money income can buy at current prices; it falls or rises with prices even when money income is unchanged. Also in [[Lec_01-Budget Constraint|Lec 01]]. → [[Lec_07-Substitution and Income Effects|Lec 07]]

## Preferences & indifference curves

### Preferences
A consumer's ranking of bundles: for any two bundles, which one they prefer or whether they are indifferent. Also in [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]], [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Completeness
Any two bundles can be compared: the consumer prefers one or is indifferent between them. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Transitivity
If bundle A is weakly preferred to B and B to C, then A is weakly preferred to C; it is what stops indifference curves crossing. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Weakly Preferred Set
All the bundles the consumer likes at least as much as a given bundle; it includes the indifference curve through that bundle. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Strictly Preferred Set
All the bundles the consumer strictly prefers to a given bundle; it lies above the indifference curve through it. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Indifference Curve
The set of bundles the consumer ranks equally, so they are indifferent between any two points on it. Also in [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]], [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Goods and Bads
A good raises the consumer's satisfaction when they have more of it; a bad lowers it. Which one a commodity is depends on the consumer's own preferences. Also in [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]], [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Economic Bad
A commodity the consumer would rather have less of, so utility falls as the quantity rises and the indifference curve slopes upward. Also in [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Perfect Complements
Two goods the consumer wants in a fixed proportion, such as left and right shoes; the indifference curves are L-shaped. Also in [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]], [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]]. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Perfect Substitutes
Two goods the consumer is willing to swap at a constant rate; the indifference curves are straight lines. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. Also in [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]], [[PS_04-Optimal Choice|PS 04]]. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Satiation Point
A bundle the consumer likes best of all, so that more of either good makes them worse off (a bliss point). → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Well-Behaved Preferences
Preferences that are monotonic and convex: more is better and averages are preferred to extremes, giving downward-sloping, bowed-in indifference curves. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Monotonicity
More of a good is never worse, and strictly better when the good is wanted; it makes indifference curves slope downward. Also in [[PP_07-Labor Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQs (2020 Moed C)|PP 07]]. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Convexity
The consumer weakly prefers an average of two equally good bundles to either one, so indifference curves bow toward the origin. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

### Marginal Rate of Substitution
The rate at which a consumer will give up one good for an extra unit of another and stay equally well off; minus the slope of the indifference curve, and for a utility function the ratio of the marginal utilities. Also in [[Lec_03-Utility and MRS|Lec 03]], [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]], [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]]. → [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]]

## Utility

### Utility Function
A function that assigns a number to each bundle so that preferred bundles get higher numbers. Also in [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]]. → [[Lec_03-Utility and MRS|Lec 03]]

### Ordinal Utility
Utility used only as a ranking device: the numbers say which bundle is preferred, not by how much. Also in [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]]. → [[Lec_03-Utility and MRS|Lec 03]]

### Monotonic Transformation
A rescaling that preserves the order of numbers, such as squaring positive values; it changes the utility numbers but not the preferences or the MRS. Also in [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]]. → [[Lec_03-Utility and MRS|Lec 03]]

### Marginal Utility
The extra utility from one more unit of a good, holding the other good fixed. Also in [[Lec_04-Optimal Bundle|Lec 04]]. → [[Lec_03-Utility and MRS|Lec 03]]

### Neutral Good
A good that does not change the consumer's utility, so the indifference curves are vertical or horizontal; in demand questions, a good whose quantity does not respond to income. Also in [[PP_05-Labour Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2020 Moed A)|PP 05]], [[PP_06-Labor Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2020 Moed B)|PP 06]]. → [[Lec_03-Utility and MRS|Lec 03]]

### Quasilinear Utility
Utility of the form v(x) + y: linear in one good and any function of the other, so the MRS depends only on x. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. Also in [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]], [[PP_02-Fruits & Tofu Multiple Choice (Undated Moed B)|PP 02]]. → [[Lec_03-Utility and MRS|Lec 03]]

### Cobb-Douglas Utility
Utility of the form x^a y^b: smooth, well-behaved preferences, with constant expenditure shares in the optimum. Also in [[Lec_03-Utility and MRS|Lec 03]], [[PP_05-Labour Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2020 Moed A)|PP 05]], [[PP_06-Labor Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2020 Moed B)|PP 06]]. → [[Lec_04-Optimal Bundle|Lec 04]]

## Optimal choice

### Optimal Choice
The affordable bundle the consumer likes best: the utility-maximising bundle in the budget constraint, usually where the indifference curve is tangent to the budget line. Also in [[PS_04-Optimal Choice|PS 04]]. → [[Lec_04-Optimal Bundle|Lec 04]]

### Corner Solution
An optimum at which the consumer buys none of one good, so the indifference curve is not tangent to the budget line. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. Also in [[PP_01-Utility Transformations, Slutsky Effects, Labor Supply & Intertemporal Choice (2025 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_05-Labour Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2020 Moed A)|PP 05]], [[PP_06-Labor Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2020 Moed B)|PP 06]]. → [[Lec_04-Optimal Bundle|Lec 04]]

### Total Utility
The overall satisfaction from consuming a given quantity of a good. → [[Lec_04-Optimal Bundle|Lec 04]]

### Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility
Each extra unit of a good adds less utility than the one before it. → [[Lec_04-Optimal Bundle|Lec 04]]

### Utility-Maximising Rule
With a fixed income, spend until the marginal utility per dollar is equal across goods. → [[Lec_04-Optimal Bundle|Lec 04]]

### Diamond-Water Paradox
Water is vital but cheap and diamonds are not, because price reflects marginal utility, not total utility. → [[Lec_04-Optimal Bundle|Lec 04]]

### Preference Map
The family of indifference curves describing one consumer; higher curves mean higher utility. → [[Lec_04-Optimal Bundle|Lec 04]]

## Demand

### Demand Function
The quantity a consumer demands as a function of prices and income. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. → [[Lec_05-Demand|Lec 05]]

### Demand Curve
The relationship between a good's own price and the quantity demanded, other things held fixed. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. Also in [[Lec_04-Optimal Bundle|Lec 04]]. → [[Lec_05-Demand|Lec 05]]

### Law of Demand
When the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls, other things equal; the ordinary demand curve slopes down. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. Also in [[Lec_07-Substitution and Income Effects|Lec 07]]. → [[Lec_05-Demand|Lec 05]]

### Normal Good
A good whose demand rises when income rises. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. Also in [[Lec_07-Substitution and Income Effects|Lec 07]]. → [[Lec_05-Demand|Lec 05]]

### Inferior Good
A good whose demand falls when income rises. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. Also in [[Lec_05-Demand|Lec 05]], [[PP_01-Utility Transformations, Slutsky Effects, Labor Supply & Intertemporal Choice (2025 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_07-Substitution and Income Effects|Lec 07]]

### Substitutes and Complements
Goods are substitutes if a price rise in one raises demand for the other, and complements if it lowers it; not the same as perfect substitutes or complements in preferences. → [[Lec_05-Demand|Lec 05]]

### Market Demand
The sum of all consumers' individual demands at each price. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. → [[Lec_05-Demand|Lec 05]]

## Revealed preference

### Revealed Preference
Inferring preferences from choices: a bundle chosen when another was affordable is revealed preferred to it. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. Also in [[PS_05-Revealed Preference, Compensation & Income and Substitution Effects|PS 05]]. → [[Lec_06-Revealed Preference|Lec 06]]

### Directly Revealed Preferred
Bundle A is directly revealed preferred to B if B was affordable when A was chosen. → [[Lec_06-Revealed Preference|Lec 06]]

### Indirectly Revealed Preferred
Bundle A is indirectly revealed preferred to C if a chain of direct revelations links A to C, using transitivity. → [[Lec_06-Revealed Preference|Lec 06]]

### Weak Axiom of Revealed Preference
If A is directly revealed preferred to B, then B is never directly revealed preferred to A; a choice pattern that breaks it is inconsistent (WARP). → [[Lec_06-Revealed Preference|Lec 06]]

## Income and substitution effects

### Slutsky Compensation
The income adjustment after a price change that leaves the consumer just able to afford their original bundle at the new prices. Also in [[PS_05-Revealed Preference, Compensation & Income and Substitution Effects|PS 05]]. → [[Lec_07-Substitution and Income Effects|Lec 07]]

### Hicks Compensation
The income adjustment after a price change that returns the consumer to their original utility level at the new prices. Also in [[Lec_07-Substitution and Income Effects|Lec 07]]. → [[PS_05-Revealed Preference, Compensation & Income and Substitution Effects|PS 05]]

### Substitution Effect
The change in the quantity demanded that comes from the change in relative prices alone, with purchasing power held fixed by compensation. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. Also in [[PS_05-Revealed Preference, Compensation & Income and Substitution Effects|PS 05]]. → [[Lec_07-Substitution and Income Effects|Lec 07]]

### Income Effect
The change in the quantity demanded that comes from the change in purchasing power when a price changes. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. Also in [[PS_05-Revealed Preference, Compensation & Income and Substitution Effects|PS 05]], [[Lec_08-Labor Supply|Lec 08]]. → [[Lec_07-Substitution and Income Effects|Lec 07]]

### Giffen Good
An inferior good whose income effect outweighs its substitution effect, so a price rise increases the quantity demanded. Seen in: [[Principles of Micro]]. Also in [[PP_01-Utility Transformations, Slutsky Effects, Labor Supply & Intertemporal Choice (2025 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_07-Substitution and Income Effects|Lec 07]]

## Endowments and labor supply

### Endowment
The bundle a consumer owns before trading; with an endowment, income is the value of what is owned at current prices. Also in [[PP_01-Utility Transformations, Slutsky Effects, Labor Supply & Intertemporal Choice (2025 Moed A)|PP 01]]. → [[Lec_08-Labor Supply|Lec 08]]

### Net Demand
Gross demand minus the endowment: positive for a net buyer, negative for a net seller. → [[Lec_08-Labor Supply|Lec 08]]

### Price Offer Curve
The path of the optimal bundle as one price varies, with endowment or income held fixed. → [[Lec_08-Labor Supply|Lec 08]]

### Endowment Income Effect
The change in demand that comes from the change in the value of the endowment when a price changes. Also in [[PP_03-Shuki Shakes and Salads, 20 MCQs (2017 Moed A)|PP 03]]. → [[Lec_08-Labor Supply|Lec 08]]

### Labor Supply
The number of hours a person chooses to work at a given wage, decided together with how much leisure to take. Seen in: [[Macro-Economics (Intro)]]. Also in [[PS_07-Labor Supply|PS 07]]. → [[Lec_08-Labor Supply|Lec 08]]

### Leisure
Time not spent working, treated as a good the consumer chooses alongside consumption; each hour costs the wage that could have been earned. Also in [[PS_07-Labor Supply|PS 07]]. → [[Lec_08-Labor Supply|Lec 08]]

### Non-Labor Income
Income that does not depend on hours worked, such as a transfer or a return on savings; it shifts the budget line without changing its slope. Also in [[PP_05-Labour Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2020 Moed A)|PP 05]], [[PP_06-Labor Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2020 Moed B)|PP 06]]. → [[Lec_08-Labor Supply|Lec 08]]

### Real Wage Rate
The wage divided by the price of consumption: how many units of consumption one more hour of work buys. → [[Lec_08-Labor Supply|Lec 08]]

### Reservation Wage
The lowest wage at which a person chooses to work at all; below it the optimum is zero hours. → [[PP_10-Labour Supply & Intertemporal Choice, Open Questions (2022 Moed A)|PP 10]]

## Intertemporal choice

### Intertemporal Choice
How a consumer splits consumption between two periods, saving or borrowing along an intertemporal budget line. Also in [[PS_06-Intertemporal Choice|PS 06]]. → [[Lec_09-Intertemporal Choice|Lec 09]]

### Intertemporal Budget Constraint
The two-period budget line: consumption now and later, linked by the interest rate and the income endowment. Also in [[PS_06-Intertemporal Choice|PS 06]]. → [[Lec_09-Intertemporal Choice|Lec 09]]

### Interest Rate
The extra amount paid after one period for each unit saved or borrowed; it sets the slope of the intertemporal budget line. Also in [[PS_06-Intertemporal Choice|PS 06]]. → [[Lec_09-Intertemporal Choice|Lec 09]]

### Real Interest Rate
The interest rate corrected for inflation, so the factor (1 + r) / (1 + inflation) sets the slope of the budget line; higher inflation at a fixed nominal rate lowers it. Seen in: [[Financial Management]], [[Macro-Economics II]]. Also in [[PP_01-Utility Transformations, Slutsky Effects, Labor Supply & Intertemporal Choice (2025 Moed A)|PP 01]], [[PP_05-Labour Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2020 Moed A)|PP 05]], [[PP_06-Labor Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2020 Moed B)|PP 06]], [[PP_08-Labour Supply, Intertemporal Choice & Consumer Theory MCQ (2018 Moed A)|PP 08]], [[PP_10-Labour Supply & Intertemporal Choice, Open Questions (2022 Moed A)|PP 10]]. → [[Lec_09-Intertemporal Choice|Lec 09]]

### Present Lover
A consumer who prefers consumption early, so the indifference curves are steeper toward the present. Also in [[PP_04-Three Consumers, Welfare Plan & Choice Mix (2018 Sample Exam)|PP 04]]. → [[PP_10-Labour Supply & Intertemporal Choice, Open Questions (2022 Moed A)|PP 10]]

## Uncertainty and risk

### Fair Lottery
A gamble whose expected payoff equals its price, so it is not worth taking for a risk-neutral person. Also in [[PS_08-Uncertainty and Risk|PS 08]]. → [[PP_01-Utility Transformations, Slutsky Effects, Labor Supply & Intertemporal Choice (2025 Moed A)|PP 01]]

### Risk Aversion
Preferring a sure amount to a fair lottery with the same expected value; reflected in a concave utility of wealth. Seen in: [[Microeconomics]]. Also in [[PS_08-Uncertainty and Risk|PS 08]]. → [[PP_01-Utility Transformations, Slutsky Effects, Labor Supply & Intertemporal Choice (2025 Moed A)|PP 01]]
