# Micro I Concepts

> One-stop glossary for Micro-Economics I (Year 1, Semester 2). Each entry: a one-line definition + the problem set where the concept first appears. The course has no lecture notes, so these are standard textbook definitions, not taken from the course. Use the outline panel or Cmd/Ctrl-F to jump.

## Budget constraints

### Budget Constraint
The set of bundles a consumer can afford: total spending at the given prices cannot exceed income. → [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]]

### Budget Line
The boundary of the budget constraint: the bundles that cost exactly the consumer's whole income. → [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]]

## Preferences & indifference curves

### Preferences
A consumer's ranking of bundles of goods: for any two bundles, which one they prefer, or whether they are indifferent. Also in [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]]. → [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]]

### Indifference Curve
The set of bundles the consumer ranks equally, so they are indifferent between any two points on it. Also in [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]]. → [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]]

### Perfect Complements
Two goods the consumer wants in a fixed proportion, such as left and right shoes. Also in [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]]. → [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]]

### Perfect Substitutes
Two goods the consumer is willing to swap at a constant rate, so one can stand in for the other. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]] (as a production function there). Also in [[PS_04-Optimal Choice|PS 04]]. → [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]]

### Goods and Bads
A good raises the consumer's satisfaction when they have more of it; a bad lowers it. Which one a commodity is depends on the consumer's own preferences. Also in [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]]. → [[PS_01-Budget Constraints & Preferences|PS 01]]

### Marginal Rate of Substitution
The rate at which a consumer is willing to give up one good for an extra unit of another and stay equally well off; the absolute value of the slope of the indifference curve. Also in [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]]. → [[PS_02-Preferences & Indifference Curves|PS 02]]

## Utility

### Utility Function
A function that assigns a number to each bundle so that preferred bundles get higher numbers. → [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]]

### Ordinal Utility
Utility used only as a ranking device: the numbers say which bundle is preferred, not by how much. → [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]]

### Quasilinear Utility
$U=v(x)+y$: utility that is linear in one good and any function of the other. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]]. → [[PS_03-The Utility Function|PS 03]]

## Optimal choice

### Optimal Choice
The affordable bundle the consumer likes best: the best bundle in their budget constraint. → [[PS_04-Optimal Choice|PS 04]]

### Utility Maximisation
The consumer's problem of choosing, among the bundles they can afford, the one with the highest utility. → [[PS_04-Optimal Choice|PS 04]]

## Revealed preference, compensation & income and substitution effects

### Revealed Preference
Inferring preferences from choices: a bundle chosen when another was affordable is revealed preferred to it. Seen in: [[Micro-Economics II]] (as a proof technique there). → [[PS_05-Revealed Preference, Compensation & Income and Substitution Effects|PS 05]]

### Slutsky Compensation
The income adjustment after a price change that leaves the consumer just able to afford their original bundle at the new prices. → [[PS_05-Revealed Preference, Compensation & Income and Substitution Effects|PS 05]]

### Hicks Compensation
The income adjustment after a price change that returns the consumer to their original utility level at the new prices. → [[PS_05-Revealed Preference, Compensation & Income and Substitution Effects|PS 05]]

### Income Effect
The change in the quantity demanded that comes from the change in the consumer's purchasing power when a price changes. → [[PS_05-Revealed Preference, Compensation & Income and Substitution Effects|PS 05]]

### Substitution Effect
The change in the quantity demanded that comes from the change in relative prices alone, with purchasing power held fixed by compensation. → [[PS_05-Revealed Preference, Compensation & Income and Substitution Effects|PS 05]]

## Labor supply

### Labor Supply
The number of hours a person chooses to work at a given wage, decided together with how much leisure to take. → [[PS_07-Labor Supply|PS 07]]

### Leisure
Time not spent working, treated as a good the consumer chooses alongside consumption; each hour of it costs the wage that could have been earned. → [[PS_07-Labor Supply|PS 07]]
