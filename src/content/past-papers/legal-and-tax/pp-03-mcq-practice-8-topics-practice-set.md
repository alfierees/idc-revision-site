## 1. The Israeli legal system

### Q1
What is the binding core reasoning of a court judgment called?

- A. Ratio decidendi
- B. Obiter dictum
- C. Lex specialis
- D. Erga omnes

> [!success] Answer: **A. Ratio decidendi**
> The *ratio decidendi* is the reasoning essential to the outcome; it is the binding precedent that the Supreme Court creates ([[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]], "How Law Is Made").
> - **B** is the non-binding remark made in passing.
> - **C** is the principle that specific law beats general law.
> - **D** means valid against the whole world (a property-right concept).

### Q2
Israel's legal system is best described as a:

- A. Pure Common Law system
- B. Mixed Jurisdiction drawing from both Common and Continental law
- C. Pure Continental Law system
- D. Customary Law system

> [!success] Answer: **B. Mixed Jurisdiction**
> Common Law came in through s.46 of the King's Order in Council (adversarial procedure, evidence, agency); Continental law supplied good faith and codification. Scotland and Louisiana are the other mixed jurisdictions ([[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]], "A mixed legal system").
> - **A** and **C** each ignore one of the two sources.
> - **D** is not how the lecture classifies Israel.

### Q3
The principle that specific law overrides general law is known as:

- A. Erga omnes
- B. Pacta sunt servanda
- C. Lex specialis
- D. Ratio decidendi

> [!success] Answer: **C. Lex specialis**
> Named in [[Lec_04-Contract Law]] (special contract laws such as Consumer Protection, Sale and Standard Contracts override the general Contracts Law) and listed in [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]] among the operating principles of the rule of law ("specific law vs. general law").
> - **A** is "against everyone".
> - **B** is "agreements must be kept".
> - **D** is the binding core of a judgment.

### Q4
Which of the following is correctly described as "hard law"?

- A. Voluntary industry codes of conduct
- B. Contractual agreements between private parties
- C. Best-practice guidelines published by an industry body
- D. Legally binding statutes, regulations, and court rulings

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> Hard law is what we are *obliged* to follow: statutes, regulations, court rulings, with the state as the source of force. Soft law is what we *choose* to regulate through contracts, founders' agreements and NDAs ([[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]], "Soft Law vs Hard Law"). Hard law always prevails: a contract term that conflicts with a statute is void to that extent.
> - **A** and **C** are voluntary guidance, not binding.
> - **B** is the lecture's own example of soft law.

### Q5
"Obiter dictum" in a court judgment refers to:

- A. The binding core reasoning of the decision
- B. Non-binding remarks made in passing
- C. The final court order
- D. A written dissenting opinion

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> Obiter dictum is what a judge says "in passing", not necessary to the result; it is persuasive only ([[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]], precedent table).
> - **A** is the ratio.
> - **C** (the order) and **D** (a dissent) are different parts of a judgment, not what the term means.

### Q6
What is the CORRECT hierarchy of norms in Israel from highest to lowest authority?

- A. Basic Laws > Knesset Statutes > Secondary Legislation
- B. Knesset Statutes > Basic Laws > Regulations
- C. Regulations > Basic Laws > Knesset Statutes
- D. Knesset Statutes > Regulations > Basic Laws

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> The lecture hierarchy is Constitution (Basic Laws), then Laws (Knesset), then Regulations (executive), then Orders, then private law. Higher norms authorise and constrain lower ones; a regulation needs a parent statute ([[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]], "Hierarchy of norms").
> - **B**, **C** and **D** each put a lower tier above a higher one.

### Q7
The foundational Latin principle meaning "agreements must be kept" is:

- A. Erga omnes
- B. Ratio decidendi
- C. Lex specialis
- D. Pacta sunt servanda

> [!success] Answer: **D. Pacta sunt servanda**
> The Roman-law bedrock of contract ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], "Sources of contract law").
> - **A**, **B**, **C** mean "against everyone", "binding core of a judgment" and "specific over general".

### Q8
In criminal law proceedings, who brings the case against the accused?

- A. A private individual
- B. An administrative authority
- C. The state / prosecution
- D. A corporate body

> [!success] Answer: **C. The state / prosecution**
> Criminal law is public law: the state prosecutes. This contrasts with private disputes (contract, tort), which the parties bring themselves.
> - **A**, **B**, **D** are not the prosecuting party in a criminal case.

> [!warning] Outside the lecture notes
> The lectures never state who brings a criminal case (they only mention criminal liability of companies under *Modiin* in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]]). The key is plainly right on general legal knowledge; kept.

### Q9
Which BEST describes the substantive sense of the "rule of law"?

- A. Protection of rights, equality, and justice
- B. Equal publication and clarity of laws
- C. Non-retroactive application of legislation
- D. Strict enforcement of all legislation regardless of content

> [!success] Answer (key): **A. Protection of rights, equality, and justice**
> In the standard theory, the *substantive* rule of law is about the content of the law (rights, equality, justice), while the formal sense is about form (published, clear, non-retroactive, enforced).
> - **D** is a purely formal, content-blind view.
> - **B** and **C** are the formal requirements in the standard theory.

> [!warning] The lecture labels this the other way round
> [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]] ("The Rule of Law") puts enforcement and equality under **Formal** and publication, clarity and non-retroactivity under **Substantive**. Read literally, that would make **B** or **C** the lecture's "substantive" answer. The key (A) matches the standard textbook meaning, so **keep A**, but if your exam follows the slide table, expect a different mapping. Check with the lecturer's slides.

### Q10
"Erga omnes" in a legal context means the right is:

- A. Valid only between contracting parties
- B. Valid against the whole world
- C. Binding only on lower courts
- D. Applicable retroactively to past events

> [!success] Answer: **B. Valid against the whole world**
> A property right (ownership, a registered patent) is *erga omnes* / *in rem*; a contractual right binds only the parties (*in personam*) ([[Lec_05-Intellectual Property]], "Types of property rights").
> - **A** describes a contractual right.
> - **C** and **D** are unrelated (binding precedent; non-retroactivity).

## 2. Startup lifecycle and key concepts

### Q11
Which corporate structure is most commonly used for startups in Israel?

- A. Sole Proprietorship
- B. General Partnership
- C. Private Limited Company (LLC)
- D. Statutory Corporation

> [!success] Answer: **C. Private limited company**
> The only common structure that shields founders (limited liability), lets you slice ownership into shares for investors and employees, and supports option plans ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "The three entity types").
> - **A** and **B** expose the owners personally and cannot sell equity to investors.
> - **D** is a body created by statute, not a vehicle for founders.

### Q12
At which fundraising stage does a startup typically focus on building an MVP and market validation?

- A. Pre-Seed
- B. Series A
- C. Bootstrapping
- D. Seed

> [!success] Answer: **D. Seed**
> The financing ladder: Seed funds the **MVP and market/concept validation** ([[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]], "Funding Stages").
> - **A** Pre-Seed is early development of the idea and the start of the product.
> - **B** Round A is for expanding the team and acquiring customers.
> - **C** Bootstrapping is founders funding it themselves.

### Q13
Which is a key risk of not signing a founders' agreement?

- A. Uncertainty in roles, equity distribution, and decision-making
- B. Increased corporate tax rates
- C. Loss of IP registration rights
- D. Automatic dissolution of the company

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> A founders' agreement fixes roles, share split, vesting, leaver terms and dispute resolution. Without it, a verbal-only deal becomes unenforceable "when the relationship sours" (the Scope 360 story) ([[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]], common mistakes; [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Founders' Agreement").
> - **B** tax rates do not depend on it.
> - **C** IP is not lost by registration; the real risk is unclear ownership, not lost registration rights.
> - **D** nothing dissolves automatically.

### Q14
What is the PRIMARY legal advantage of incorporating as a Private Limited Company?

- A. Avoidance of all tax obligations
- B. Separate legal personality and limited liability
- C. Automatic intellectual property protection
- D. No requirement to register with government authorities

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> s.4 Companies Law: separate legal personality; a shareholder's exposure is capped at what they invested ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], Parts 2 and 6; [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Corporation").
> - **A** a company pays corporate tax (and shareholders are taxed on dividends: double taxation).
> - **C** IP must be assigned into the company; it is not automatic.
> - **D** the company must register and receives a Certificate of Incorporation.

### Q15
Which startup lifecycle stage involves negotiating a term sheet and amending Articles for investor rights?

- A. Pre-Incorporation
- B. Incorporation
- C. Investment Rounds
- D. Exit

> [!success] Answer: **C. Investment Rounds**
> That stage covers the term sheet, investment agreement, amending the Articles for liquidation preference and anti-dilution, and clean documents for due diligence ([[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]], "The four stages").
> - **A** is the founders' agreement and NDAs.
> - **B** is registering, drafting the first Articles, moving IP in.
> - **D** is M&A or IPO, warranties and indemnities.

### Q16
A "cap table" refers to:

- A. A government-imposed tax threshold for companies
- B. The maximum permitted number of board members
- C. A limit on returns permitted to angel investors
- D. A table showing equity ownership percentages of all shareholders

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> A capitalization table tracks share capital: who holds what, as a percentage of the total. It is a living document that investors price, including the option pool ([[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]], related notes; the cap table deck itself is a later note in this course).
> - **A**, **B**, **C** invent caps that do not exist; "cap" is short for capitalization.

### Q17
What distinguishes an angel investor from a VC fund?

- A. Angels invest after IPO; VCs invest at seed stage
- B. Angels invest personal wealth at early stages; VCs invest institutional money at later stages
- C. Angels are government-backed; VCs are privately funded
- D. Angels take majority control; VCs take minority positions

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> Angels: very early, personal wealth, low involvement. VCs: later, institutional funds (pension funds, banks), high involvement ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Fundraising and the investor ladder").
> - **A** reverses the order (and IPO investors are the public).
> - **C** angels invest their own money.
> - **D** neither is defined by majority or minority control.

### Q18
Anti-dilution provisions primarily protect:

- A. Existing shareholders when new shares are issued at a lower price
- B. New investors from existing shareholders
- C. Employees participating in ESOP from vesting losses
- D. Creditors in the event of insolvency proceedings

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> When the company issues shares in a "down round" below what the earlier investor paid, anti-dilution lowers the conversion price of the earlier shares and restores some of the lost ownership ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Anti-Dilution Provisions").
> - **B** it is the earlier holder, not the newcomer, who is protected.
> - **C** vesting is a separate mechanism.
> - **D** creditors are protected by other doctrines (subordination, veil piercing).

### Q19
In a General Partnership, each partner's legal relationship to the partnership is best described as:

- A. A silent investor with no active role
- B. A secured creditor of the firm
- C. An independent contractor
- D. An agent of the partnership

> [!success] Answer: **D. An agent**
> Mutual agency: every partner acts as agent of the partnership and the other partners and can bind them in ordinary-course actions, with personal joint and several liability ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], "Partnership").
> - **A** a general partner is active and liable.
> - **B** a partner is an owner, not a creditor.
> - **C** a partner is a principal, not an outsider.

### Q20
"Due diligence" in a fundraising context is best described as:

- A. The process of registering IP with a government authority
- B. A mandatory audit required by law prior to an IPO
- C. An investor's systematic examination of a company's finances, contracts, IP, and clients before investing
- D. The negotiation of individual clauses in a term sheet

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> Investors examine finances, client base, legal agreements and IP *before* investing to verify the founders' statements ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Due Diligence"). Skipped agreements and IP left in a founder's name surface here.
> - **A** is IP registration.
> - **B** is not a legal audit requirement.
> - **D** is term-sheet negotiation.

## 3. Key business terms and agreements

### Q21
What is the PRIMARY purpose of a founders' agreement?

- A. To define share ownership, roles, vesting, and prevent future disputes between founders
- B. To replace the company's Articles of Association
- C. To register the company with the relevant government authority
- D. To set the applicable tax rates for each founder

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> It aligns founders on vision and roles, fixes profit and share distribution, protects the company if a founder leaves, and sets decision and dispute mechanisms ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Founders' Agreement").
> - **B** the Articles are a separate, mandatory document (s.15 Companies Law).
> - **C** registration is filed with the Registrar of Companies.
> - **D** tax rates are set by law.

### Q22
A Non-Compete Agreement in Israel is generally:

- A. Always fully enforceable regardless of circumstances
- B. Required by law for all startup employees
- C. Unenforceable EXCEPT to protect genuine trade secrets or with special compensation
- D. Only valid for members of the board of directors

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> Under *AES v. Saar* an employee's promise not to compete is generally unenforceable, except in narrow cases such as protecting genuine trade secrets or special compensation. The practical protection is NDAs, IP assignment and good incentives ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Non-Compete Agreement").
> - **A** is the opposite of the rule.
> - **B** no law requires them.
> - **D** the lecture draws no such board-only limit.

### Q23
Which cloud model provides virtual servers and storage where the customer controls the operating system?

- A. SaaS (Software as a Service)
- B. PaaS (Platform as a Service)
- C. DaaS (Desktop as a Service)
- D. IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service)

> [!success] Answer: **D. IaaS**
> With IaaS you manage the OS, apps and data; the provider manages servers, storage and networking (renting empty rooms; AWS, Google Cloud) ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "IaaS vs PaaS vs SaaS").
> - **B** PaaS: the provider also runs the OS; you manage only your application and data.
> - **A** SaaS: you manage nothing.
> - **C** DaaS is not one of the three models taught.

### Q24
A "cliff" in an ESOP vesting schedule refers to:

- A. The maximum number of options that can be granted to an employee
- B. The minimum period that must pass before any vesting begins
- C. The exercise price at which options can be purchased
- D. The tax rate applied to gains from share sales

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> The standard schedule is 4 years with a 1-year cliff: nothing vests before the cliff (leave earlier and you get nothing), then 25% vests and the rest vests monthly ([[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]], "The Life Cycle of an Option"). Same as Q78.
> - **A** is plan or pool size.
> - **C** is the exercise (strike) price.
> - **D** is the tax rate (25% or marginal).

### Q25
What is the key risk of signing an NDA without carefully reviewing the final text?

- A. The signed version is legally binding and may permit broader information use than originally intended
- B. The NDA becomes automatically void
- C. Regulatory penalties are automatically triggered upon signing
- D. Intellectual property ownership automatically transfers to the receiving party

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> An NDA obliges the parties to keep information confidential ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "NDA"). What you sign is what binds you; if the text carves out uses or exceptions you did not intend, the agreed text governs, not your recollection of the talks (the lecture's general point: skipped or sloppy paper surfaces later).
> - **B** nothing makes a signed NDA void.
> - **C** no penalties trigger on signing.
> - **D** an NDA does not transfer IP; that needs an assignment.

> [!warning] Reasoned, not quoted
> The lecture defines an NDA but does not discuss the review risk. The answer follows from basic contract logic (a signed contract binds). Kept.

### Q26
"Post-money valuation" equals:

- A. Company value minus total liabilities
- B. The share price multiplied by total shares outstanding
- C. Pre-money valuation plus the new investment amount
- D. Annual revenue multiplied by the industry multiple

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> Post-money = Pre-money + Investment, and the investor's ownership = Investment / Post-money. Example: \$1M into a \$10M company gives 1 / 11, about 9% ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Valuation").
> - **A** is a net-asset (book) figure.
> - **B** is also arithmetically a valuation, but of the *whole company after a round at that price*, not the lecture's definition of post-money.
> - **D** is the revenue-multiple method, a way to estimate value.

### Q27
A Service Level Agreement (SLA) primarily defines:

- A. Terms for employee share ownership and vesting schedules
- B. Tax arrangements between the company and government
- C. Intellectual property licensing terms and royalties
- D. Service levels, uptime guarantees, response times, and remedies for non-performance

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> The SLA is the quantitative layer of a service agreement: measurable response times, availability, maximum downtime (example: 99.9% uptime, restore within four hours), with compensation if breached ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Service Agreement & SLA").
> - **A** is an ESOP.
> - **B** is tax law.
> - **C** is an IP licence.

### Q28
Which valuation method calculates value based on the cost to replicate what the company has built?

- A. Multiple-Based Valuation
- B. Cost / Entry Barrier Valuation
- C. Revenue-Based Valuation
- D. User-Based Valuation

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> Entry-barrier (replacement cost): what it would cost to replicate the development and technology; development cost of \$2M gives a value of about \$2M ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Methods for calculating valuation").
> - **A** applies an industry multiple to profit or revenue.
> - **C** is annual revenue times a multiple.
> - **D** is users times value per user.

### Q29
An EULA (End User Licence Agreement) is best described as:

- A. A contract between an employer and employee governing terms of employment
- B. An agreement between investors and founders on governance rights
- C. End-user terms governing the use of a software product or digital service
- D. A government licensing requirement for technology companies

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> The EULA defines the terms of use of purchased software or digital products and limits the company's liability for misuse ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], the "website/product trio").
> - **A** is an employment contract.
> - **B** is the investment agreement.
> - **D** it is a private contract, not a state licence.

### Q30
What is the PRIMARY purpose of vesting in employee equity arrangements?

- A. To grant immediate ownership of all shares upon hiring
- B. To prevent dilution of existing shareholders
- C. To reduce the company's corporate tax liability
- D. To ensure long-term commitment and retention of employees

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> Rights are earned gradually, so the employee only fully benefits by staying ("golden handcuffs"); it also avoids "dead equity" from someone who leaves after two months ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Vesting & ESOP"; [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]], "Why Equity Awards?").
> - **A** is the opposite of vesting.
> - **B** equity awards in fact *cause* dilution (the cost of the option pool).
> - **C** tax is not the purpose (the Section 102 track choice is separate).

## 4. Contract law

### Q31
Which condition for a valid contract is NOT required under Israeli law (unlike common law)?

- A. Offer and Acceptance
- B. Consideration (exchange of value)
- C. Serious Intention (Gmirat Da'at)
- D. Legal Capacity of the parties

> [!success] Answer: **B. Consideration**
> Israeli law dropped the common-law consideration requirement: a gratuitous promise can bind (Gift Law, *Barashi*). Required: offer and acceptance, intention to create legal relations, capacity, legality of purpose, definiteness ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], "Conditions for a valid contract").
> - **A**, **C**, **D** are all required.

### Q32
"Gmirat Da'at" (serious intention) in Israeli contract law is tested:

- A. Objectively, assessed at the moment of signing
- B. Subjectively, based solely on what each party privately intended
- C. By examining post-signing conduct only
- D. By a government authority before the contract is enforced

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> The test is objective (*Barashi*), examined "exactly at the moment of signing" (*Rabinai*) ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], "Serious intention"). It bends only for signers with impaired capacity (*Barashi*), and a proven identical subjective intention would govern in the rare case.
> - **B** subjective intent alone is not the test.
> - **C** conduct after signing is not the focus.
> - **D** courts decide it, not an authority.

### Q33
The principle of contra proferentem (Section 25(b1)) means that:

- A. Courts prefer interpretations that uphold contract validity
- B. Missing terms are filled by customary trade usage
- C. Ambiguous terms are interpreted against the party who drafted them
- D. Contracts must always be interpreted in their commercial context

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> Post-2011 s.25(b1): where one side had the drafting advantage (a standard form), prefer the reading against that party ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], "The 2011 amendment of Section 25").
> - **A** is s.25(b), the validity-preserving rule.
> - **B** is custom (s.25(c) and s.26).
> - **D** loosely echoes the *Apropim* circumstances approach, not contra proferentem.

### Q34
Section 39 of the Contracts Law imposes an obligation of good faith on:

- A. Contract performance only
- B. Pre-contractual negotiations only
- C. Post-contractual termination and remedies only
- D. Both negotiation AND performance of the contract

> [!success] Answer (key): **D**
> Good faith runs through the contract's life: s.12 for negotiation and s.39 for performance, so the principle covers both stages ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], "Section 12" and "completion tools").
> - **B** and **C** cover only one stage and leave out performance.
> - **A** is the literal reading of s.39 (see the warning).

> [!warning] Arguably imprecise: negotiation sits in s.12, not s.39
> The lecture notes place **negotiation** good faith in **Section 12** ("In negotiating a contract, a person shall act in an accepted manner and in good faith") and list s.39 as a good-faith *completion tool*. On the statute's wording, **A** (performance) is the strictest answer for s.39 itself. The key says D, so **keep D**: it is the only option that covers the whole span of good faith, and the question is probably using "s.39" loosely for the good-faith duty.

### Q35
Under the Market Overt Rule (Section 34, Sale Law), which is NOT one of the required conditions?

- A. Good faith of the purchaser (subjective test)
- B. Written documentation of the transaction
- C. Payment of value by the purchaser
- D. Purchase from a seller in the ordinary course of their business

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> The four conditions: good faith (subjective), for value, good faith at the time of transfer, and a purchase from someone who appeared to be the seller in the ordinary course of that seller's business (*Knaan*, the flea-market Reuven Rubin). No written record is required ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], "The market-overt rule").
> - **A**, **C**, **D** are all conditions.

### Q36
Which contract type involves a future-facing transaction that includes an element of risk?

- A. Real Transaction
- B. Gratuitous Contract
- C. Obligatory Transaction
- D. Adhesion Contract

> [!success] Answer: **C. Obligatory transaction**
> An obligatory transaction "faces the future": performance and compensation come later, so there is risk (a contractor selling an apartment "on paper"). Most venture deals are obligatory ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], "Obligatory vs. real transactions").
> - **A** a real transaction exchanges on the spot, with no risk.
> - **B** and **D** are about no payment and about drafting, not timing and risk.

### Q37
Under Section 8 of the Land Law, a real estate transaction:

- A. Must be in writing to be legally valid
- B. Can be made orally if both parties expressly consent
- C. Requires notarial authentication but need not be in writing
- D. Is valid if witnessed by two independent witnesses

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> Writing is *constitutive* for land: without a written document the transaction is invalid. The narrow exception is *Kalmer v. Gai* (good faith can relax it where there was reliance and fault) ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], "The writing requirement").
> - **B** consent cannot replace writing.
> - **C** it is writing, not notarisation (notaries are for prenups).
> - **D** witnesses are not the test.

### Q38
"Mesuyamut" (definiteness) for real estate contracts requires, at minimum:

- A. Full names of parties, payment schedule, and complete legal description
- B. All terms including taxes, expenses, and financing arrangements
- C. A comprehensive written contract with no missing terms whatsoever
- D. Delivery date, price, and nature of the transaction

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> *Dor Energy* eroded the traditional long list of essentials into three: **delivery date, price, nature of the transaction**. Courts then use completion mechanisms for gaps ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], "Definiteness (mesuyamut)").
> - **A** and **B** are the old conservative "all terms" approach.
> - **C** a contract need not be perfectly complete if serious intention is proven.

### Q39
Section 30 of the Contracts Law renders a contract void if it is:

- A. Unilaterally drafted by one party without negotiation
- B. Illegal, immoral, or contrary to public policy
- C. Missing the element of consideration
- D. Not formally registered with a government authority

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> s.30: illegal, immoral or against public policy (*Egged v. Mashiach*), the counterweight to freedom of contract in s.24. *Achi Tekuma*: a hidden illegal deal is void too ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], Part 4).
> - **A** a standard form is not void, just construed against the drafter.
> - **C** Israel does not require consideration.
> - **D** no general registration requirement.

### Q40
Under Section 26, "gap filling" of missing contract terms is done first by:

- A. Custom between the parties; absent that, common usage in similar contracts
- B. A court rewriting unfair terms to achieve a fair outcome
- C. A government authority filling in any missing terms
- D. Automatically voiding any contract that has missing terms

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> s.26: undetermined details follow the custom between the parties, and absent that the custom common in contracts of the same type; they are deemed agreed ([[Lec_04-Contract Law]], "Section 26").
> - **B** rewriting for fairness is not s.26.
> - **C** no authority fills gaps.
> - **D** courts strain to save a contract with gaps (*Dor Energy*).

## 5. Company law

### Q41
Which of the following powers cannot be delegated by the Board of Directors?

- A. Negotiating commercial contracts with suppliers
- B. Hiring employees at all levels
- C. Managing day-to-day operational matters
- D. Approving the company's financial statements

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> Non-delegable (s.92): appointing or dismissing the CEO, approving the financial statements, setting general business strategy, dividend decisions and significant capital changes, structural change or IPO. Operational and reversible matters go to the CEO ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], "Powers of the board of directors").
> - **A**, **B**, **C** are ongoing, operational or pre-defined tasks the CEO can carry.

### Q42
Under Section 4 of the Companies Law, a company:

- A. Has the same legal status as its shareholders personally
- B. Has a separate legal personality independent from its shareholders
- C. Bears unlimited liability shared equally with its founders
- D. Has no independent capacity to enter into contracts

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> s.4: a company can hold any right or obligation consistent with its nature; it owns, contracts, sues and is sued. *Salomon* (1897) is the foundation ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], Part 6).
> - **A** and **C** deny the corporate veil.
> - **D** the company can contract (through its organs).

### Q43
Which scenario BEST illustrates an agency problem between majority and minority shareholders?

- A. A majority shareholder directing company assets to a business they personally control
- B. A CEO using company funds for personal international travel
- C. The company taking excessive financial risks that harm creditors
- D. An employee leaving before their options have fully vested

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> Majority versus minority: controllers decide for their own benefit at the minority's expense ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], Part 5, the second of three conflicts).
> - **B** is shareholders versus management (perks).
> - **C** is shareholders versus creditors.
> - **D** is a vesting event, not an agency conflict.

### Q44
What is the legal nature of a bond issued by a company?

- A. A unit of ownership in the company granting voting rights
- B. A derivative instrument whose value is based on the share price
- C. A loan from the investor to the company; the bondholder is a creditor, not an owner
- D. A guaranteed share of the company's annual profits

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> A bond is a loan: repay principal plus interest; the bondholder is a creditor and ranks ahead of shareholders on liquidation ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], "Bonds").
> - **A** describes a share.
> - **B** describes an option-style instrument.
> - **D** a bond pays fixed interest, not a profit share.

### Q45
Under which circumstances may a court "pierce the corporate veil" (Section 6)?

- A. When the company is consistently unprofitable
- B. When shareholders are in dispute over dividend distribution
- C. When the company fails to file annual returns with the Registrar
- D. When the corporate structure is used to perpetrate fraud or oppress creditors

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> s.6 allows piercing where separate personality is abused: use of the company for fraud or to wrong creditors, unreasonable risk about repaying debts, or shareholder awareness or wilful blindness. It is exceptional ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], "Piercing the corporate veil").
> - **A**, **B**, **C** are ordinary business or compliance problems, not abuse.

### Q46
The "subordination doctrine" (thin capitalisation) means:

- A. Shareholder loans may be subordinated to external creditor claims when the company is under-capitalised
- B. Minority shareholders rank below majority shareholders in voting power
- C. A secured creditor ranks below unsecured creditors in the repayment hierarchy
- D. The CEO's authority is legally subordinate to that of the board

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> *Northern Chicks*: where a company is thinly capitalised and badly managed, the owners' repayment can be suspended until external creditors are paid in full. It re-orders the queue without destroying limited liability ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], Part 7).
> - **B** is about voting power, unrelated.
> - **C** reverses the hierarchy (secured creditors come first).
> - **D** is a governance point, not this doctrine.

### Q47
According to Section 57, which is a power of the General Meeting (shareholders)?

- A. Approving the company's annual financial statements
- B. Approving interested-party transactions
- C. Appointing the CEO
- D. Setting the company's long-term business strategy

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> s.57 powers per the lecture: appoint the board, approve interested-party transactions, change share capital, appoint the auditor and internal auditor ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], "Powers of shareholders").
> - **A**, **C**, **D** are all board powers (financial statements, CEO, strategy).

### Q48
What must be satisfied for a company to lawfully distribute dividends?

- A. Board approval alone is sufficient
- B. A 75% shareholder vote is required
- C. Sufficient profits AND distribution must not endanger the company's solvency
- D. Prior approval from the Securities Authority is mandatory

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> s.190: both sufficient profits and no threat to solvency; this protects creditors ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], "Dividends, the solvency guardrail").
> - **A** the board decides, but the statutory tests still apply.
> - **B** no 75% rule in the lecture.
> - **D** no regulator pre-approval.

### Q49
The "Organ Theory" in company law is significant because it:

- A. Allows shareholders to bypass board decisions
- B. Prevents companies from ever being held criminally liable
- C. Defines the mandatory organisational hierarchy required by law
- D. Enables courts to attribute the mental state of a corporate organ to the corporation itself

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> The mental and behavioural elements of an organ (a senior officer) are treated as the corporation's own (*Tzuk Or*); *Modiin* allows criminal liability where justified ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], "Organ theory").
> - **A** and **C** are about governance, not attribution.
> - **B** is the opposite: it makes criminal liability possible.

### Q50
Which item is classified as optional (not mandatory) in the Articles of Association?

- A. The number of directors
- B. The company name
- C. The company's objectives
- D. The registered share capital

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> Mandatory (s.18): name, objectives, registered share capital, limitation of liability. Optional (s.19): number of directors, governance rules, powers and rights of shareholders and directors, share-transfer conditions ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], "The Articles of Association").
> - **B**, **C**, **D** are all mandatory.

## 6. Digital regulation and privacy

### Q51
Under GDPR Article 3, which organisation must comply with GDPR?

- A. Only companies physically located within EU member states
- B. Only public sector organisations operating in EU countries
- C. Any organisation processing personal data of EU residents, regardless of location
- D. Companies with more than 250 employees based in the EU

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> Extraterritorial reach: it applies to organisations established in the EU and to any organisation anywhere that processes EU residents' data. No office or server in Europe is needed ([[Lec_07-Digital Regulation]], §6).
> - **A** and **B** are too narrow (it binds companies and public bodies alike).
> - **D** the lecture gives no size threshold for Art. 3.

### Q52
Which category of data is classified as a "special category" requiring heightened protection?

- A. Email address
- B. Home address
- C. Phone number
- D. Biometric data

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> Sensitive (special category) data: biometric, genetic and health data, and similar, with extra protection ([[Lec_07-Digital Regulation]], §7).
> - **A**, **B**, **C** are basic identifiers (personal data, but not special category).

### Q53
Under GDPR's "Privacy by Design" principle (Article 25), privacy must be:

- A. Added as a feature after products are launched to the market
- B. Built into products and systems from the outset
- C. Implemented only upon a specific user request
- D. Outsourced entirely to a designated DPO after deployment

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> Privacy engineered in from the start, not bolted on after launch; the companion Privacy by Default means protective settings are the out-of-the-box state ([[Lec_07-Digital Regulation]], §5 and §10).
> - **A** is bolting it on later, the thing the principle rejects.
> - **C** it applies by default, not on request.
> - **D** the DPO oversees; design duty stays with the controller.

### Q54
What is the role of a Data Protection Officer (DPO) under GDPR?

- A. To independently monitor compliance with data protection law
- B. To process personal data on behalf of the controller
- C. To determine the purposes and means of data processing
- D. To represent data subjects in legal proceedings against the company

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> The DPO (Arts. 37 to 39) is the appointed privacy officer responsible for oversight and liaison with regulators ([[Lec_07-Digital Regulation]], §10).
> - **B** is the data processor.
> - **C** is the data controller.
> - **D** the DPO does not litigate for data subjects.

### Q55
Standard Contractual Clauses (SCC) under GDPR are primarily used for:

- A. Employment contracts within EU member states
- B. Processing sensitive health data within EU organisations
- C. Transferring personal data to countries outside the EU
- D. Implementing cookie consent mechanisms on websites

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> SCCs (Art. 46) are pre-approved terms binding the importer to EU-level protection when data leaves the EU, one of four transfer mechanisms with adequacy decisions, BCRs and codes of conduct ([[Lec_07-Digital Regulation]], §11).
> - **A**, **B** are internal EU matters.
> - **D** cookies fall under the ePrivacy Directive (§14).

### Q56
Under the EU AI Act, AI systems used for real-time biometric surveillance in public spaces are:

- A. High-risk (subject to strict regulatory requirements)
- B. Limited risk (requiring transparency disclosures only)
- C. Minimal risk (free to use without specific obligations)
- D. Unacceptable risk (prohibited / banned)

> [!success] Answer: **D. Unacceptable risk**
> The AI Act is risk-based: outright prohibited uses, then high-risk systems with strict requirements, down to minimal risk ([[Lec_07-Digital Regulation]], §15). Real-time biometric identification in public spaces sits in the prohibited tier.
> - **A**, **B**, **C** are the lower tiers.

> [!warning] Placement of this example is outside the lecture notes
> The notes state the tiers but do not list which uses are prohibited. Real-time remote biometric surveillance in public is a classic prohibited practice under the Act (with narrow law-enforcement exceptions), so the key is right on general knowledge. Kept.

### Q57
Which of the following is a lawful basis for processing personal data under GDPR Article 6?

- A. Competitive advantage of the processing organisation
- B. Contractual necessity — processing is necessary to perform a contract
- C. Public curiosity or general interest
- D. Commercial profitability of the processing activity

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> The six bases: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public interest, legitimate interest ([[Lec_07-Digital Regulation]], §8.2). If processing is necessary to deliver the service, the contract basis covers it with no separate consent click.
> - **A** and **D** are commercial motives, not a basis (legitimate interest is balanced against the subject's rights).
> - **C** "public curiosity" is not "public interest" (a task carried out in the public interest).

### Q58
A DPIA (Data Protection Impact Assessment) under GDPR Article 35 is required for:

- A. Processing activities likely to result in high risk to individuals' rights
- B. All data processing activities undertaken by any organisation
- C. Only processing conducted outside EU member states
- D. Marketing and advertising activities exclusively

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> A mandatory risk assessment before starting high-risk processing (continuous AI processing, data brokering, health or financial data) ([[Lec_07-Digital Regulation]], §10).
> - **B** too broad.
> - **C** location is not the trigger.
> - **D** marketing is one possible high-risk area, not the only one.

### Q59
The Digital Markets Act (DMA) primarily targets:

- A. Small and medium-sized digital businesses
- B. Public broadcasters and media organisations
- C. Large online platforms designated as "gatekeepers"
- D. Financial institutions offering digital banking services

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> The DMA (second generation, with the DSA, adopted 2022) is digital-competition law aimed at gatekeepers ([[Lec_07-Digital Regulation]], §2 and §3).
> - **A**, **B**, **D** are not its targets. The DSA covers platform transparency.

### Q60
Under GDPR, a data subject's "right to be forgotten" means the right to:

- A. Remain completely anonymous online
- B. Have their personal data erased under certain conditions
- C. Opt out of all direct marketing emails permanently
- D. Have all cookies automatically deleted from their device

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> The right to erasure, one of the eight data subject rights, first established in *Google Spain* ([[Lec_07-Digital Regulation]], §9 and §13).
> - **A** anonymity is not a GDPR right.
> - **C** opting out of marketing is the right to object.
> - **D** cookies fall under the ePrivacy Directive.

> [!warning] "Under certain conditions" is not spelled out in the notes

## 7. Intellectual property

### Q61
Which type of intellectual property is protected automatically upon creation, without registration?

- A. Copyright
- B. Patent
- C. Trademark
- D. Industrial Design

> [!success] Answer: **A. Copyright**
> Copyright arises the moment the work is created, with no registration, fee or examination (a startup's source code is protected from the first commit) ([[Lec_05-Intellectual Property]], §4).
> - **B**, **C**, **D** all require registration with the relevant authority.

### Q62
What are the three conditions required for a patent to be granted?

- A. Novelty, Distinctiveness, and Industrial Application
- B. Uniqueness, Registration, and Commercial Use
- C. Creativity, Originality, and Non-disclosure
- D. Novelty, Non-obviousness (inventive step), and Industrial Applicability

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> Patents Law 1967 screens: **novelty**, **non-triviality / inventive step**, **technological applicability** (the key's "industrial applicability" is the same idea) ([[Lec_05-Intellectual Property]], §5).
> - **A** distinctiveness is a trademark test.
> - **B** and **C** are not the statutory conditions (a patent needs *disclosure*, not non-disclosure).

### Q63
How long does patent protection last from the filing date?

- A. Life of inventor + 70 years
- B. 10 years
- C. 20 years
- D. 50 years

> [!success] Answer: **C. 20 years**
> 20 years from the filing date, subject to maintenance fees; then the technology is free to copy (Viagra generics from 2013) ([[Lec_05-Intellectual Property]], §5).
> - **A** is the copyright-style term.
> - **B** and **D** are not the patent term.

### Q64
A trade secret differs from a patent in that trade secrets:

- A. Require registration with a government authority
- B. Have no time limit but protection is permanently lost if the secret is disclosed
- C. Last for only 20 years from the date of creation
- D. Provide no protection against independent reverse engineering

> [!success] Answer (key): **B**
> A trade secret can last forever, but only while it stays secret; once disclosed the protection evaporates, with no 20-year fallback (the Coca-Cola formula) ([[Lec_05-Intellectual Property]], §6).
> - **A** no state examination or registration.
> - **C** 20 years is the patent term.
> - **D** see the warning.

> [!warning] Option D is also defensible
> The lecture's comparison table says a trade secret gives **no protection against independent discovery** (a rival who independently invents it is free), and its fragility box lists reverse engineering among the ways secrecy is lost. So D is arguably also true. The key is B because "no time limit, lost on disclosure" is the cleanest contrast with a patent's fixed 20-year term. Keep B; if you see D on an exam with B absent, take D.

### Q65
Under copyright law, "moral rights" refer to:

- A. The right of attribution and protection of the integrity of the work
- B. The right to earn royalties from exploitation of the work
- C. The right to prevent others from patenting similar inventions
- D. The right to transfer copyright to another party or employer

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> Moral rights protect the author's personal link to the work: being named as author (attribution) and preventing distortion (integrity).
> - **B** and **D** are economic rights (exploitation, assignment).
> - **C** is not copyright; patents are a separate regime.

> [!warning] Outside the lecture notes
> [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property]] covers copyright's purposes and protected works but never mentions moral rights. A is the standard meaning of the term. Kept.

### Q66
Which of the following is NOT registrable as a trademark?

- A. A distinctive logo unique to the brand
- B. A distinctive combination of colours associated with the brand
- C. A unique product name
- D. A purely descriptive term (e.g. "Fresh Bread" for a bread product)

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> A sign must be unique and not directly descriptive, nor generic or misleading (refused examples: "Orange Juice" for orange juice, "Bread" for a bakery, "Leather Shoes" for shoes) ([[Lec_05-Intellectual Property]], §7).
> - **A** and **C** are plain registrable signs (logos, words).
> - **B** a distinctive combination can qualify (the lecture lists "combinations" of signs); colours are not discussed explicitly.

### Q67
An IP Assignment agreement in a startup is critical because it:

- A. Prevents founders and employees from leaving the company
- B. Automatically registers the startup's IP with international authorities
- C. Ensures all IP created by founders is formally and legally transferred to the company
- D. Grants employees the personal right to use company IP outside of work

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> At incorporation all pre-existing IP must be transferred into the company; IP left in a founder's name is a diligence deal-breaker, and investors will not fund it ([[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System]], "The four stages" and "legal debt").
> - **A** retention is the job of vesting.
> - **B** an assignment does not register anything.
> - **D** the opposite: it concentrates rights in the company.

### Q68
Industrial design protection focuses on:

- A. The functional mechanism or technical process of a product
- B. The visual and aesthetic features of an industrial product
- C. The manufacturing process used to produce the product
- D. The raw materials used in the manufacture of the product

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> A design protects how a product *looks* (appearance), not how it works ([[Lec_05-Intellectual Property]], §8). Patent = function; design = aesthetics; copyright = artistic work.
> - **A** and **C** are patent territory (function, process).
> - **D** materials are not the object of design protection.

### Q69
Under current global approaches, AI-generated works without significant human creative input are generally:

- A. Not automatically eligible for copyright protection
- B. Automatically protected by copyright in the same way as human-authored works
- C. Protected for a fixed term of 70 years from the date of creation
- D. Protected under patent law as technological inventions

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> The pattern everywhere: the more substantive the human creative contribution, the more likely protection attaches. In the US a machine cannot own copyright and there is no automatic recognition; the Beijing court protected an image only because of significant human input; Israel has no ruling yet ([[Lec_05-Intellectual Property]], §9).
> - **B** ignores the human-contribution requirement.
> - **C** no fixed AI-specific term exists.
> - **D** patents are for technological inventions (and *Thaler v. Vidal* limits inventors to natural persons).

### Q70
An open source licence typically:

- A. Allows completely unrestricted commercial use with absolutely no conditions
- B. Grants exclusive ownership of the software to every user
- C. Prohibits any commercial use or monetisation of the software
- D. Provides free use and distribution subject to specific conditions (e.g. attribution, share-alike)

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> Free use and distribution, often monetised through paid add-ons (WordPress with paid plugins); but licence terms still bind you, and copyleft can force disclosure of your own code ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Open Source" and "IP Licensing").
> - **A** ignores the conditions.
> - **B** the licensor keeps ownership.
> - **C** commercial use is generally allowed.

## 8. Tax aspects

### Q71
What is the primary purpose of Section 102 of the Israeli Income Tax Ordinance?

- A. To regulate the company incorporation and registration procedures
- B. To provide special tax arrangements for employee equity compensation
- C. To impose taxation on dividends paid to company shareholders
- D. To regulate venture capital investments and fund structures

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> A statutory safe harbour that taxes employee equity gains as capital gains (25%) instead of ordinary income (up to about 59%) if the company follows the rules; it covers employees and officers only ([[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]], §5).
> - **A** is the Companies Law.
> - **C** and **D** are not what s.102 does.

### Q72
Under the Capital Gain Track of Section 102, what holding period is required for the reduced tax rate?

- A. 12 months
- B. 36 months
- C. 24 months
- D. 48 months

> [!success] Answer: **C. 24 months**
> Do not sell or release the shares for 24 months from the **grant** date (not exercise or vesting) for the 25% rate; before that the gain is taxed at the marginal rate, up to about 59% ([[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]], §7.2).
> - **A**, **B**, **D** are not the statutory period.

### Q73
What is an "option" as used in an ESOP?

- A. Immediate ownership of company shares upon grant
- B. A guaranteed bonus payment based on company performance
- C. A loan from the company to the employee for share purchase
- D. A contractual right to purchase shares in the future at a pre-agreed price

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> A right (not an obligation) to buy a fixed number of shares at a fixed exercise price during a set period; the price is fixed at grant ([[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]], §2).
> - **A** grant gives no shares; they arrive on exercise.
> - **B** it is worth nothing unless the share price rises above the strike.
> - **C** it is not a loan.

### Q74
What is the main tax disadvantage of a corporation compared to a sole proprietorship?

- A. Double taxation — profits taxed at corporate level, then dividends taxed again at shareholder level
- B. Higher initial registration and incorporation fees
- C. Inability to deduct ordinary business expenses
- D. No access to international tax treaties or reliefs

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> A sole proprietor has simple single-layer taxation; a company pays corporate tax and the shareholders then pay dividend or capital-gains tax ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], "Sole Proprietor" and "Company vs. Partnership").
> - **B** is a cost, not a tax disadvantage.
> - **C** the lecture names deductible business expenses as a sole-proprietor *plus*; companies deduct them too.
> - **D** nothing in the lectures supports it.

### Q75
What does "dilution" mean in the context of equity investment?

- A. A reduction in the company's operating profit margin
- B. A decrease in the ownership percentage of existing shareholders when new shares are issued
- C. The mandatory reduction of an investor's stake after a successful exit
- D. A reduction in tax liability achieved through allowable deductions

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> More shares outstanding means a smaller percentage for existing holders. A higher valuation means less dilution for the same cash; the option pool is itself a source of dilution ([[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts]], "Valuation"; [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]], §1).
> - **A** and **D** are about profit and tax, unrelated.
> - **C** there is no mandatory post-exit reduction.

### Q76
Under the Capital Gain Track (Section 102), shares are held during the holding period by:

- A. The employee directly in their own name
- B. The company's board of directors
- C. A trustee appointed for this purpose
- D. The Israeli Tax Authority

> [!success] Answer: **C. A trustee**
> The trustee is the linchpin of the track: it holds the options and shares for the employee through the holding period, files the plan with the ITA and reports quarterly and annually ([[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]], §6 and §9).
> - **A** the employee holding directly is the non-trustee route (taxed at marginal rates).
> - **B** and **D** do not hold the shares.

### Q77
Which BEST describes the key difference between equity investment and a loan?

- A. Equity does not require repayment and involves ownership dilution; loans require repayment and do not dilute
- B. Equity investors receive fixed interest; loan providers receive dividends
- C. Loans confer voting rights; equity gives creditors repayment priority
- D. Equity must be repaid within five years; loans carry no repayment obligation

> [!success] Answer: **A**
> Equity buys ownership (voting, dividends, last in line on failure) and dilutes; a loan or bond is a debt owed with interest, ranking ahead of shareholders, and does not dilute ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], "Bonds": bond versus share).
> - **B** swaps interest and dividends.
> - **C** swaps votes and priority.
> - **D** swaps the repayment duty.

### Q78
A "cliff" in an ESOP means:

- A. The maximum number of shares available under the plan
- B. The applicable tax rate when options are exercised
- C. The market price of shares at the date of option grant
- D. The minimum period that must pass before any options begin to vest

> [!success] Answer: **D**
> Same as Q24: the period before any vesting; usually one year, after which 25% vests ([[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]], §3).
> - **A** is pool size.
> - **B** exercise is not a tax event under the Capital Gain Track anyway.
> - **C** is not the cliff (the exercise price is fixed at grant).

### Q79
The Section 102 Capital Gain Track is particularly beneficial for employees because:

- A. It exempts employees from all taxation on their share gains entirely
- B. The company pays the employee's full tax liability on their behalf
- C. Gains are taxed at the reduced capital gains rate rather than the higher personal income rate
- D. It allows immediate vesting of all options upon joining the company

> [!success] Answer: **C**
> About 25% rather than up to about 59% (the employee keeps \$75,000 rather than \$41,000 on a \$100,000 gain in the lecture's worked example), and tax is deferred to the sale, since exercise is not a taxable event ([[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]], §7).
> - **A** tax is reduced, not removed.
> - **B** the employee pays; the company forgoes its own deduction.
> - **D** vesting is set by the plan, unrelated to the tax track.

### Q80
A startup financed primarily through shareholder loans rather than equity investment risks:

- A. Loss of limited liability protection for all shareholders
- B. Shareholder loans being subordinated to external creditor claims under the thin capitalisation doctrine
- C. Automatic dissolution upon the company becoming insolvent
- D. Criminal liability for the CEO for improper financing

> [!success] Answer: **B**
> Same doctrine as Q46: with thin capitalisation plus improper management, the shareholders' loan repayment can be delayed until external creditors are paid in full (*Northern Chicks*) ([[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures]], Part 7).
> - **A** piercing is exceptional (s.6 abuse); subordination is the gentler, proportionate tool.
> - **C** insolvency does not dissolve a company automatically.
> - **D** no criminal liability follows from the financing mix as such.
