Use this at the end of the first diamond, once your research has narrowed to one problem and before you brainstorm solutions.

1. Name the **customer or user**: who the question is for.
2. State their **need or desire**.
3. State the **problem or challenge** they face.
4. Put these into a template: "How might we [action] for [user] to [outcome]?" or "How could we [action] to help [user] who [problem]?"
5. Check the scope. It should be broad enough to invite many ideas and narrow enough to act on.

## Common pitfalls

- "How might we fix public transport?" is too broad for a team to brainstorm.
- "How might we add a bench at stop 42?" is too narrow, because the solution is already inside the question.

## Worked example

"How might we improve the commuting experience for students who travel to Reichman University by bus, so that it feels more reliable, comfortable, and stress-free?" It names the user and the need, and leaves the solution open. See [[Lec_10-Practical Innovation 2|Lec 10]].
