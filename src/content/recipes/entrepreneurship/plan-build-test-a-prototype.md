Use this in the second diamond, when you have a design question and need to find out whether a solution works.

1. **Plan**: decide what you aim to learn or test.
2. **Plan**: choose the most critical aspects of the idea to prototype.
3. **Build**: use basic materials, mostly digital tools, to create a low-fidelity prototype that demonstrates the key functionality or concept.
4. **Test**: try it with at least two real users or stakeholders and gather insights.
5. **Refine**: use the feedback to fix the prototype, then repeat the loop.

## Common pitfalls

- Building without a learning goal produces feedback you cannot act on.
- Treating the prototype as a draft product leads the team to defend it instead of questioning it.
- A mockup looks like the product but cannot show whether users can navigate it. That needs an interactive prototype.
- Watch what users do, rather than only asking whether they like the idea.

## Worked example

For the bus commute: the learning goal is whether students would use a live "seats available and arrival" screen. The team builds a clickable Figma mockup with no real data, and two commuting students tap through it. Feedback that they care more about when the next bus leaves reshapes the next build.
