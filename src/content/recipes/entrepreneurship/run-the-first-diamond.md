Use this when you have a user or problem area and need to understand it properly before proposing solutions.

1. Answer the two opening questions: who are you designing for, and what is the design problem?
2. Decide what information you need and why.
3. Identify representative interviewees: current customers, potential users, or stakeholders.
4. Develop open-ended questions, then record the interviews with permission or take detailed notes.
5. Fill in an [[Empathy Map]] with what the user says, thinks, feels and does.
6. Build a [[Persona]] from that evidence: name, age and background, goals, frustrations, needs, motivations, and a typical quote from the interview.
7. Draw a [[Customer Journey Map]] of the stages, emotions and touchpoints, and mark **one** moment where improvement would have a big impact.
8. Write the [[Design Question]] before any brainstorming.

## Common pitfalls

- Closed questions produce one-word answers. Open questions produce stories, and stories contain needs.
- Pitching your idea makes the interviewee react to you instead of describing their life.
- A persona built without research only reflects the team's own assumptions.
- Map the user's whole journey, not just your intended product flow.

## Worked example

In class, ChatGPT role-played a student commuting to Reichman University by bus. The team asked at least five open-ended questions, such as "What are the most frustrating parts?", then used the transcript for the three maps. See [[Lec_10-Practical Innovation 2|Lec 10]].
