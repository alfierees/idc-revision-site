Use this when you need to judge how attractive or profitable an industry is before choosing how to compete in it.

1. Rate the **threat of new entrants**: how easily can new companies enter and add capacity?
2. Rate the **bargaining power of suppliers**: can those selling inputs to you drive up costs?
3. Rate the **bargaining power of buyers**: can customers push for lower prices or better quality and service?
4. Rate the **threat of substitutes**: what alternatives meet the same need?
5. Rate **rivalry among existing competitors**: how intense is competition between current players?
6. Read the result. Strong forces drain profit out of the industry, and an attractive industry has mostly weak forces.

## Common pitfalls

- Suppliers sit upstream and sell inputs to you. Buyers sit downstream and buy outputs from you. Mixing them up is the most common Five Forces error.

## Worked example

Fast food: new entrants medium, supplier power low, buyer power high, substitutes high, rivalry very high. Streaming has the same pattern except that supplier power is high, because studios and content creators can demand premium deals. That one force explains why streaming platforms are squeezed from both ends. See [[Lec_03-Innovation Strategy|Lec 03]].
