Use this procedure for any comparative statics question: an outside event hits a market in equilibrium, and you must say what happens to price and quantity. It compares the two equilibria and does not describe the journey between them.

1. Draw the starting equilibrium: demand $D_0$, supply $S_0$, price $P_0$, quantity $Q_0$.
2. Identify the event and ask which curve it affects: supply (the cost or ability of firms to produce) or demand (what buyers want at each price).
3. Shift that curve in the right direction. Shift one curve at a time.
4. Read off the new equilibrium and say what happened to price and quantity. At the old price there is now a [[Shortage]] or a [[Surplus]], and market forces push the price to the new equilibrium.

## Common pitfalls

- Only a change in something other than the good's own price shifts a curve. The price change that follows is a [[Movement Along a Curve]] of the curve that did not shift.
- A supply shift to the left is the same as a shift up: every quantity costs more to produce. A shift to the right is a shift down.

## Worked example

The government bans foreign workers in the cucumber market. Start from the original equilibrium. The ban raises the cost of producing every quantity, so it affects supply, and the supply curve shifts to the left (up).
