    text: |
      If two goods are [[Perfect Complements]]:

      - a. There is a bliss point and the indifference curves surround this point.
      - b. Consumers will only buy the cheaper of the two goods.
      - c. Indifference curves have positive slope.
      - d. None of the above.
    solution: |
      > [!warning] Unofficial answer from a student-annotated copy. Check before relying on it.

      > [!success] Answer (ink): d, None of the above

      The ink reasons as follows:

      - Perfect complements are **goods that are always consumed together in fixed proportions**, e.g. left shoe + right shoe.
      - a is crossed out: **no bliss point**.
      - b is crossed out: consumers **do not substitute based on price**.
      - c is crossed out: the indifference curves are **L-shaped**, not positively sloped.

    text: |
      Professor Weiss gives one midterm exam and a final exam. He weights the final twice as heavily as the midterm to determine the course grade. Let $X$ denote the midterm exam and $Y$ the final exam. A student's indifference curve in Professor Weiss's class that cares only for her grade is:

      - a. A line with a slope of 2.
      - b. A line with a slope of 1.
      - c. A line with a slope of 0.5.
      - d. L-shaped with the kink at $(x, 2y)$.
    solution: |
      > [!warning] Unofficial answer from a student-annotated copy. Check before relying on it.

      > [!success] Answer (ink): c, a line with a slope of 0.5

      The working in the ink:

      $$
      \text{Grade} = x + 2y
      $$

      Hold the grade fixed at $G$ and solve for $x$:

      $$
      x = G - 2y
      $$

      $$
      \frac{dx}{dy} = -2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{1}{2}
      $$

      > [!warning] Check: "slope of 0.5" means a magnitude of 0.5
      > The ink's own result is $dy/dx = -1/2$, so with $Y$ on the vertical axis the curve slopes **downward**. Option c says "0.5", so it only fits if read as the absolute value of the slope. The ink's answer is consistent with that reading, and no other option fits: a and b have the wrong magnitude, d is the perfect-complements shape, not a straight line.

  - id: "5"
    text: |
      If the indifference curves have a negative slope that decreases as $X$ increases, it means that:

      - a. Each additional unit of $X$ becomes more desirable for the consumer.
      - b. Each additional unit of $X$ becomes less desirable for the consumer.
      - c. Each additional unit of $X$ is weighted the same as the previous one for the consumer.
      - d. None of the above.
    solution: |
      > [!warning] Unofficial answer from a student-annotated copy. Check before relying on it.

      > [!success] Answer (ink): b, each additional unit of $X$ becomes less desirable

      The ink's explanation, with the curve it draws (redrawn below, points $A$, $B$, $C$ at $X = 1, 2, 3$, with $\Delta y_1$ the vertical gap from $A$ to $B$ and $\Delta y_2$ from $B$ to $C$):

      - The consumer **needs less and less of $Y$ to compensate** for additional units of $X$.
      - The **[[Marginal Rate of Substitution]] of $X$ for $Y$ is diminishing**.

      ![](/images/micro-i/ps02-q5-diminishing-mrs-curve.png)

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > This is my own reading of the picture, not in the ink: moving right along the curve, each extra unit of $X$ is worth giving up fewer units of $Y$, which is exactly why $\Delta y_2$ is smaller than $\Delta y_1$ on the curve.
