  - id: "1"
    text: |
      ![](/images/maths-for-economists-a/ps11-q1-given-graph.png)

      (a) Intervals where the function is concave up. (b) Concave down. (c) Inflection points. (d) Relative extrema. (e) Critical points.
    solution: |
      > [!note] Working reconstructed to reach the printed answer
      > Read the shape of the curve between the labelled $x$-values. $a$ is the left end of the graph and $h$ the right end.
      >
      > - From $a$ to $c$ the curve is a bowl (a minimum at $b$): concave up. At $c$ it has a sharp peak (a cusp), so the function has a maximum there.
      > - From $c$ to $d$ the curve falls and flattens into a horizontal tangent at $d$: still concave up.
      > - From $d$ to $e$ the curve bends downward, getting steeper: concave down. The tangent at $e$ is steep but finite, with no flat spot.
      > - From $e$ to $g$ the curve curves back up into a bowl with a minimum at $f$: concave up.
      > - At $g$ the tangent is vertical, and from $g$ to $h$ the curve is concave down.
      >
      > Concavity changes at $d$, $e$, $g$: inflection points. Extrema (the curve turns around): $b$ (min), $c$ (max, a cusp), $f$ (min). Critical points are where the tangent is horizontal, vertical, or does not exist: $b$, $c$, $d$ (horizontal tangent), $f$, $g$ (vertical tangent). Point $e$ has a steep but ordinary tangent, so it is not critical.

      > [!success] Answer (printed)
      > (a) Concave up: $a<x<c$, $c<x<d$, $e<x<g$.
      > (b) Concave down: $d<x<e$, $g<x<h$.
      > (c) Inflection: $x=d,\ e,\ g$.
      > (d) Extrema: $x=b,\ c,\ f$.
      > (e) Critical: $x=b,\ c,\ d,\ f,\ g$.

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > Point $d$ is critical **and** an inflection point, but not an extremum: a horizontal tangent where the curve keeps going down. This is the standard trap in "flat but not a turning point".

      > [!note] Working reconstructed to reach the printed answer
      > Critical points: $x=-1$ ($f'=0$), $x=0$ ($f'$ has a corner: slope $-2$ on the left and $\tfrac13$ on the right) and $x=1$ (vertical tangent).
      >

      > Concavity: $f''=-2<0$ for $x<0$. For $0<x<1$ the factor $(x-1)^5<0$, so $f''>0$. For $x>1$, $f''<0$. The concavity changes at $x=0$ and at $x=1$, and $f$ is defined and continuous at both, so both are inflection points.

      > [!success] Answer (printed)
      > $x_{\max}=-1$, $x_{\min}=0$. Inflection: $x=1$, $x=0$.
