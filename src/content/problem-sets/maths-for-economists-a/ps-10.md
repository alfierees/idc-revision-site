      **(c)** piecewise

      $$
      f(x)=\begin{cases}x^2+2,& x<1\\ 4-x^2,& x\ge1\end{cases}
      $$
    solution: |
      > [!note] Working reconstructed to reach the printed answer
      > At the joint: $1^2+2=3$ and $4-1^2=3$, so $f$ is continuous at $x=1$.
      >
      > $f'(x)=2x$ for $x<1$ and $f'(x)=-2x$ for $x>1$.
      >
      > At $x=1$ the slopes are $2$ from the left and $-2$ from the right: a corner, so $f'(1)$ does not exist, and $x=1$ is critical.
      >
      > $f'=0$ at $x=0$, also critical.
      >
      > Signs: $x<0$: $f'<0$; $0<x<1$: $f'>0$; $x>1$: $f'<0$.
      >
      > So $x=0$ is a minimum ($f(0)=2$) and $x=1$ is a maximum ($f(1)=3$).

      > [!success] Answer (printed)
      > Increase: $0<x<1$. Decrease: $x>1$ and $x<0$. Critical: $x=0,1$. Extremum: $x_{\min}=0$, $x_{\max}=1$.
    related_terms: ["relative-extremum", "critical-point", "piecewise-function", "corner-point", "continuity"]

      **(c)** $\;f(x)=6\sqrt[3]{(4-x)^{2}}+4$
    solution: |
      > [!note] Working reconstructed to reach the printed answer
      > $f(x)=6(4-x)^{2/3}+4$, so
      >
      > $$
      > f'(x)=6\cdot\tfrac23(4-x)^{-1/3}\cdot(-1)=\frac{-4}{\sqrt[3]{4-x}}.
      > $$
      >
      > $f'$ never equals $0$ and is undefined at $x=4$, where $f(4)=4$ is defined: critical.
      >
      > $x<4$: $4-x>0$, so $f'<0$. $x>4$: $4-x<0$, the cube root is negative, so $f'>0$.
      >
      > The sign goes $-\to+$: minimum at $x=4$ (a cusp, with $f(4)=4$).

      > [!success] Answer (printed)
      > Increase: $x>4$. Decrease: $x<4$. Critical: $x=4$. Extremum: $x_{\min}=4$.

      **(d)** $\;f(x)=x^{4}(x-6)^{2}$
    solution: |
      > [!note] Working reconstructed to reach the printed answer
      > Product rule:
      >
      > $$
      > f'(x)=4x^3(x-6)^2+2x^4(x-6)=2x^3(x-6)\big[2(x-6)+x\big]=6x^3(x-6)(x-4).
      > $$
      >
      > Zeros: $x=0,4,6$. Signs: $x<0$: $-$; $0<x<4$: $+$; $4<x<6$: $-$; $x>6$: $+$.
      >
      > So minimum at $x=0$ ($f=0$), maximum at $x=4$ ($f=4^4\cdot4=1024$), minimum at $x=6$ ($f=0$).

      > [!success] Answer (printed)
      > Increase: $0<x<4$, $x>6$. Decrease: $x<0$, $4<x<6$. Critical: $x=0,4,6$. Extremum: $x_{\min}=0$, $x_{\max}=4$, $x_{\min}=6$.

    solution: |
      > [!note] Working reconstructed to reach the printed answer

      > Increase: $0<x<1$. Decrease: $x>1$. Critical: $x=1$. Extremum: $x_{\max}=1$.

    text: |
      Marginal profit, in dollars, when the firm invests $x$ thousand dollars a year in advertising:

      $$
      P'(x)=200x-\frac{1}{10}x^{2}+500,\qquad 0\le x\le1000.
      $$

      The firm now invests $\$20{,}000$ a year and its profit is $\$100{,}000$ a year. What is the yearly profit if it invests $\$21{,}000$?

      Lecture: [[Lec_09-Marginal Functions and Tangent Lines of Composites]].
    solution: |
      > [!note] Working reconstructed to reach the printed answer
      > Here $x=20$ means $\$20$ thousand. Use marginal profit as the extra profit from one more unit (one thousand dollars):
      >
      > $$
      > P'(20)=200\cdot20-\tfrac1{10}\cdot400+500=4000-40+500=4460.
      > $$
      >
      > New profit $\approx 100{,}000+4460=104{,}460$.

      > [!success] Answer (printed)
      > $\$104{,}460$.

      > [!warning] Printed answer is the marginal approximation
      > The $\$4460$ is the marginal profit **at** $x=20$ used as the gain from the whole next thousand dollars. My check with the exact change, $\int_{20}^{21}P'(x)\,dx=\big[100x^2-\tfrac{x^3}{30}+500x\big]_{20}^{21}\approx4557.97$, gives about $\$104{,}558$. The printed $\$104{,}460$ is kept as the course answer: it is the "marginal function approximates the next unit" reading from Lecture 9.

      > At $2000$ units one more unit sold adds about $\$6$ of revenue. At $5000$ units revenue is at its peak: one more unit adds nothing. At $7000$ units, selling one more unit means the price must fall so far that revenue drops about $\$4$.
