      (a) $x$- and $y$-intercepts. (b) Intervals of increase and decrease. (c) Relative extrema. (d) Concavity intervals. (e) Inflection points. (f) Asymptotes.
    solution: |
      > [!note] Working reconstructed to reach the printed answer
      > - **Intercepts:** the curve crosses the $x$-axis at $c$, $e$, $g$, $i$ and the $y$-axis at height $f$.
      > - **Asymptotes:** dashed vertical lines at $x=d$ and $x=h$; dashed horizontal line at height $m$ (the left branch flattens onto it); and the $x$-axis $y=0$ (the right branch flattens onto it).
      > - **Monotonic behaviour:** the left branch falls to a minimum at $b$, then rises to $+\infty$ at $d$. In the middle the curve rises from $-\infty$ at $d$ to a peak at $0$ (height $f$), then falls to $-\infty$ at $h$. The right branch falls from $+\infty$ at $h$ to a minimum at $j$, then rises toward $y=0$. The asymptote values $d$ and $h$ are not in the domain, so they are excluded from the intervals.
      > - **Concavity:** the left branch bends down, then switches at $a$ to a bowl, which holds until the asymptote $d$. The middle is a cap, from $d$ to $h$. On the right, a bowl from $h$ to $k$, then the curve bends down toward $y=0$. Concavity changes only at $a$ and $k$ (the changes at $d$ and $h$ are at excluded points).

      > [!success] Answer (printed)
      > (a) $(c,0),\ (e,0),\ (g,0),\ (i,0),\ (0,f)$.
      > (b) Increasing: $b<x<d$, $d<x<0$, $x>j$. Decreasing: $x<b$, $0<x<h$, $h<x<j$.
      > (c) $x_{\min}=b$, $x_{\max}=0$, $x_{\min}=j$.
      > (d) Concave down: $x<a$, $d<x<h$, $x>k$. Concave up: $a<x<d$, $h<x<k$.
      > (e) Inflection: $x=a$, $x=k$.
      > (f) $y=m$, $y=0$, $x=d$, $x=h$.

      > $$

      >

    text: |
      $$
      f(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x}{(x-1)^2},& x\ne1\\[6pt] 2,& x=1\end{cases}
      $$

      **(a)** Investigate: (1) domain, (2) intercepts, (3) asymptotes, (4) extrema, (5) increase/decrease, (6) concavity, (7) inflection, (8) graph. **(b)** On $x<0$, find the point where the rate of change of $f$ is most negative. **(c)** Using (a), find the relative extrema of $y=\lvert f(x)\rvert$.

      **(a)**
    solution: |
      ![](/images/maths-for-economists-a/ps12-q5-graph.png)

      > [!note] Working reconstructed to reach the printed answer
      > 1. **Domain:** the formula fails at $x=1$ but the second line defines $f(1)=2$, so the domain is all $x$.
      > 2. **Intercepts:** $f(0)=0$ and $f(x)=0$ only at $x=0$: $(0,0)$.
      > 3. **Asymptotes:** near $x=1$ the numerator tends to $1$ and the denominator to $0^+$, so $f\to+\infty$ from both sides: vertical asymptote $x=1$ (the value $f(1)=2$ does not remove it). At $\pm\infty$, $f\to0$: horizontal $y=0$.
      > 4. **Derivative:**
      >
      > $$
      > f'(x)=\frac{(x-1)^2-2x(x-1)}{(x-1)^4}=\frac{(x-1)-2x}{(x-1)^3}=\frac{-(x+1)}{(x-1)^3}.
      > $$
      >
      > $f'=0$ at $x=-1$, with $f(-1)=-\tfrac14$.
      >
      > 5. **Signs of $f'$:** $x<-1$: $-$; $-1<x<1$: $+$; $x>1$: $-$. So decreasing for $x<-1$ and $x>1$, increasing for $-1<x<1$. Minimum at $(-1,-0.25)$.
      > 6. **Second derivative:**
      >
      > $$
      > f''(x)=\frac{-(x-1)+3(x+1)}{(x-1)^4}=\frac{2(x+2)}{(x-1)^4}.
      > $$
      >
      > The denominator is positive for $x\ne1$, so concave up for $x>-2$ (with $x\ne1$) and concave down for $x<-2$.
      >
      > 7. **Inflection:** at $x=-2$: $f(-2)=\dfrac{-2}{9}$.

      > [!success] Answer (printed)
      > 1. All $x$. 2. $(0,0)$. 3. $y=0$, $x=1$. 4. $(-1,-0.25)$ minimum. 5. Increasing: $-1<x<1$; decreasing: $x<-1$, $x>1$. 6. Concave up: $-2<x<1$, $x>1$; concave down: $x<-2$. 7. Inflection $(-2,-\tfrac29)$.
    related_terms: ["inflection-point", "concavity", "vertical-asymptote", "horizontal-asymptote", "domain"]
