    text: |
      **A density made of two rectangles.** $X$ is a continuous random variable with the density function

      $$
      f(x) =
      \begin{cases}
      2 & 0 \le x \le 0.3 \\
      1 & 0.5 \le x \le 0.9 \\
      0 & \text{otherwise}
      \end{cases}
      $$
    solution: |
      For a [[Continuous Random Variable]], probability is **area under the density curve**, so almost every question reduces to drawing the graph and computing areas.

      | Tool | What it says |
      |---|---|
      | Valid density | $f(x) \ge 0$ and total area $= 1$ |
      | CDF | $F(t) = P(X \le t) = \int_{-\infty}^{t} f(x)\,dx$ (the running area) |
      | Percentile | $C_p$ solves $F(C_p) = p$; the median is $C_{0.5}$ |
      | Expected value | $E(X) = \int x\,f(x)\,dx$ |
      | Function of $X$ | $E(aX) = aE(X)$ is linear; $E(X^2)$ needs its own integral |

      The habit that makes the whole set easy: **draw the graph first**, then split the area into rectangles and triangles.

      ![](/images/statistics-a/ps10-q1-pdf-cdf-two-rectangles.png)

      *Left: the density is two rectangles, with a gap (no density) between 0.3 and 0.5. Right: the CDF rises steeply, goes flat across the gap, rises more slowly, then stays at 1.*
    related_terms: ["continuous-random-variable", "probability-density-function", "cumulative-distribution-function"]

  - id: "1a"
    text: |
      Show that this is a valid probability density function (the area under the curve is 1).
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > Total area $= 1$, so it is a valid density function.

      The area is the sum of two rectangles (height times width). Both heights are positive, so $f \ge 0$ holds.

      $$
      2 \cdot 0.3 + 1 \cdot (0.9 - 0.5) = 0.6 + 0.4 = 1
      $$

  - id: "1b"
    text: |
      What is the CDF of $X$?
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > $$
      > F(x) =
      > \begin{cases}
      > 0 & x < 0 \\
      > 2x & 0 \le x \le 0.3 \\
      > 0.6 & 0.3 < x < 0.5 \\
      > x + 0.1 & 0.5 \le x \le 0.9 \\
      > 1 & 0.9 < x
      > \end{cases}
      > $$

      Build it region by region, as "area collected so far":

      - **$x < 0$:** no area yet, so $F = 0$. **$x > 0.9$:** all the area is behind us, so $F = 1$.
      - **$0 \le x \le 0.3$:** the integral of the constant density 2 is

      $$
      F(x) = \int_0^x 2\,du = 2x
      $$

      - **$0.3 < x < 0.5$:** there is no extra density in the gap, so the CDF stays at its value at $x = 0.3$, which is $2 \cdot 0.3 = 0.6$.
      - **$0.5 \le x \le 0.9$:** the earlier area 0.6 plus the new rectangle of height 1 and width $x - 0.5$:

      $$
      F(x) = 0.6 + (x - 0.5) \cdot 1 = x + 0.1
      $$

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > Flat stretches of the CDF are exactly the gaps where the density is 0. A steeper CDF means a higher density (slope $2$ on the first block, slope $1$ on the second). The pieces join up with no jumps: $F(0.3) = 0.6$ from both sides, and $F(0.5) = 0.5 + 0.1 = 0.6$.
    related_terms: ["cumulative-distribution-function", "probability-density-function"]

  - id: "1c"
    text: |
      What is the median of this distribution?
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > $Md = 0.25$

      By definition $F(Md) = 0.5$. Since $0.5 < 0.6$ and $F$ reaches 0.6 at $x = 0.3$, the median must lie in the first block $0 \le x \le 0.3$. There $F(x) = 2x$:

      $$
      2x = 0.5 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{0.5}{2} = 0.25
      $$

  - id: "1d"
    text: |
      What is the 90th percentile of this distribution?
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > $C_{0.9} = 0.8$

      By definition $F(C_{0.9}) = 0.9$. Since $0.9 > 0.6$, the value must lie in the second block $0.5 \le x \le 0.9$, where $F(x) = x + 0.1$:

      $$
      x + 0.1 = 0.9 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 0.8
      $$

      > [!warning] Pick the right piece of the CDF
      > Always compare the target probability with the CDF values at the block boundaries (here 0.6) to decide which formula to invert. Using $2x$ for the 90th percentile would give $0.45$, which is wrong.
    related_terms: ["percentile", "cumulative-distribution-function"]

  - id: "1e"
    text: |
      What are the percentiles of the values $x = 0.44$ and $x = 0.63$?
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > $0.44$ is the **60th** percentile ($C_{0.6} = 0.44$). $0.63$ is the **73rd** percentile ($C_{0.73} = 0.63$).

      Here we go the other way: plug the value into the CDF.

      - $0.44$ lies in $0.3 < x < 0.5$, where $F$ is flat, so $F(0.44) = 0.6$.
      - $0.63$ lies in $0.5 \le x \le 0.9$, so $F(0.63) = 0.1 + 0.63 = 0.73$.

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > Every value in the gap $0.3 < x < 0.5$ is the 60th percentile, because no probability sits between 0.3 and 0.5. So $C_{0.6}$ is not a single point here; the official solution reports $0.44$ because that is the value asked about.

  - id: "2b"
    text: |
      What is the value of $a$ for which the following is a valid density function?

      $$
      f(x) =
      \begin{cases}
      a - x & 0 \le x \le a \\
      0 & \text{otherwise}
      \end{cases}
      $$
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > $a = \sqrt{2}$

      The triangle has base $a$ and height $f(0) = a$, so we need

      $$
      \frac{a \cdot a}{2} = 1
      $$

      $$
      a^2 = 2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad a = \pm\sqrt{2}
      $$

      The density is 0 for $x < 0$ and the interval is $0 \le x \le a$, so $a$ must be positive. The only valid solution is $a = \sqrt{2}$.

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > This is the same density that appears in [[Lec_10-Continuous Random Variables and the Uniform Distribution]], where it is written as a rising triangle $f(x) = x$ on $[0, \sqrt{2}]$. Here the triangle is mirrored, but the area argument is identical.

  - id: "3c"
    text: |
      What is the expected value and standard deviation of $X$?
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > $E(X) = 1.5$ and $\mathrm{Var}(X) = \dfrac{49}{12} = 4\tfrac{1}{12}$.

      Using the uniform formulas, with $a = -2$ and $b = 5$:

      $$
      E(X) = \frac{a + b}{2} = \frac{-2 + 5}{2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5
      $$

      $$
      \mathrm{Var}(X) = \frac{(b - a)^2}{12} = \frac{(5-(-2))^2}{12} = \frac{7^2}{12} = \frac{49}{12} = 4\tfrac{1}{12}
      $$

      > [!note] Standard deviation is not stated in the source
      > The official solution stops at the variance. The standard deviation is its square root (a step added in the vault note, with no change to the source's numbers):
      >
      > $$
      > \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{49}{12}} = \frac{7}{2\sqrt{3}} \approx 2.02
      > $$
    related_terms: ["expected-value", "variance", "standard-deviation", "continuous-uniform-distribution"]

  - id: "3d"
    text: |
      Jody proposes a bet: she will pay you (or take from you) $10X$ shekels. What is the expected value of this bet?
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > The expected value of the bet is **15 shekels**.

      Multiplying by 10 is a [[Linear Transformation]], so

      $$
      E(10X) = 10 \cdot E(X) = 10 \cdot 1.5 = 15
      $$

      > [!warning] Typo in the source
      > The official solution's final sentence says "the expected value of the bet is 25 shekels", but the line directly above it computes $E(10X) = 15$. **Check:** $10 \times 1.5 = 15$. The answer is 15; "25" is a typo.

  - id: "3e"
    text: |
      Jorden proposes a bet: you pay him 2 shekels and he will pay you $X^2$ shekels. What is the expected value of this bet?
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > The expected value of the bet is $4\tfrac{1}{3}$ shekels.

      Squaring is **not** linear, so $E(X^2) \ne E(X)^2$. Compute $E(X^2)$ directly from the density:

      $$
      E(X^2) = \int_{-2}^{5} x^2 \cdot \frac{1}{7}\,dx = \frac{1}{7}\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-2}^{5}
      $$

      $$
      = \frac{1}{7} \cdot \frac{5^3 - (-2)^3}{3} = \frac{125 + 8}{21} = \frac{133}{21} = 6\tfrac{1}{3}
      $$

      The 2 shekels paid to enter is a fixed cost, so subtract it:

      $$
      E(X^2 - 2) = E(X^2) - 2 = 6\tfrac{1}{3} - 2 = 4\tfrac{1}{3}
      $$

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > $E(X)^2 = 1.5^2 = 2.25$ would badly understate the answer. Squaring makes both ends of the range ($-2$ and $5$) positive and magnifies the large values, so $E(X^2)$ is well above $E(X)^2$. The gap is exactly the variance: $6\tfrac13 - 2.25 = 4\tfrac1{12}$.
    related_terms: ["expected-value", "continuous-uniform-distribution", "probability-density-function", "variance"]
    source_doc_page: 1
