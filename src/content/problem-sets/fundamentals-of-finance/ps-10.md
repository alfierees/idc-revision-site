  - id: "1a"
    text: |
      **From the CML to a share's return.** Portfolio P has an expected return of $25\%$ and an SD of $4\%$. It combines a risk-free asset (expected return $5\%$) with the market portfolio (expected return $20\%$).

      What is the SD of the market portfolio?
    solution: |
      P mixes the market and the risk-free asset, so it is efficient and lies on the [[Capital Market Line]]:

      $$
      0.25 = 0.05 + \frac{0.20 - 0.05}{\sigma_m}(0.04) \Rightarrow \boxed{\sigma_m = 3\%}
      $$
    related_terms: ["capital-market-line", "market-portfolio"]
  - id: "1b"
    text: |
      In the same market, what is the expected return of a share with an SD of $2\%$ and a correlation of $0.5$ with the market portfolio?
    solution: |
      A single share is not efficient, so price it with the [[Security Market Line]]:

      $$
      \beta_i = \rho_{i,m}\frac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_m} = 0.5 \times \frac{0.02}{0.03} = 0.333
      $$

      $$
      E(r_i) = 0.05 + \beta_i(0.20 - 0.05) = 0.05 + 0.15(0.333) = \boxed{10\%}
      $$

      > [!warning] Typos in the official file
      > It prints $0.5 \times \frac{0.2}{0.03}$ and $0.05 + \beta_i(0.02 - 0.05)$; the numbers it actually uses are $0.02$ and $0.15$, as above.
    related_terms: ["security-market-line", "beta", "correlation"]
  - id: "2"
    text: |
      **Which portfolios are efficient?**

      | | A | B | C | D |
      |---|---|---|---|---|
      | Expected return | 7% | 8% | 12% | 15% |
      | SD | 3.75% | 7.25% | 10% | 14.5% |

      The risk-free rate is $4\%$ and at least one portfolio is efficient. (a) Which portfolios are efficient? (b) If the market's SD is $8.75\%$, what is its expected return?
    solution: |
      **(a)** All efficient portfolios lie on the CML, so they share its slope, the [[Sharpe Ratio]] $\frac{E(r_i) - r_f}{\sigma_i}$:

      | | A | B | C | D |
      |---|---|---|---|---|
      | Slope | 0.80 | 0.55 | 0.80 | 0.76 |

      The highest slope is the CML's: **A and C are efficient**. (The official file prints C's denominator as 0.01; it is 0.10.)

      **(b)** $0.8 = \frac{E(r_m) - 0.04}{0.0875} \Rightarrow \boxed{E(r_m) = 11\%}$.
    related_terms: ["sharpe-ratio", "capital-market-line", "efficient-frontier"]
  - id: "3"
    text: |
      **A share as volatile as the market.** All investors follow the CAPM. A friend has found a share that is positively correlated with the market and has the same SD as the market portfolio.

      (a) Is this possible? (b) What can be said about the share's beta compared with the market's? (c) If the market's SD is twice the share's specific SD, what is the share's beta?
    solution: |
      **(a)** Yes. A share's SD combines systematic and specific risk, and that total can happen to equal the market's.

      **(b)** Variance decomposition: $\sigma_i^2 = \beta_i^2\sigma_m^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2$. With $\sigma_i = \sigma_m$ and $\sigma_\varepsilon > 0$: $\sigma_m^2 > \beta_i^2\sigma_m^2$, so $\boxed{0 < \beta_i < 1}$.

      **(c)** $\beta_i^2 = 1 - \left(\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon}{\sigma_m}\right)^2 = 1 - \left(\frac12\right)^2 = 0.75 \Rightarrow \boxed{\beta_i = 0.866}$.

      > [!warning] The official file says $\beta_i > 1$ in (b)
      > That contradicts its own inequality and its part (c) answer. The lecturer's board in the last class also gives $0 < \beta_i < \beta_m = 1$.
    related_terms: ["beta", "systematic-risk", "idiosyncratic-risk"]
  - id: "4"
    text: |
      **Twiddle-dee and Twiddle-dam** invest optimally. Twiddle-dee's portfolio has an expected return of $6\%$; Twiddle-dam's has an SD of $12\%$. The market portfolio has an expected return of $8\%$ and an SD of $10\%$; the risk-free rate is $5\%$; the CAPM holds.

      (a) Write the CML. (b) What share of each brother's portfolio is in the risk-free asset? (c) Can you rank their risk aversion? (d) Write the SML. (e) What is each portfolio's beta?
    solution: |
      **(a)** $E(r_p) = 0.05 + \frac{0.08 - 0.05}{0.10}\sigma_p = 0.05 + 0.3\,\sigma_p$.

      **(b)** Both portfolios are efficient, so $\sigma_p = (1 - x_f)\sigma_m$.
      - Dee: $0.06 = 0.05 + 0.3\sigma_p \Rightarrow \sigma_p = 3.33\%$, so $x_f = 66.7\%$ (a lender).
      - Dam: $0.12 = (1 - x_f)(0.10) \Rightarrow x_f = -20\%$ (a borrower: he borrows 20% of his wealth and puts 120% in the market).

      **(c)** Yes. Dam is levered and holds the riskier portfolio, so he is **less risk averse** than Dee.

      **(d)** $E(r_i) = 0.05 + \beta_i(0.08 - 0.05) = 0.05 + 0.03\,\beta_i$.

      **(e)** For an efficient portfolio $\beta_p = x_m = 1 - x_f$: Dee $\beta = 0.333$, Dam $\beta = 1.2$.
    related_terms: ["capital-market-line", "security-market-line", "beta"]
