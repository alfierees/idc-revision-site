    text: |
      **Changes in demand and in quantity demanded.** For each task, describe the curves in a graph before and after the change, pick one of three options (demand increases, decreases, or does not change) and give the reason for the shift.

      **(a)** What would happen to the demand for foreign holiday deals if there were an increase in consumers' income? Draw the initial and the new demand curve. Does demand increase, decrease or stay the same? Why?
    solution: |
      The whole curve shifts up and to the right, so **demand increases**. Reason: foreign holidays are a [[Normal Good]].

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > A normal good is one you buy more of when richer, at every price. Income is not the good's own price, so this is a shift, not a movement along the curve.

      ![](/images/principles-of-micro/ps03-q1-demand-shifts.png)

      *All five answers (1a to 1e) shift the whole demand curve. The solid blue line is the original demand curve D0, the dashed red line is the new curve D1, the grey line is supply. The diagrams are schematic: the source graphs carry no numbers.*
    related_terms: ["normal-good", "change-in-demand", "demand-curve"]

      **(b)** What would happen to the demand for hot dogs if you lost your job, under the condition that hot dogs are considered an inferior good for you (as opposed to steaks in restaurants)? Draw the initial and the new demand curve. Does demand increase, decrease or stay the same? Why?
    solution: |
      The whole curve shifts up and to the right, so **demand increases**. Reason: when income falls, the quantity demanded of an [[Inferior Good]] increases.

      > [!warning] Exam trap
      > Income fell, yet demand for the good **rose**. For an inferior good the income effect has the opposite sign to a normal good. Read the type of good before deciding the direction.

      **(c)** What would happen to the demand for waffles if the price of French pancakes increases? Draw the initial and the new demand curve. Does demand increase, decrease or stay the same? Why?
    solution: |
      The whole curve shifts up and to the right, so **demand increases**. Reason: French pancakes are a substitute for waffles. When the price of a substitute rises, people turn to the current product, which has become relatively cheaper.

      Link: [[Substitutes]].

  - id: "1d"
    text: |
      **(d)** What would happen to the demand for mobile phone apps if the price of mobile phones decreases significantly? Draw the initial and the new demand curve. Does demand increase, decrease or stay the same? Why?
    solution: |
      The whole curve shifts up and to the right, so **demand increases**. Reason: when phones get cheaper, more people buy them (a rise in the quantity demanded of phones). Apps and phones are [[Complements]], so as more people own phones they demand more apps at every price of an app.

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > The cheaper phone is a **movement along** the phone market's curve. The same event is a **shift** in the app market's curve, because for apps the phone price is a "price of a related good". The same price change can be either, depending on which market you are looking at.
    related_terms: ["complements", "change-in-demand", "change-in-quantity-demanded", "movement-along-a-curve"]

  - id: "1e"
    text: |
      **(e)** What would happen to the demand for fizzy drinks if a well-researched article about the dangerous health consequences of their consumption appeared in a national newspaper? Draw the initial and the new demand curve. Does demand increase, decrease or stay the same? Why?
    solution: |
      The whole curve shifts down and to the left, so **demand decreases**.

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > The official key gives no written reason here. The cause is a change in **tastes** (information that makes the good less attractive), one of the non-price determinants of demand in [[Lec_03-Perfect Competition and Demand]]. The price of fizzy drinks has not changed, so it is a shift and not a movement. This reasoning is mine.

  - id: "2b"
    text: |
      **Multiple choice 2.** Pizza is a normal good if the demand

      - a. for pizza rises when income rises.
      - b. for pizza rises when the price of pizza falls.
      - c. curve for pizza slopes upward.
      - d. curve for pizza shifts to the right when the price of burritos rises, assuming pizza and burritos are substitutes.
    solution: |
      **a. for pizza rises when income rises.**

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > Option b describes the [[Law of Demand]] (a movement along the curve, true of nearly every good). Option c is the opposite of the law. Option d describes a substitute and not the income response. Only a defines a [[Normal Good]]. This reasoning is mine.

  - id: "2c"
    text: |
      **Multiple choice 3.** Which of the following would shift the demand curve for gasoline to the right?

      - a. a decrease in the price of gasoline
      - b. an increase in consumer income, assuming gasoline is a normal good
      - c. an increase in the price of cars, a complement for gasoline
      - d. a decrease in the expected future price of gasoline
    solution: |
      **b. an increase in consumer income, assuming gasoline is a normal good.**

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > Option a is a movement along the curve. Option c lowers demand for a complement (dearer cars, fewer drivers, less gasoline), a shift left. Option d: if buyers expect a lower price later, they wait, so demand now falls, a shift left. Only b shifts it right. This reasoning is mine.
    related_terms: ["change-in-demand", "normal-good", "complements", "movement-along-a-curve"]

  - id: "2d"
    text: |
      **Multiple choice 4.** Suppose that a decrease in the price of good X results in fewer units of good Y being demanded. This implies that X and Y are

      - a. complement goods.
      - b. normal goods.
      - c. inferior goods.
      - d. substitute goods.
    solution: |
      **d. substitute goods.**

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > If X gets cheaper and people buy less Y, they have switched from Y to X, which is what [[Substitutes]] do. For complements the two would move in the same direction (cheaper X, more Y). Normal and inferior describe the response to income, not to another good's price. This reasoning is mine.
    related_terms: ["substitutes", "complements", "normal-good", "inferior-good"]

  - id: "2e"
    text: |
      **Multiple choice 5.** Holding all other things constant, a higher price for ski lift tickets would

      - a. increase the number of skiers.
      - b. increase the price of skis.
      - c. decrease the number of skis sold.
      - d. decrease the demand for other winter recreational activities.
    solution: |
      **c. decrease the number of skis sold.**

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > Dearer lift tickets cut the quantity of tickets demanded (law of demand), so there are fewer skiers. Skis are a complement to lift tickets, so the demand for skis falls and fewer are sold. Option a contradicts the law of demand. Option b is wrong because falling demand for skis would, if anything, push the price of skis down. Option d is wrong because other winter activities are substitutes, whose demand would rise. This reasoning is mine.

**The one idea behind the whole set.** Two different things can change the quantity of a good bought, and the set trains you to tell them apart.

| What changed | Curve effect | Name |
|---|---|---|
| The good's **own price** | Movement **along** the same curve | [[Change in Quantity Demanded]] |
| Anything else (income, related prices, tastes, expectations) | The **whole curve shifts** | [[Change in Demand]] |

For every Part A question the recipe is: (1) name the factor that changed, (2) ask whether it is the good's own price (it never is here), (3) decide the direction from the type of good, (4) draw D0 and D1.

| Part | Change | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | Income up, holidays | Demand **increases** | Normal good |
| 1b | Income down, hot dogs | Demand **increases** | Inferior good |
| 1c | Price of French pancakes up | Demand for waffles **increases** | Substitute |
| 1d | Price of phones down | Demand for apps **increases** | Complement |
| 1e | Health article | Demand **decreases** | Tastes change |
| 2a to 2e | Multiple choice | a, then a, b, d, c | See each part |
