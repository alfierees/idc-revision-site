      **Barry's Golf Ball Manufacturing.** The table gives revenues and costs for Barry's Golf Ball Manufacturing. All data are per hour. Assume the market price is P = EUR 5. To decide how many golf balls per hour to produce, Barry needs to know his revenues and costs.

      Glossary on the sheet: P = market price; TR = total revenue; TC, FC, VC = total, fixed and variable cost; MR = marginal revenue; MC = marginal cost; ATC, AVC, AFC = average total, variable and fixed cost; AR = average revenue per unit.

      | Q | TC | FC |
      |---|---|---|
      | 0 | 3 | 3 |
      | 1 | 10 | 3 |
      | 2 | 15 | 3 |
      | 3 | 17 | 3 |
      | 4 | 18 | 3 |
      | 5 | 22 | 3 |
      | 6 | 27 | 3 |
      | 7 | 33 | 3 |
      | 8 | 40 | 3 |
      | 9 | 48 | 3 |
      | 10 | 57 | 3 |
    solution: |
      > [!tip] Intuition
      > A competitive firm is a [[Price Taker]]: it cannot move the price, so every extra ball sells for the same P. That makes MR = P. The firm keeps producing while the next ball adds more revenue (P) than cost (MC), and stops at the quantity where MC reaches P. See [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets]].

      > - **TR** = P x Q = 5Q.

  - id: "1b"
    text: |
      How many balls per hour should Barry produce? Explain what criteria you used to find this number. In case you have two equal profit values, choose the one generated by producing a bigger number of units.
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > Maximum profit is EUR 3, reached at Q = 5 and Q = 6. By the tie rule, Barry produces **6 balls per hour**.

      > [!example] Two ways to see it
      > **Total profit.** Profit rises to 3 at Q = 5, stays at 3 at Q = 6, then falls (2 at Q = 7). The maximum is 3, tied between 5 and 6, so the tie rule picks 6.
      >
      > **Marginal rule (MR = MC).** MR = 5 for every unit. The 6th ball has MC = 5, so it adds exactly as much cost as revenue: profit is unchanged (3 to 3). The 7th ball has MC = 6 > MR = 5, so it lowers profit. So the firm goes up to Q = 6 and no further. This is the [[Profit Maximisation]] rule from [[Lec_06-Firms in Competitive Markets]].
    related_terms: ["profit-maximisation", "marginal-revenue", "marginal-cost"]

  - id: "1d"
    text: |
      How did you decide how many balls Barry produces per hour when the price is EUR 6?
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > Profit is maximised at **7 balls per hour**, with maximum profit EUR 9.

      > [!example] Reasoning
      > Profit is 9 at both Q = 6 and Q = 7, so the tie rule from 1b picks the larger quantity, 7. Marginal check: the 7th ball has MC = 6 = MR, and the 8th has MC = 7 > 6. A higher price therefore moves Barry up his MC curve, from 6 balls to 7. That is how an MC curve traces out the firm's supply: see [[Short-Run Supply Curve]].

      **Multiple choice 1 (Nuby, water bottles).** Nuby, a competitive firm, produces children's water bottles. Fixed cost is USD 20 per day. The market price is USD 50 per bottle. What is the best daily quantity and the daily profit?

      Total variable cost (USD) by bottles per day:

      | Bottles | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
      |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
      | Total variable cost | 0 | 10 | 30 | 60 | 100 | 150 | 210 |

      a. Bottles = 5, daily profit = 80
      b. Bottles = 3, daily profit = 120
      c. Bottles = 6, daily profit = 100
      d. Bottles = 1, daily profit = 30
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer: (a)

      > [!example] Working
      > Total cost = VC + 20, total revenue = 50 x Q, profit = TR - TC.

      | Bottles | VC | TC | TR | Profit |
      |---|---|---|---|---|
      | 0 | 0 | 20 | 0 | -20 |
      | 1 | 10 | 30 | 50 | 20 |
      | 2 | 30 | 50 | 100 | 50 |
      | 3 | 60 | 80 | 150 | 70 |
      | 4 | 100 | 120 | 200 | 80 |
      | 5 | 150 | 170 | 250 | 80 |
      | 6 | 210 | 230 | 300 | 70 |

      Profit peaks at 80. The 5th bottle has MC = 150 - 100 = 50 = P, so it adds nothing; Q = 4 and Q = 5 both give 80, and (a) is the only option with a correct pair (5 bottles, profit 80). Option (b) claims profit 120 at 3 bottles, but the table gives 70.

    text: |
      When the price equals EUR 5, will the firm produce the quantity you found in (a) in the short run (i.e. your answers from last week)? Explain.
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer
      > The profit-maximising quantity is Q = 6. AVC at Q = 6 is 4.0, below the price of 5, so the firm covers its variable costs and **will produce this quantity in the short run**.

      > [!tip] Intuition
      > In the short run fixed cost is sunk (see [[Sunk Cost]]): it is paid whether Barry produces or not. So the only question is whether revenue covers variable cost. If P is at least AVC, producing loses less than shutting down. This is the [[Shutdown Point]] rule.

      **Multiple choice 3.** Which of the following is correct?

      a. When P = MC and P > AVC and P < ATC, the firm will produce in the short run, but will exit in the long run.
      b. When P = MC and P < AVC and P < ATC, the firm will produce in the short run, but will exit in the long run.

      d. All the answers a, b, c are wrong.
    solution: |
      > [!success] Answer: (a)
      > The official solution marks (a), and notes that (c) is not even possible.

      ![](/images/principles-of-micro/ps06-q3-price-bands.png)

      *Schematic only (no numbers from the sheet): the three price bands relative to AVC and ATC. The "min" lines are at the minimum points of the curves.*

      > [!example] Why each option
      > - **(a) correct.** P > AVC means revenue covers variable cost, so producing in the short run loses less than shutting down. P < ATC means revenue does not cover total cost, so there is a loss, and in the long run (when no cost is sunk) the firm leaves.
      > - **(b) wrong.** P < AVC means the firm does not even cover variable cost: it shuts down in the short run, so "will produce in the short run" is false.
      > - **(c) wrong.** P < AVC and P > ATC would need AVC > ATC. But ATC = AVC + AFC, and AFC is not negative, so ATC is never below AVC. The official solution gives exactly this reason.
      > - **(d) wrong**, since (a) is correct.
