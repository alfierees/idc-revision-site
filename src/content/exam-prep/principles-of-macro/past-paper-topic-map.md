## Patterns that repeat

| Stem | Answer every time |
|---|---|
| "Households increase consumption at every level of disposable income" | saving falls, **interest rate rises**, investment falls |
| "...decrease consumption" | saving rises, $r$ falls, **investment rises** |
| "If exports exceed imports..." | domestic **savings exceed domestic investment** |
| "Opening to trade, saving does not depend on $r$": closed rate below world rate | $NX > 0$ (the country lends) |
| Closed rate above world rate | $S < I$, $NX < 0$ |
| "Which is an assumption of Solow?" | constant TFP, diminishing returns to capital and labour (never increasing returns, never a saving rate that depends on $k$) |
| "Which is **not** an assumption?" | technology that keeps growing, $MPK$ rising with $k$, or a fixed labour force (the model has $n$) |
| "Which describes the steady state?" | $k$ and $y$ per worker constant; investment $= (\delta + n)k$ (total $K$, $Y$ still grow at $n$) |
| "...**not** in steady state?" | capital per worker changes between periods |
| Same function, $n$, $\delta$; different steady states | **different saving rates** (the starting $k$ is irrelevant) |
| $s$ falls permanently | $k$ and $y$ **permanently lower**; growth unchanged in the long run |
| Capital gifted away or destroyed | $k$, $y$ fall, then **return to the same** steady state |
| A capital gift received | $k$, $y$ rise, then fall back |
| Emigrants take their share of capital | $k$, $y$ **unchanged** |
| Maximise consumption, $Y = AK^{\alpha}L^{1-\alpha}$ | set **$s = \alpha$**; consumption share $1 - \alpha$ |
| "Three economists" on saving | only the one who says $s = \alpha$ (not $\delta + n$) is right |
| More capital, slower growth | **diminishing returns** to capital |
| Fewer workers, same $K$ | higher $k$, **lower total output** |
| "A decrease in reserves reflects..." | a current account deficit when the capital account is zero |
| "The balance of payments..." | **always sums to zero** |
