## Budget lines (Lec 01)

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Slope $-p_2/p_1$ | The good on the **horizontal** axis goes on top | $-p_1/p_2$ with $x_1$ horizontal |
| Intercepts $p/m$ | Intercepts are quantities | $m/p_1$, $m/p_2$ |
| Pivoting a price change around the wrong intercept | A price moves only its own good's intercept | Cheaper $x_1$: the $x_2$ intercept stays put |
| Discount applied to every unit | "Above 10 units" changes only the later units | Kink at the threshold, flatter after it |
| Thinking a price and income change always move the line | Equal proportional changes cancel | $(tp_1, tp_2, tm)$ is the same line |

## Preferences and utility (Lec 02 to 03)

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Calling a good with $\partial U/\partial x < 0$ a good | It is a **bad** | In a denominator or subtracted: bad (over the domain given) |
| "Same preferences, so same utility level" | Utility is ordinal | $U$ and $100U$ rank alike, levels differ |
| Accepting any transformation | Only **strictly increasing** ones keep the ranking | $-U$ and $1/U$ reverse it |
| Perfect substitutes drawn as an L | Substitutes are straight lines; complements are Ls | $\min$ means L, sum means line |
| Diminishing MRS read as "MRS gets more negative" | Convex curves **flatten** | $\lvert MRS\rvert$ falls as $x_1$ rises |

## The optimal bundle (Lec 04)

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Setting MRS = price ratio for concave utility | The tangency is the **worst** point on the line | Curves bowed out, or $x^a + y^a$ with $a > 1$: compare the corners |
| Tangency with perfect complements | There is no slope at the kink | Use $y = ax$ with the budget |
| Interior answer for perfect substitutes | Lines meet lines at a corner | Compare $a/p_1$ with $b/p_2$ |
| Quasilinear: forgetting the corner | If income is too low, $x_2$ would be negative | Check $x_2 = (m - p_1x_1)/p_2 \ge 0$ |
| Cobb-Douglas: letting $x_1$ react to $p_2$ | Spending shares are fixed | $x_1 = \tfrac{a}{a+b}\tfrac{m}{p_1}$ has no $p_2$ in it |

## Slutsky and Hicks (Lec 07)

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Drawing the Slutsky line through the endowment or the new bundle | It goes through the **old bundle** | Old bundle $A$, new slope |
| Drawing the Hicks line through $A$ | Hicks keeps **utility**, not the bundle | Tangent to the old indifference curve |
| "Hicks compensation is larger" | The Hicks line is on or below the Slutsky line | Hicks $\le$ Slutsky |
| Calling $B$ (the Slutsky point) the new choice | It is a thought experiment | The real choice is on the real new line |
| Substitution effect with the wrong sign | It always moves toward the good that got cheaper | Only the income effect can flip |
| "Inferior means Giffen" | Giffen needs the income effect to **win** | Inferior goods usually still have downward demand |
| "Both goods can be inferior" | Extra income has to be spent on something | At most one of two goods is inferior |

## Endowments and labor supply (Lec 08)

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Labor on the horizontal axis | The good is **leisure** | $L$ horizontal, $Y$ vertical, $H = T - L$ |
| Slope $-W$ when $P_Y \ne 1$ | The slope is the real wage | $-W/P_Y$ |
| Putting earnings into the endowment point | The endowment is $(T, W_0)$ | Earnings appear as you move left along the line |
| Pivoting an endowment budget around an intercept | It pivots around the endowment | The endowment is affordable at any prices |
| "A price fall always helps" | Not if she is a net **seller** of that good | Net demand sign first |
| Ignoring the cap or the no-work corner | The unconstrained answer may be infeasible | Check $H \le$ cap and $0 \le L \le T$ |
| Writing the answer "as a function of $W$" when $W$ cancels | The graph is then a flat line | Say so and draw it flat |

## Two periods (Lec 09)

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Using the nominal rate for the slope | Consumption is real | $1 + \rho = \frac{1+r}{1+\pi}$ |
| "Inflation makes everyone worse off" | It lowers the real rate, which helps **borrowers** | Saver or borrower first |
| "A higher rate always raises saving" | The income effect pushes the other way for savers | Saver: $C_1$ ambiguous |
| Pivoting around an intercept | The line pivots around the endowment $(I_1, I_2)$ | Mark $E$ on the graph first |
| Moving both branches when only one rate changes | A one-sided change creates a kink at $E$ | Borrowing branch right of $E$, saving left |
| No "range of B" | The question asks where the new bundle can be | Shade the part of the new line consistent with your table |
