## Open Q2, part by part (two periods)

| Part | What it asks | Every year? |
|---|---|---|
| a. | Draw the budget line and optimum, mark intercepts and slope ($r = \pi$ so slope $-1$) | **Yes** |
| b. | Savings positive, negative or zero | **Yes** |
| c. | Inflation rises (or falls): utility and the range of the new bundle | **Yes** |
| d. | The Slutsky line, substitution and income effects on one graph | **Yes** |
| e. | The interest rate changes instead | Most years |
| f. | Only the borrowers' (or only the savers') rate changes: kink at the endowment | Most years |

The person varies: Yoni with no income now and a taste for the future (2025, 2022), Yudit with $I_1 < I_2$ and no preference between periods (2020 A), and savers in other years. **Settle saver or borrower in part b and every later part follows from the tables in the cheat sheet.**

## The MCQ block, 2025 Moed A

| Q | Topic | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bads, increasing transformation | Both goods bad; same ranking for any $a, b$ |
| 2 | Concave utility, convex utility and income, $(\frac{U + 1000}{2})^2$ | Only the transformation statement |
| 3 | $U = XY$, $P_Y$ rises | Effects on $X$ equal and opposite |
| 4 | Consumer at her endowment, $P_X$ falls | Utility may rise; Slutsky compensation 0 |
| 5 | Price of Y up, Y up | X normal, Y inferior |
| 6 | Fair lottery | All three statements |
| 7 | Which statement is wrong | "Income effect always equals substitution effect" |
| 8 | $\sqrt{x_1} + \sqrt{x_2}$, $p_1 = 8$ | Less $x_1$ than $x_2$ |
| 9 | $X^a + Y^a$, $a > 1$, $P_Y$ falls | Only Y |
| 10 | 10% discount above 10 units | Slope $P_x/P_y$, then $0.9P_x/P_y$ |
