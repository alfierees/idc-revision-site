## The classical model and money

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| A tax rise lowers national saving | consumption falls by $MPC\cdot\Delta T$, so saving **rises** by that much | 2022 B Q1: $+60$ |
| Higher investment demand raises investment in the long run | saving is fixed in the classical model: only $r$ rises | $S = I(r)$, vertical $S$ |
| $i = r - \pi$ | Fisher adds expected inflation | $i = r + E\pi$ (2026 A Q6) |
| Inflation = money growth when output grows | real money demand grows with $Y$ | $\pi = \mu - g_Y$ (2022 B Q2: 7%) |
| An announced future money increase does nothing today | it raises expected inflation and $i$, lowers money demand, raises $P$ now | 2022 A Q4 |
| Open-market purchases raise the multiplier | they change the base $B$, not $m$ | $m$ depends on $cr$ and $rr$ |
| Bank debt left out of liabilities | everything the bank owes is a liability | 2026 A Q1 |

## Solow

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| A higher saving rate raises long-run growth | only the **level**; growth is set by $g$ | Solow vs endogenous growth (2022 B Q8) |
| Saving more always raises consumption | only when starting below the golden rule; consumption falls at first | compare $MPK$ with $\delta + n + g$ |
| Output per worker is constant in the steady state with technology | per **effective** worker is constant; per worker grows at $g$ | three-row growth table |
| Break-even investment $\delta k$ when $n$ or $g$ is positive | capital must also equip new workers and new efficiency | $(\delta + n + g)k$ |
| The rental rate grows with technology | $MPK = f'(k)$ and $k$ per effective worker is constant | constant (2021 A Q5) |
| Golden rule $MPK = 0$ or $MPK = n$ | consumption per worker peaks where the net return equals growth | $MPK - \delta = n + g$ |
| Population growth raises output per worker | it spreads capital thinner | $k^*$ falls (2021 sample Q1) |
| AK: any positive saving rate gives growth | growth needs $sA > \delta$ | 2024 B Q17: $s > 20\%$ |

## Keynesian cross and IS-LM

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Tax multiplier $= 1/(1 - MPC)$ | a tax cut raises spending only by $MPC$ per unit at first | $-MPC/(1 - MPC)$ (2024 A Q2) |
| Balanced-budget multiplier 0 | spending raises demand by 1, taxes cut it by $MPC$ | multiplier 1 |
| Output above equilibrium means firms raise output | unsold goods pile up as inventories | firms cut production |
| IS-LM gives the full Keynesian-cross effect | $r$ rises and crowds out investment | smaller (2022 B Q14) |
| Crowding out = cross shift − actual shift | that is the output gap; investment fell by $(1 - MPC)$ of it | 2021 sample Q14: 25, not 100 |
| More income lowers $r$ on LM | more income raises money demand; $r$ must rise | LM slopes up |
| A change in $P$ shifts AD | $P$ is on AD's axis: a movement along AD | it shifts LM |
| Monetary policy strongest with interest-elastic money demand | that makes LM flat and $M$ changes $r$ little | steep LM, flat IS |

## AD-AS

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| A demand shock changes output in the long run | prices adjust until $Y = \bar Y$ | only $P$ (and the mix) changes |
| After a recession LM shifts left | falling prices **raise** $M/P$ | LM shifts right (2022 B Q15) |
| One policy fixes both $Y$ and $P$ after a supply shock | stabilising one worsens the other | accommodate or not |
| Fewer sticky firms make SRAS flatter | more flexible prices make SRAS steeper | $\alpha = s/((1-s)a)$ |

## Mundell-Fleming

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| $e$ up read as depreciation | $e$ is foreign currency per unit of home currency | $e$ up = appreciation |
| Fiscal expansion raises $Y$ under floating rates | the appreciation cuts $NX$ by exactly the stimulus | $Y$ unchanged |
| Monetary expansion works under a fixed rate | defending the peg reverses it | powerless |
| A quota improves the trade balance under floating rates | appreciation brings $NX$ back | imports and exports both fall |
| $r$ changes in a small open economy | capital flows hold $r = r^*$ | only $e$ or $M$ adjust |
| A change in $e$ shifts IS* | $e$ is on IS*'s axis | movement along (2021 sample Q17) |
| Nominal and real exchange rates confused | real $= eP/P^*$ | 2024 A Q13: nominal 1/3, real 2 |
| Small-open answers for a large open economy | $r$ can move there | in between |

## Phillips curve

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Sign of the unemployment gap | unemployment **above** $u^n$ lowers inflation | $-\beta(u - u^n)$ |
| A long-run trade-off | in the long run $\pi = E\pi$ | vertical at $u^n$ |
| Credibility raises the sacrifice ratio | inflation falls through expectations instead of unemployment | lower (2026 A Q19) |
| Sacrifice ratio = cyclical unemployment | multiply by Okun's 2 | $2/\beta$ |
| Hysteresis: $u^n$ jumps back after the recession | it follows past unemployment | compute it period by period |
| Units: 0.04 against 4 | keep $u$, $u^n$ and $\pi$ all in decimals or all in points | 2026 A Q17 |
