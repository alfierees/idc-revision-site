> Counted from the 12 papers on this site: [[PP_01|2021 sample exam]], [[PP_02|2021 Moed A]], [[PP_03|2021 Moed B]], [[PP_04|2022 Moed A]], [[PP_05|2022 Moed B]], [[PP_06|2023 Moed A]], [[PP_10|2023 Moed B]], [[PP_11|2024 Moed A]], [[PP_07|2024 Moed B]], [[PP_08|2025 Moed A]], [[PP_09|2025 Moed B]] and [[PP_12|2026 Moed A]]. Every paper is 25 four-option questions except 2026 A (20 plus a bonus).

## Which answers you can trust

| Paper | Key |
|---|---|
| 2023 B, 2024 A, 2026 A | official answers (2024 A also has official working) |
| 2021 sample, 2021 A, 2022 B, 2023 A, 2024 B | official key for most questions, the rest worked out and flagged |
| 2021 B, 2022 A, 2025 A, 2025 B | no key: every answer is worked out and flagged |

Two official answers look wrong: 2026 A Q3 (the money multiplier with a constant currency ratio gives \$3.48M, not \$15.25M) and 2026 A Q13 (the "only deflationary" wording ignores the fall in $r$). Both are flagged on the paper's page.

## How the 25 questions split

| Model | Questions per paper | Lectures | Cheat sheet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solow growth, golden rule, endogenous growth | 4 to 6 (only 1 in 2026 A) | no deck (Mankiw Ch. 8 and 9) | §3 |
| Classical model and money (saving, quantity theory, Fisher, multiplier) | 2 to 8, more in recent papers | 3, 4, 5 | §1, §2 |
| Keynesian cross and IS-LM | 3 to 8 | 7, 8 | §4 |
| AD-AS, short run to long run, stabilisation | 4 to 9 | 6 | §5 |
| Mundell-Fleming (floating, fixed, large open economy) | 3 to 6 | 9 | §6 |
| Sticky prices, Phillips curve, sacrifice ratio, hysteresis | 2 to 5 | 14 | §7 |

The order inside a paper follows the book: growth and the classical model first, then IS-LM and AD-AS, then the open economy, then the Phillips curve.

## Questions that come back

| Question (short) | Answer | Papers |
|---|---|---|
| Why does LM slope up? | higher $Y$ raises money demand; $r$ must rise | 2021 sample, 2022 A, 2025 A, 2025 B |
| Why does AD slope down? | lower $P$, more $M/P$, LM right, lower $r$, more $I$ | 2021 sample, 2023 A, 2024 A |
| IS-LM vs Keynesian-cross multiplier | IS-LM smaller: $r$ rises, investment crowded out | 2022 B, 2023 A, 2021 sample |
| Fiscal policy, floating rate | $e$ up, $Y$ unchanged | almost every paper |
| Monetary policy, fixed rate | powerless | 2022 B, 2025 A |
| Import quota, floating vs fixed | no effect on $Y$ vs $Y$ up | 2021 B, 2023 A, 2024 B |
| Lower price level in Mundell-Fleming | $LM^*$ right, $e$ down, $Y$ up | 2021 A, 2023 B, 2026 A |
| Only source of lasting growth per person | technological progress | 2021 sample, 2022 A, 2022 B, 2024 A |
| Growth rates in the steady state | per effective worker 0, per worker $g$, total $n + g$ | 2021 sample, 2021 A, 2023 A |
| Rental rate / labour share in the steady state | constant | 2021 A, 2024 A, 2024 B |
| Saving rate and long-run growth | level only (Solow), growth only in endogenous models | 2022 B, 2025 B |
| Announced future money growth | $P$ rises today (money demand falls) | 2022 A, 2023 A, 2024 A, 2025 B |
| Long-run Phillips curve | vertical, no lasting trade-off | 2023 A, 2024 B |
| Rational expectations and credibility | lower sacrifice ratio | 2026 A |
| Two central banks (price vs output target) | demand shock: same action; supply shock: opposite | 2022 A, 2025 B, 2026 A |
| Payment apps cut money demand | LM right; to stabilise, cut $M$ | 2023 B, 2026 A |

## What this means for revision

1. **Learn the direction tables cold.** About 15 of the 25 questions are "which way does it move" in IS-LM, Mundell-Fleming or AD-AS; the [[Macro II Formula Sheet]] has the tables.
2. **Drill the six calculation types** above until each takes two minutes.
3. **Solow has no lecture deck but 4 to 6 questions a paper.** Learn the growth-rate table and the golden rule from the cheat sheet §3.
4. **Do the official papers first** (2023 B, 2024 A, 2026 A), then the partly official ones.
