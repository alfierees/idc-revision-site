> Most wrong options in this exam are built from one specific slip. If your answer matches an option, still run through the check in the right-hand column.

## The market block (Q1 to Q5)

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Forgetting the ½ in CS, PS or DWL | They are triangles | The option that is exactly double yours is the trap (2025 A Q2 b) |
| Using the price axis intercept of the wrong curve | CS uses the **demand** intercept, PS the **supply** intercept | CS height = $a - P^*$, PS height = $P^* - c$ |
| Shifting supply **down** for a tax on sellers | A tax raises the price sellers need | Tax: $c + T$. Subsidy: $c - s$ |
| Reading $P_B$ off the new supply curve but $P_S$ off the new one too | $P_S$ comes from the **original** supply (or $P_B - T$) | $P_B - P_S$ must equal $T$ exactly |
| Swapping buyer and seller prices | After a tax buyers always pay **more** than before, sellers get **less** | $P_B > P^* > P_S$ |
| Thinking it matters who the law taxes | Statutory incidence is irrelevant | Same $Q_T$, $P_B$, $P_S$ either way |
| Halving the DWL twice | $DWL = \tfrac12 \cdot T \cdot \Delta Q$, already one half | 2025 A Q4: 2.67, not 1.33 |
| Quantity after a tax **above** the old $Q^*$ | A tax always cuts quantity | Typo options like "333.37" are wrong as printed |
| Calling a non-binding control binding | Ceiling must be **below** $P^*$, floor **above** | A floor of 90 when $P^* = 100$ does nothing |
| Ceiling → "surplus" | A ceiling below equilibrium raises $Q_d$ and cuts $Q_s$ | Ceiling ⇒ shortage, floor ⇒ surplus |
| Quantity traded under a control = $Q_d$ | The **short side** trades | Ceiling: $Q_s$. Floor: $Q_d$ |

## Shifts and comparative statics

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| A change in the good's own price shifts its curve | Own price = **movement along**. Only other factors shift |
| Moving along the curve that shifted | The price change is a movement along the **other** curve |
| Supply "shifts up" read as "supply increases" | Up = **left** = a decrease in supply |
| Normal-good logic applied to an inferior good | Inferior: income ↓ ⇒ demand ↑ (potatoes, 2023 A Q6) |
| Expecting a **lower** future price ⇒ supply falls | Sellers sell more **now**: supply shifts right (2023 A Q7) |
| Claiming both $P$ and $Q$ when two curves shift | One is always ambiguous. Same direction: $Q$ certain. Opposite: $P$ certain |
| Not solving the starting equilibrium | Options say "price > 3", so you need $P^*$ first |
| Adding prices to build market demand | Market curves add **quantities** at each price (horizontal sum) |

## Elasticity

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Simple % change when the paper wants midpoint | Default to midpoint; if no option fits, try the simple method (2021 A Q15) |
| Reporting the price **change** when asked for the new price (or vice versa) | Re-read: "new price" vs "by how much must the price change" |
| Using slope as elasticity | A straight line has constant slope but elasticity changes along it |
| Thinking a price rise raises revenue when demand is elastic | Elastic: price and TR move **opposite** ways |
| Dropping the sign of cross-price or income elasticity | Sign is the answer: + substitutes / normal, − complements / inferior |
| Tax the elastic good to minimise DWL | Tax the **inelastic** one (antibiotics, gasoline, cigarettes) |

## Costs and the firm

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Counting only cash costs as cost | Economic cost includes forgone salary, rent, interest |
| Mixing months and years in profit questions | Convert everything to the period asked (12 months, 52 weeks) |
| Including sunk costs in a decision | Sunk costs never matter (the Mustang's 1,000) |
| Recomputing FC at each output | FC is constant: find it once, then divide by the new Q |
| Confusing AFC and AVC | $AVC = ATC - AFC$ (2022 B Q17 option a swaps them) |
| Shutdown when $P < ATC$ | Short run: shut down only if $P < \min AVC$. $P < ATC$ means exit in the **long run** |
| Producing where MC crosses P while **falling** | Use the crossing on the **rising** part of MC |
| $MC \ne P$ in long-run equilibrium | Long run: $P = MR = MC = \min ATC$ all at once |
| Zero economic profit ⇒ "the firm earns nothing" | Accounting profit can still be positive |
| Reading P off MR on a diagram with downward demand | Q from MR = MC, **price from demand** |

## Externalities, goods, trade

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Tax a positive externality | Positive ⇒ **subsidy**; negative ⇒ tax |
| Putting the externality on the wrong curve | Production externality moves the **cost** curve (MSC); consumption moves the **benefit** curve (MSB) |
| Pigouvian tax = gap at the **market** quantity | Evaluate both curves at the **target** quantity |
| "Free" means public good | Public = non-excludable **and** non-rival. Free food is still rival |
| Public good vs common resource | Both non-excludable; common resources are **rival** (over-used) |
| Absolute advantage in both ⇒ comparative in both | Comparative advantage is split between the two goods |
| OC from the wrong ratio | OC(X) = hours per X ÷ hours per Y, or Y-max ÷ X-max |
| Bow-shaped PPF from three workers drawn linear | Three different OCs ⇒ three slopes ⇒ bowed out |
| Terms of trade at an endpoint | Must be **strictly between** the two OCs |
| Tariff revenue makes the tariff "net positive" | Revenue is already in the total; net effect is still − DWL |
