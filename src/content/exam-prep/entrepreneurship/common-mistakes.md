> Most of these are flagged as exam traps or common errors in the lecture notes themselves. The right versions are on the [[Cheat Sheet (Classes 1–11)]].

## Terms (Class 1)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Using creativity, invention and innovation as synonyms | Creativity is the idea; innovation is its **successful implementation**; an invention may never be used |
| "Novel" means new to the world | New **within its context** is enough |
| Calling a subscription model a product innovation | It changes how value is captured: **business model** (outward, strategic) |
| Calling empowered front-line staff process innovation | It changes how people are managed: **management** (inward, strategic) |

## Structure and strategy (Classes 2 and 3)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Flat structures are best for innovation", full stop | Flat **generates** ideas but struggles to **scale** them; argue from fit with the environment |
| Listing pros and cons with no reason | Explain through decision rights, information flow and accountability |
| Mixing up the innovation-design exemplars | Centralized Apple, decentralized Johnson & Johnson, hybrid Procter & Gamble |
| Treating business model and strategy as the same | Model: how value is created and captured. Strategy: the initiatives that build competitive advantage |
| Blue ocean means inventing new technology | It means **uncontested market space**, which can come from a new combination of value; it reddens as imitators arrive |
| Swapping suppliers and buyers in the Five Forces | Suppliers sell inputs **to** you (upstream); buyers buy **from** you (downstream). The notes call this the most common error |
| A strong force makes the industry attractive | Strong forces **drain** profit; attractive industries have mostly **weak** forces |
| Five Forces with ratings but no reasons | Each rating needs an industry fact behind it, then an overall read-out |

## Culture and barriers (Class 6)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Hierarchy culture "cannot innovate" | It **limits radical** innovation but supports **incremental**, cost-cutting process innovation |
| Market culture is the most innovative because it is competitive | **Adhocracy** is most conducive to radical innovation; market culture drives incremental, customer-led change |
| Mixing up the OCAI exemplars | Clan Lego, adhocracy Nvidia, market GE, hierarchy McKinsey |
| Calling customer resistance an internal barrier | It is **external** (a specific actor); internal ones are mindset, competences, resources, structure |
| Proposing to "remove" an external barrier | External barriers are **managed** (partnerships, lobbying, timing); internal ones can be fixed |
| "Lack of competences" with no stage | Say which: discovery, incubation, or acceleration and commercialisation |

## Leadership and creativity (Classes 7 and 8)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Leadership is a job title | Rost: an **influence relationship** toward shared purposes; it can appear anywhere |
| Vision and mission swapped | Vision is the future horizon; mission is the daily steps now |
| "Leadership is better than management" | Both are needed: doing the right things and doing things right |
| Transformational leadership as "a charismatic boss" | It changes **vision, strategy and culture** and lifts followers past self-interest |
| More bonuses and tighter deadlines will raise creativity | Extrinsic pressure can crowd out **intrinsic** motivation; make the work meaningful first |
| Naming only two of Amabile & Pratt's components | All three: domain skills, creativity-relevant processes, task motivation |
| Assigning each person a permanent thinking hat | Everyone wears the **same hat at the same time**; hats are modes, not roles |
| Criticising ideas during brainstorming | Defer judgment; generate first, judge later |

## Design Thinking (Classes 9 to 11)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Running the five stages once, in a line | They loop: a failed test sends you back |
| Starting from feasibility or a technology | Start from **desirability**: what users need |
| Brainstorming solutions before the problem is defined | Close the first diamond (Discover, Define) before the second (Develop, Deliver) |
| Diverging and converging at the same time | Open up fully, then narrow down |
| A persona made up from the team's assumptions | Build it from **interview data**, with a real quote |
| Closed interview questions ("Do you like the bus?") | Open ones ("Walk me through your morning commute") |
| A design question with the solution inside it | User + need + problem; keep the "how" open |
| A design question that is too broad or too narrow | Broad enough for many ideas, narrow enough to act on |
| Polishing the first prototype | Rapid prototyping: simplest, cheapest, fastest; it is a tool for thinking |
| Testing usability on a static mockup | A mockup only **looks** like the product; usability needs an interactive prototype |
| Asking users whether they like it | Watch at least two real users **use** it |
