> **No past paper.** The course publishes no past exam or sample questions, so nothing here is taken from a real paper. The final exam is the individual assignment, worth 60%, "on the course material" (Class 1). The map below lists what each class gives you to be asked about, using the notes' own "exam trap" warnings and the in-class exercises as the best guide to question shapes.

## What each class can be asked

| Class | Topic | Definitions to quote | Likely question shapes | Cases to cite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction | Entrepreneurship (Stevenson), intrapreneurship, creativity, innovation (Amabile et al.), invention, organization | Distinguish creativity, invention and innovation (**flagged exam trap**); classify a scenario into the four types (**class exercise**) | Smartwatch, AI inventory, subscription software, mobile banking, eco packaging, empowered workers |
| 2 | Structure | Organisational structure | How structure supports or constrains innovation (**slide question**); pros and cons of a structure; "is flat best?" (**flagged trap**); innovation design | Apple, Johnson & Johnson, Procter & Gamble |
| 3 | Strategy | Business model, business strategy, red and blue ocean, Five Forces | Model vs strategy; red vs blue ocean; Five Forces on an industry; suppliers vs buyers (**flagged as the most common error**) | Netflix; fast food; streaming; airlines; Cirque du Soleil (notes) |
| 6 | Culture and barriers | Organisational culture, OCAI, barriers, radical innovation | Which OCAI culture suits radical innovation; one barrier and a practical solution (**class exercise**); internal vs external barriers | Lego, Nvidia, GE, McKinsey, Google (20% rule, Googliness, "YES" culture) |
| 7 | Leadership | Leadership (Rost), followership, vision, mission, transformational leadership | Vision vs mission; leadership vs management (**flagged recurring distinction**); transformational leadership in a company (**class clip**) | LinkedIn, Apple statements; Bezos and Amazon; Google's four levers |
| 8 | Problem solving | Creativity as process and product; Amabile & Pratt's three components; intrinsic and extrinsic motivation; psychological safety; progress principle | What drives creativity; why bonuses may not help; brainstorming rules; Six Thinking Hats | Osborn at BBDO; de Bono |
| 9 | Design Thinking | Design Thinking, empathy, Empathy Map, persona, Double Diamond | Five stages; desirability, feasibility, viability; map the Double Diamond to the five stages | IDEO (Kelley, Brown), Stanford d.school |
| 10 | First diamond | User interviews, Customer Journey Map, touchpoint, design question | Write a "How might we" question; good vs bad interview questions; persona fields | Reichman bus commute; homeless people in London |
| 11 | Second diamond | Prototyping, rapid prototyping, fidelity, iteration, product validation | Plan, Build, Test; wireframe vs mockup vs interactive prototype; why test with real users | Bus-stop screen prototype (notes' example) |
| Reading | Barriers (Sandberg & Aarikka-Stenroos 2014) | The full taxonomy | Classify barriers in a case; the discovery, incubation, commercialisation pipeline | — |

Classes 4, 5, 12 and 13 have no notes on the site. Class 11's notes say the GTM (Go-to-Market) Canvas was introduced the following week, so it appears here only as a team-assignment requirement.

## The question shapes, from most to least likely to need

| Shape | What the answer needs | Playbook |
|---|---|---|
| Define and distinguish two terms | Both definitions with their source, the one-line difference, an example of each | A |
| Classify a scenario | The framework, the two tests, the verdict, why not the neighbour | B |
| Apply a framework to a company or industry | Each element rated or described with evidence, then an overall conclusion | C, D |
| Diagnose a problem and recommend | Name the barrier or weak lever, the theory behind it, a concrete fix | E |
| Run a design-thinking step on a case | The stage, the tool, a filled-in example (design question, persona, prototype plan) | F, G |
| Analyse a company through the four levers | Structure, strategy, culture, leadership, and whether they reinforce each other | H |

The playbook letters refer to the [[Exam Answer Playbook]].
