## Frameworks to reproduce from memory

### Four types of innovation

| | Operational | Strategic |
|---|---|---|
| **Outward** | Product | Business model |
| **Inward** | Process | Management |

### Organizational structures

| Type | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Hierarchical | Clear chain of command | Slow decisions |
| Functional | Deep expertise | Silos |
| Flat | Fast decisions, more innovation | Hard to scale |
| Matrix | Cross-functional collaboration | Two bosses, power struggles |

### Innovation design

| Model | Where innovation sits | Exemplar |
|---|---|---|
| Centralized | A central innovation department | Apple |
| Decentralized | Each business unit | Johnson & Johnson |
| Hybrid | Corporate strategy connected to each unit | Procter & Gamble |

### Porter's Five Forces

New entrants, supplier power, buyer power, substitutes, rivalry. Strong forces drain profit, and an attractive industry has mostly weak forces.

### OCAI culture types

| Culture | Character | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Clan | Family-like, supportive | Lego |
| Adhocracy | Entrepreneurial, risk-taking. Most conducive to radical innovation | Nvidia |
| Market | Results-oriented, competitive | GE |
| Hierarchy | Formalised, efficiency-focused | McKinsey |

### Barriers to innovation (Sandberg & Aarikka-Stenroos 2014)

- **External, specific actors**: customer resistance, unsupportive government, shortage of external finance, rivalry.
- **External, macro environment**: undeveloped network and ecosystem, technological turbulence, inappropriate infrastructure, restrictive local culture.
- **Internal**: restrictive mindset, lack of competences (discovery, incubation, acceleration and commercialisation), insufficient resources, unsupportive structure.

### Double Diamond

| Diamond | Phase | Mode | Design Thinking stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | Discover | Diverge | Empathy |
| Problem | Define | Converge | Define |
| Solution | Develop | Diverge | Ideate, Prototype |
| Solution | Deliver | Converge | Test |

## Distinctions that are easy to confuse

- **Creativity vs. innovation vs. invention**: an idea, its successful implementation, and something new that may never be used.
- **Business model vs. business strategy**: how value is created and captured, and the initiatives that build competitive advantage.
- **Suppliers vs. buyers**: upstream sellers of inputs, and downstream purchasers of outputs.
- **Leadership vs. management**: vision and the "right things", and resources, schedules and "things right".
- **Intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation**: drive from the task itself, and drive from outside factors such as pay or deadlines.
- **Mockup vs. interactive prototype**: a mockup looks like the product, and an interactive prototype behaves like it.
- **Flat is not automatically best**: flat designs generate ideas but can struggle to commercialise and scale them. Argue from fit with the environment.
