## 3. The shape of the questions

| Pattern | Where | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| A **short case** (a startup, a study, two researchers) then a question | 14 of 19 | Find the concept the case illustrates before reading the options |
| A plain **definition** | 4, 7, 12, 13, 15 | Learn the course's wording: the options differ by one word |
| **"All the answers are correct"** is the best answer | 5, 6, 10, 11 | When every option is a valid method or a valid property, pick "all" |
| "No answer / none of the answers is correct" offered | 2, 4, 17 | Never the answer on this paper |
| A distractor that **reverses** the roles | 1, 17 | Check IV vs DV and structured vs unstructured both ways |
| A distractor that sounds **academic but isn't in the course** | 19 (citations, journal ranking) | Stick to the course's own list |

## 5. What to drill first

1. **Read a case and name its IV, DV and any third variable** (Q1, Q2, Lec 09's "spot the variables").
2. **Match a goal to a method** and spot when a case misuses "experiment" or uses an unrepresentative sample (Q3, Q9, Q11).
3. **Scales:** put any variable on nominal / ordinal / interval / ratio in one step (Q7, Q16, Lec 09's "hypotheses by scale").
4. **Bias and wording:** preconceived notions, leading questions, double-barrelled questions, sample and non-response bias (Q8, Lec 07, Lec 09).
5. **Definitions in the course's words:** positive correlation, random sampling, method section, the five criteria, quantitative's disadvantage, interview types.
6. **Then APA and PAIR**, which the sample didn't test but the course teaches.
