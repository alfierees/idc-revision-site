## More conceptual relationship patterns

### Data inheritance ("is a")

[[Data Inheritance]] is a 1-to-1 relationship where the child entity inherits all the parent's attributes and adds its own.

```text
Person (Name, Home Address)
  ↑ is a
Employee (Department, Salary)
  ↑ is a
Salesperson (Sales Goal, Bonus per Sale)
```

A Salesperson therefore also has a Department, Salary, Name and Home Address.

### Recursive relationships

In a [[Recursive Relationship]], an entity type relates to **itself**. People have parents who are also people. Groups can contain other groups.

### Weak entities and identifying relationships

A [[Weak Entity]] **cannot exist without its parent**. A Line Item cannot exist without a Purchase Order.

An [[Identifying Relationship]] links a weak entity to its parent. In diagrams, a **double rectangle** marks the weak entity and a **double rhombus** marks the identifying relationship. These can be 1-to-1, 1-to-many or many-to-many.

### Associative entities

An [[Associative Entity]] appears when **a relationship matures into an entity**. If you realise a relationship has its own attributes, promote it. A Registration linking Course, Semester and Student (and possibly Year) is the classic case.

## Logical building blocks

Every **entity becomes a table**, and every **attribute becomes a column**. Relationships take more work: they become **PK-FK relationships**.

### Tables, rows, columns and NULL

| First Name | Last Name | Shoe Size |
|---|---|---|
| Bart | Simpson | 36 |
| Homer | Simpson | NULL |
| Monty | Burns | 36 |

[[NULL]] is the standard SQL way to represent the **absence of data**. It avoids inventing stand-ins like `0`, `-1`, `""` or `"N/A"`, which can be mistaken for real values.

## Primary keys

A [[Primary Key]] is a column (or columns) that **uniquely identifies every row**.

- PKs let us address a specific row to select, update, delete or link to it.
- PKs are inherently **NOT NULL**.
- A table can have several unique keys, but **only one primary key**.
- A table can exist without a PK, but "99% of the time this is a bad idea".

### Composite keys

A [[Composite Primary Key|composite key]] is made of two or more columns whose **combination** is unique. In Sakila's `film_actor`, the pair `(actor_id, film_id)` is unique even though each column repeats.

### Natural vs surrogate keys

| | [[Natural Key]] | [[Surrogate Key]] |
|---|---|---|
| **Source** | The real data (username, SSN, shirt size) | Generated by the DB, usually `AUTO_INCREMENT` |
| **Coupling** | Tied to the business domain | Decoupled from the domain |
| **Stability** | Breaks when the domain's rules change | Stable |
| **Course advice** | **Avoid** | **Use throughout the course** |

> [!example] Why natural keys hurt
> Suppose `users` uses SSN as its PK, and then the company signs up non-US customers who have no SSN. The PK must change, and so must every FK pointing at it in `payments`, `orders`, `profiles` and `messages`.

## Foreign keys

A [[Foreign Key]] is a column in one table that identifies rows in another table (or the same table, for recursive relationships). It contains values from the other table's primary key.

- The table **with the FK** is the **child**.
- The table **with the PK** is the **parent**.

```text
staff (child)                          address (parent)
staff_id | first_name | address_id →   address_id | address
1        | Mike       | 3              3          | 23 Workhaven Lane
2        | Jon        | 4              4          | 1411 Lillydale Drive
```

## 1-to-many in a logical model

A 1-to-many relationship is implemented with **one PK-FK pair**, with the FK on the "many" side.

```text
person: person_id (PK), name, last_name
car:    car_id (PK), make, model, person_id (FK → person)
```

## Controlling cardinality with constraints

A [[Constraint]] is a rule about data that we define on a column and that the RDBMS always enforces.

- [[NOT NULL Constraint|NOT NULL]]: NULL is never allowed in the column.
- [[UNIQUE Constraint|UNIQUE]]: no two values in the column may be the same.

| Applied to the FK column | Effect on the relationship |
|---|---|
| The FK itself | A child has **at most 1** parent |
| `NOT NULL` | A child has **at least 1** parent |
| `UNIQUE` | A parent has **at most 1** child (making it 1-to-1) |

> [!warning] Exam favourite
> The **minimum number of children** a parent has **cannot** be set by the logical model. No constraint can force "every lecturer teaches at least one course". The 2026 Moed A asks exactly this in [[PP_08-Query Reading, ERD to Tables, Subqueries & DELETE vs TRUNCATE (2026 Moed A)|PP 08]].

## Summary

- Inheritance, recursive relationships, weak entities and associative entities are the richer conceptual patterns.
- Entities become tables and attributes become columns.
- A PK identifies a row. An FK links a child to a parent.
- Prefer surrogate keys over natural keys.
- FK alone means max 1 parent. Adding NOT NULL means min 1 parent. Adding UNIQUE means max 1 child.
