# Barriers to Innovation

> Part of: [[Entrepreneurship]] | Key concepts: [[Barriers to Innovation]], [[Radical Innovation]], [[Discovery Competences]], [[Organisational Structure]], [[Culture of Innovation]]

---

## 📚 Reading Overview

This reading is the companion source to [[Lec_06-Culture of Innovation]], which raised ==barriers to innovation== as one reason firms fail to innovate even when they *want* to. The framework follows **Sandberg & Aarikka-Stenroos (2014)**, whose review of why **[[Radical Innovation]]** so often stalls organises the obstacles into a clean two-level taxonomy.

The central question: *if innovation creates so much value, what actually stops firms from doing it?* The answer is that innovation is blocked on **two fronts at once** — the **outside world** the firm cannot fully control ([[External Barriers to Innovation|external barriers]]) and the **inside of the firm** it supposedly can ([[Internal Barriers to Innovation|internal barriers]]).

> [!info] Why the internal/external split matters ^def-barriers
> A ==barrier to innovation== is any factor that **prevents, delays, or weakens** a firm's innovative activity. Splitting them into **external** (in the environment / market / ecosystem) and **internal** (in the firm's own mindset, competences, resources, and structure) matters because it tells you *who owns the fix*. External barriers usually have to be **navigated, absorbed, or shaped**; internal barriers can — in principle — be **removed by management** directly. Radical innovation is hard precisely because it trips over barriers on **both** sides simultaneously.

## 🌍 External Barriers

External barriers live **outside the firm's boundary**. The firm can influence but rarely command them. They come in two flavours: resistance from **specific actors** the firm deals with, and a **restrictive macro-environment** that sets the backdrop.

### Resistance or Lack of Support from Specific Actor(s)

These are barriers thrown up by an identifiable player the innovator depends on.

| Barrier | What it is | Why it blocks innovation |
|---|---|---|
| ==Customer Resistance== | Customers resist due to changing needs and lack of experience with the new technology | If the buyer won't adopt, there is no market to justify the innovation — adoption inertia kills demand before the product proves itself |
| ==Unsupportive Government== | Restrictive regulations, laws, and standards imposed by the state | Regulation can make an innovation illegal, uneconomic, or slow to approve, raising the cost and risk of pursuing it |
| ==Shortage of External Finance== | Financial institutions are **conservative** toward innovative firms | Radical projects are uncertain and hard to collateralise, so lenders/investors ration capital exactly where it is most needed |
| ==Rivalry== | Competitors' advancements push **competing** technologies | A rival's momentum can lock in a standard or capture the market first, foreclosing the space you were aiming at |

> [!tip] The common thread
> Each of these is a **withheld "yes"** from someone the innovator needs — the customer, the regulator, the financier, or the market itself. Innovation is a *relationship* with these actors, and any one of them can veto it. Note how [[Porter's Five Forces]] echoes here: rivalry, buyer behaviour, and entry conditions are the *same* competitive forces, now read as **obstacles** rather than industry diagnostics.

### Restrictive Macro Environment

These are diffuse, systemic conditions — nobody "decides" them, but they still constrain.

| Barrier | What it is | Why it blocks innovation |
|---|---|---|
| ==Undeveloped Network & Ecosystem== | Resistance or **inertia** within the stakeholder network | Innovation rarely stands alone — it needs suppliers, complementors, and partners; a weak ecosystem starves it of support |
| ==Technological Turbulence== | Rapid, unpredictable change in the underlying technology | Committing to one technology becomes risky when the ground keeps shifting — today's bet may be obsolete tomorrow |
| ==Inappropriate Infrastructure== | Inadequate facilities and services | Without the roads, power, platforms, or standards to run on, even a good innovation cannot be implemented at scale |
| ==Restrictive Local Culture== | Cultural and religious beliefs that resist the new | Societal norms can reject an innovation outright regardless of its technical merit, shrinking or closing the market |

> [!warning] Turbulence cuts both ways
> [[Technological Turbulence]] is a **double-edged** barrier. It is often the *source* of radical-innovation opportunity (new tech opens new markets — the [[Blue Ocean Strategy|blue ocean]] logic from Class 3), yet it is *also* a barrier, because the same volatility makes any single commitment fragile. The firm must move fast enough to catch the wave but stay flexible enough not to drown when it turns. This is why turbulent industries reward **options and modularity** over big irreversible bets.

## 🏢 Internal Barriers

Internal barriers sit **inside the firm** — which is both the good news and the bad news. They are, in principle, within management's control, yet they are also **self-inflicted** and often invisible from the inside. Sandberg & Aarikka-Stenroos group them into four buckets.

### 1. Restrictive Mindset

> [!info] The status-quo trap
> Firms exhibit a ==fear of change== and a **preference for maintaining the status quo**, which stifles innovation before it starts. This is a *psychological* barrier: the organisation would rather protect what it has than risk what it might gain.

This is the deepest barrier because it is **upstream of all the others** — a firm that fears change will not build the competences, allocate the resources, or redesign the structure needed for innovation. It is the organisational-level opposite of the [[Psychological Safety]] and [[Intrinsic Motivation]] that Class 8 identified as *fuel* for creativity.

### 2. Lack of Competences

Even a willing firm can fail because it lacks the specific **capabilities** to carry an idea across the innovation pipeline. The competences map onto three sequential stages:

| Competence | Pipeline stage | The failure it represents |
|---|---|---|
| ==Discovery Competences== | **Identify** | Inability to spot and articulate opportunities for radical innovation — you never see the opening |
| ==Incubation Competences== | **Develop** | Inability to turn an opportunity into a viable business proposal — the idea never becomes a plan |
| ==Acceleration & Commercialisation Competences== | **Scale** | Inability to grow the innovation to a self-standing level — the plan never becomes a business |

> [!tip] A leaky pipeline, not a single gap
> Read these as a **funnel**: Discovery → Incubation → Acceleration/Commercialisation. A firm can be strong at one stage and fatally weak at another — great at *spotting* ideas (discovery) but unable to *scale* them (commercialisation), so promising innovations die on the launch pad. The barrier is wherever the pipeline is *thinnest*. This is why [[Discovery Competences]] alone are never enough.

### 3. Insufficient Resources

> [!info] The fuel problem
> Firms may lack ==internal finance==, ==skills==, or ==information== — the three resources radical innovation consumes. Without capital to fund it, people who can build it, or knowledge to direct it, ambition stays theoretical.

Note the pairing with the external barrier: **[[Shortage of External Finance]]** (outside) and **insufficient internal finance** (inside) can bite *at the same time*, leaving a firm squeezed from both directions — unable to fund innovation itself and unable to borrow to do so.

### 4. Unsupportive Organizational Structure

> [!info] When the wiring fights the work
> Traditional **hierarchical structures** impede the coordination and communication innovation requires. Rigid, top-down reporting slows the cross-unit information flow that novel projects depend on.

This links directly back to [[Lec_02-Organisation Structure]]: the [[Hierarchical Structure|hierarchical]] design that is efficient for routine execution becomes a **barrier** for innovation, whereas [[Flat Structure|flat]] and [[Matrix Structure|matrix]] designs are built for the lateral communication radical projects need. Structure is not neutral — it either **carries** or **chokes** innovation.

## 🗺️ The Full Taxonomy at a Glance

> [!example] Sandberg & Aarikka-Stenroos (2014) — barriers to radical innovation
>
> | Level | Category | Specific barriers |
> |---|---|---|
> | **External** | Resistance from specific actors | Customer resistance · Unsupportive government · Shortage of external finance · Rivalry |
> | **External** | Restrictive macro environment | Undeveloped network/ecosystem · Technological turbulence · Inappropriate infrastructure · Restrictive local culture |
> | **Internal** | Restrictive mindset | Fear of change · status-quo preference |
> | **Internal** | Lack of competences | Discovery · Incubation · Acceleration & commercialisation |
> | **Internal** | Insufficient resources | Internal finance · skills · information |
> | **Internal** | Unsupportive structure | Rigid hierarchy impeding coordination & communication |

> [!warning] Barriers compound — they don't just add
> The reason [[Radical Innovation]] is so rare is that these barriers **interact**. A restrictive mindset (internal) means the firm never builds discovery competences; missing competences mean it can't attract external finance; without finance it can't upgrade its structure — and meanwhile customers resist and the government regulates. The barriers form a **reinforcing web**, which is why removing one is usually not enough. Overcoming them takes coordinated action across *strategy, structure, culture, and leadership* — the four levers of [[Organizational Innovation]].

## ✅ Summary

- A ==barrier to innovation== is anything that prevents, delays, or weakens innovative activity; the core split is **external** (environment/market/ecosystem) vs. **internal** (mindset/competences/resources/structure), following **Sandberg & Aarikka-Stenroos (2014)**. ^summary-core
- **External — specific actors:** customer resistance, unsupportive government, shortage of external finance, rivalry. Each is a **withheld "yes"** from someone the innovator depends on.
- **External — macro environment:** undeveloped network/ecosystem, technological turbulence, inappropriate infrastructure, restrictive local culture. Diffuse conditions the firm must navigate, not command.
- **Internal — four buckets:** (1) *restrictive mindset* (fear of change) — the upstream barrier; (2) *lack of competences* across the discovery → incubation → acceleration/commercialisation pipeline; (3) *insufficient resources* (finance, skills, information); (4) *unsupportive structure* (rigid hierarchy chokes coordination).
- The **decisive insight**: barriers hit **both sides at once** and **compound** into a reinforcing web, which is why radical innovation is rare and why overcoming it needs coordinated action across all four levers of [[Organizational Innovation]] (strategy · structure · culture · leadership).
