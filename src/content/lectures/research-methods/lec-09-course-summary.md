## 3. Correlation is not causation: practice

Each of these is an association that **does not prove** the causal claim:

- Time on social media is associated with number of friends.
- More television goes with lower academic performance.
- Rich people are happier.
- People who work long hours complain more about health problems.
- Hours of exercise correlate positively with weight loss.

For each, ask: is there a [[Third Variable Problem|third variable]]? Could the [[Directionality Problem|direction]] run the other way?

## 5. Bias

[[Bias]] can enter through questionnaires, interviews and the sampling method.

| Where | Example | Why it is biased |
|---|---|---|
| **Question wording** | "Do you think the new technology is a complete waste of time and resources?" | Implies it is useless |
| **Interview question** | "Do you think your age will be a hindrance in keeping up?" | Implies age is a problem |
| **Sample** | A consumer survey that includes only urban residents | Not representative of rural consumers |
| **Non-response** | Unhappy employees are less likely to answer a job satisfaction survey | Results do not reflect all employees |

## 6. Identify the method and sampling

| Case | Data | Sampling |
|---|---|---|
| Random 500 adults from a city report annual income | Quantitative | Random |
| 100 random students report daily food calories | Quantitative | Random |
| 100 random high-blood-pressure patients, assigned to placebo or medication | Quantitative | Random |
| 100 recent luxury car buyers from one dealership who volunteered | Quantitative | Non-random |
| 100 people from a chronic illness database, in-depth interviews | Qualitative | Random |
| 20 random classrooms observed using a new teaching method | Qualitative | Random |
| 5 knowledgeable members of an indigenous community | Qualitative | Non-random |
| 10 people with a diagnosis who personally know the researcher | Qualitative | Non-random |
