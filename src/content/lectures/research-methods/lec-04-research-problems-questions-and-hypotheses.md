# Lec 04: Research Problems, Questions and Hypotheses

> Part of: [[Research Methods]]
> Key concepts: [[Research Problem]], [[Research Question]], [[Hypothesis]], [[Operational Definition]], [[Exploratory Research]], [[Descriptive Research]], [[Causal Research]]

> [!info] What this note covers
> The deck "Research Problem and Question": what makes a problem researchable, how to turn it into a hypothesis, and the three types of study.

## 1. Three criteria for a research problem

A [[Research Problem]] must:

1. **Express a relationship between two or more variables.** For example: do personality traits such as creativity, resilience and victimhood relate to entrepreneurial intentions and behavior? (Maaravi, Hameiri & Gur, 2022)
2. **Be clearly formulated as a [[Research Question]].**
3. **Be testable empirically.** If there is no practical way to measure the variables and the relations between them, the question is not scientific.

## 2. From question to hypothesis

A [[Hypothesis]] is a potential answer to the research question, stated **before** collecting data. Without one, the data has nothing to approve or disprove, and science progresses by strengthening or weakening hypotheses.

If the question is about a relationship between variables, the hypothesis is an approximate statement of that relationship's **existence or non-existence, direction, or order of magnitude**.

> [!example] Question to hypothesis
> **Question:** Is trait victimhood related to entrepreneurship?
> **Hypothesis (a statement):** Trait victimhood would be negatively associated with entrepreneurship.

### Examples from the deck

- Low self-efficacy will **mediate** the relationship between the tendency for victimhood and entrepreneurship.
- Job characteristics will **positively affect** job satisfaction (Maaravi et al., 2020).
- The effect of job characteristics on job satisfaction will be **mediated** by the relevant psychological states, such as responsibility for outcomes (Maaravi et al., 2020).

Mediation is explained in [[Lec_05-Variables, Correlation and Causation]].

### What makes a hypothesis scientific

- It can be tested empirically.
- That requires moving from **theoretical** definitions to [[Operational Definition|operational definitions]], which state how each concept will be measured.
- It stems from an existing theory.
- Testing it can disprove or confirm theories, which advances knowledge.

## 3. Types of study by their hypotheses

| Type | Purpose | Tests hypotheses? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Exploratory Research\|Exploratory]] | Get to know a phenomenon and formulate problems or hypotheses for later studies | No, it helps locate them | First look at a new market |
| [[Descriptive Research\|Descriptive]] | Accurately describe characteristics, the frequency of a phenomenon, or the strength of a relationship | Can describe relationships, which stay **correlational** | Brand attitude survey, election survey |
| [[Causal Research\|Causal]] | Test whether one variable causes another | Yes, with an **experimental design** | How does education influence entrepreneurship orientation? |

> [!important] To claim that A caused B
> 1. Show that **A occurred before B**.
> 2. **Disprove alternative explanations**, such as a third variable C.
> 3. Use an **experimental research design**.
