# Lec 06: Quantitative Data Collection

> Part of: [[Research Methods]]
> Key concepts: [[Quantitative Data]], [[Discrete Data]], [[Continuous Data]], [[Interval Scale]], [[Ratio Scale]], [[Population]], [[Sample]], [[Sampling Method]], [[Random Sampling]], [[Systematic Sampling]], [[Non-Probability Sampling]], [[Convenience Sampling]], [[Snowball Sampling]], [[Survey]], [[Questionnaire]], [[Experiment]], [[Experimental Group]], [[Control Group]]

> [!info] What this note covers
> The deck "Quantitative Data Collection": data types, sampling, and the two main collection methods, surveys and experiments.

## 1. Quantitative data

[[Quantitative Data]] is any information that can be measured or counted and represented by a number. It tells us **how much, how many, how often**: revenue, weight, height, age, hours worked from home, the cost of a house.

| Type | Definition | Examples from the deck |
|---|---|---|
| [[Discrete Data\|Discrete]] | Counted, not measured | Number of family members, cost of an item, age of a person |
| [[Continuous Data\|Continuous]] | Placed on a measurement scale | Weight, length of a building, GPA |

> [!warning] Slide check: cost and age
> The deck lists cost and age as discrete, and the summary lesson also lists age as continuous. **Family members is clearly discrete.** Age and money are usually treated as continuous in analysis. If an exam asks, go by how the variable is measured: age in whole years is counted, exact age is measured.

Continuous data has two scales:

- [[Interval Scale|Interval]]: equal distances between points, and values **can go below zero**. Temperature.
- [[Ratio Scale|Ratio]]: equal distances **and an absolute zero**. Weight, height.

The full four-level ladder, including nominal and ordinal, is in [[Lec_07-Questionnaire Design]].

## 2. Sampling

[[Sampling Method|Sampling]] isolates a group for analysis. Studying a whole [[Population]] is typically impossible, so we research a representative [[Sample]], which saves time and money.

| Family | How units are chosen | Methods |
|---|---|---|
| [[Random Sampling\|Random (probability)]] | Each unit has an **equal chance** of selection | Simple, [[Systematic Sampling\|systematic]] |
| [[Non-Probability Sampling\|Non-probability]] | Selected by the researcher or other subjective means | [[Convenience Sampling\|Convenience]], [[Snowball Sampling\|snowball]] |

## 3. Surveys and questionnaires

- A [[Survey]] is a **method**: it collects and analyzes data from a sample to learn about that group. Polls and evaluations are examples.
- A [[Questionnaire]] is a **set of questions** within a survey, open or closed. Examples: the Oxford Happiness Questionnaire, the Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale.

## 4. Experiments

An [[Experiment]] tests a hypothesis by **manipulating one or more independent variables and measuring their effect on the dependent variable**.

| Statement from the deck | [[Independent Variable\|IV]] | [[Dependent Variable\|DV]] |
|---|---|---|
| Stress increases the likelihood of a headache | Stress | Headache |
| Tiredness decreases time spent on a project | Tiredness | Time on project |
| Income earned is decreased by closing down stores | Closing stores | Income |
| Profit is increased by improved product quality | Product quality | Profit |

### Three settings

| Setting | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural** | Observe an environment **without manipulating** the IV | Observing how productive employees are |
| **Controlled** | Compare an [[Experimental Group]] (IV manipulated) with a [[Control Group]] (not manipulated). The groups are identical except for the IV. | Cutting break time between math problems for one group only |
| **Field** | An experiment in a **real-world** setting | Increasing office break time to see if productivity rises |

### The experimental method

1. Make an observation.
2. Formulate a hypothesis based on it.
3. Design and run an experiment to test it.
4. Analyze the results.
5. Accept or reject the hypothesis.
6. If rejected, formulate and test a new hypothesis.

## 5. Strengths and weaknesses

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **Extensive analysis:** a comprehensive picture through numbers | **Insufficient information:** the descriptive side of the data is lost |
| **Reduces personal bias** in participants' answers | **Misleading conclusions:** researcher bias from prior assumptions |
| **Objective results:** accurate, reliable and manageable to analyze | |

> [!summary] Quantitative at a glance
> - **Definition:** data that can be numerically analyzed into objective conclusions
> - **Collection:** online or in person, through surveys or experiments
> - **Used for:** revealing measurable data to formulate theories and facts
> - **Analysis:** statistical
> - **Population:** ideally a large, representative sample
> - **Question:** "Did you buy the Tesla stock today? 1) Yes, 2) No"
> - **Data:** 77% of participants bought the Tesla stock today
