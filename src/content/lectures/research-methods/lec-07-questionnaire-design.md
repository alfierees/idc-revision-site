# Lec 07: Questionnaire Design

> Part of: [[Research Methods]]
> Key concepts: [[Questionnaire]], [[Longitudinal Design]], [[Cross-Sectional Design]], [[Open-Ended Question]], [[Dichotomous Question]], [[Multiple-Choice Question]], [[Dummy Question]], [[Scaling Question]], [[Likert Scale]], [[Nominal Scale]], [[Ordinal Scale]], [[Interval Scale]], [[Ratio Scale]], [[Biased Question]], [[Pilot Study]]

> [!info] What this note covers
> The deck "Designing the Questionnaire" and the Moodle class exercise on building one.

## 1. What a questionnaire is

A [[Questionnaire]] is a set of questions that collects information about participants' **views, preferences, experiences, intentions and mental states**. It is a relatively cheap, quick and efficient way to gather a lot of information.

## 2. The eight design steps

There are no hard rules, but the deck gives this sequence:

1. Decide on the information required.
2. Define the population.
3. Select the method(s) of reaching participants.
4. Decide on question content.
5. Formulate the question wording.
6. Place the questions in a meaningful order and format.
7. Run a pilot.
8. Develop the final survey.

The questionnaire sits within the wider project: **research objective → data collection → sampling design → questionnaire design → data analysis**.

### Information required

Ask what information you need to answer your hypothesis. Draw on the literature, other studies, prior questionnaires and your own knowledge.

### Population and design

Define who you are interested in, considering age, culture, education and wealth, and whether they are current users or non-users.

| Design | How it works | Shows | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Longitudinal Design\|Longitudinal]] | Repeated sessions over months or years with **one group** | How a group **changes over time** | Twin studies |
| [[Cross-Sectional Design\|Cross-sectional]] | The same questionnaire to **a number of groups** | **Differences between groups** | Customer feedback from different countries |

How you reach people affects the form and wording of the questions: in person, in a group or focus setting, by email, or online.

## 3. Question content

Include a question only if it provides data related to the hypothesis. Mixing question types keeps respondents engaged.

| Type | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| [[Open-Ended Question\|Open-ended]] | Free-form answer, giving qualitative data | What were your experiences while living on campus? |
| [[Dichotomous Question\|Dichotomous]] | Closed yes/no | Did you live on campus: yes or no? |
| [[Multiple-Choice Question\|Multiple-choice]] | Select one answer from a list | Where did you live? 1) At home, 2) In an apartment, 3) With roommates... |
| **Pictorial** | Multiple choice with images, for quick, intuitive answers | Which picture represents your living experience? |
| [[Dummy Question\|"Dummy"]] | Disguises the purpose of the study | From a scale of 1-5, what is your view on the University? |
| [[Scaling Question\|Scaling]] | Based on the four measurement scales, typically a [[Likert Scale]] | How much did you enjoy living on campus? 1. Very unsatisfied ... 5. Very satisfied |

## 4. The four levels of measurement

Each level adds one property to the level below.

| Level | Named | Ordered | Equal intervals | Absolute zero | Example |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| [[Nominal Scale\|Nominal]] | Yes | | | | 1. Morning, 2. Afternoon, 3. Night |
| [[Ordinal Scale\|Ordinal]] | Yes | Yes | | | Likert scale |
| [[Interval Scale\|Interval]] | Yes | Yes | Yes | | Temperature |
| [[Ratio Scale\|Ratio]] | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Money |

> [!tip] Exam shortcut
> Numbers on a **nominal** scale are only labels. **Ordinal** gives rank but not distance, so a Likert satisfaction rating is ordinal. **Interval** has a meaningful distance but an arbitrary zero. **Ratio** has a true zero.

## 5. Wording

Poor wording makes people skip questions or answer wrongly.

| Rule | Bad example from the deck | The problem |
|---|---|---|
| Don't ask several things at once | What is your favorite type and form of ice cream? | Two questions in one |
| Use ordinary, direct, familiar words | Are you against the countermeasures stipulated on the consumption of gelato? | Ambiguous, unfamiliar language |
| Avoid assumptions and generalizations | From the following choices, which is your favorite way of eating ice cream? | Assumes you eat ice cream |
| Avoid [[Biased Question\|biased questions]] | As people do not like ice cream, why do you not enjoy ice cream? | Pushes the respondent to an answer |

## 6. Order and format

- Follow an inherent order and group related questions.
- Open with **easy, simple** questions to build rapport and engagement.
- Vary question types to break the monotony.

## 7. The pilot

A [[Pilot Study|pilot test]] tries the questionnaire on a representative focus group before release, under conditions similar or identical to the final version. Check:

- Do participants understand the questions?
- Do the questions serve their intended purpose?
- Are the phrasing and order right?
- Are there too many or too few questions?
- Are the instructions clear?

You can run more than one pilot. Keep modifying the questionnaire until it achieves the desired results, and check prior questionnaires you could adapt.

## 8. Class exercise: build and run one

From the Moodle activity "Exercise in creating questionnaires", in groups of 4 to 5:

1. **Research questions (10 min).** Pick a simple topic such as study habits or social media use. Write one main question and 3 to 5 sub-questions.
2. **Questionnaire (15 min).** About 10 concise, unbiased closed questions (Likert, multiple-choice, yes/no), plus demographics for segmentation.
3. **Distribution (25 min).** Use paper or an online tool such as Google Forms, aiming for about 10 responses from classmates.
4. **Preliminary analysis (15 min).** Tally results, look for patterns, and discuss surprises or inconsistencies.
