# Lec 03: Generative AI Skills and the PAIR Framework

> Part of: [[Research Methods]]
> Key concepts: [[PAIR Framework]], [[Fabricated Citation]]

> [!info] What this note covers
> The deck "Developing Key Generative AI Skills", adapted from the PAIR framework by Prof. Oguz A. Acar (King's College London). It opens with a line from Prof. Amnon Shashua (2023): AI will not replace humans, but humans who use AI will replace those who do not.

## 1. The PAIR framework

The [[PAIR Framework]] has four steps.

| Step | What you do | What to document |
|---|---|---|
| **P: Problem formulation** | Define the focus, scope, components and parameters of the issue. State your expectations of the tool precisely. | The problem and its constraints |
| **A: AI tool selection** | Explore, compare and evaluate tools, and pick the best fit. | Why you chose some tools and rejected others |
| **I: Interaction** | Use the tool in different ways, experiment with inputs and outputs, and judge what is accurate and useful. | Successful **and** unsuccessful prompts |
| **R: Reflection** | Assess how the tool helped or hindered your problem solving. | Challenges, how you overcame them, how to improve |

> [!warning] AI can be confidently wrong
> The deck names three failures to watch for in the Interaction step: **[[Fabricated Citation|fabricated citations and references]], false statistics and inaccurate content**. Check every source an AI gives you before citing it.

### Reflection questions

- What challenges did you meet with the AI tools, and how did you overcome them?
- In what ways did the tools enhance or hinder your problem solving?
- How can you improve your skills for future academic tasks?

## 2. Prompting techniques

| Technique | Pattern | Example from the deck |
|---|---|---|
| **Creating connections** | Give your context and material | "I am a university student doing a bachelor's degree course focusing on Algorithms. Please create a summary of the basic principles for me utilizing the course's syllabus." |
| **Conversing with a character** | Act as a role | "Pretend you are a sustainability consultant. Summarize the 3 significant problems arising from the attached text on waste disposal." |
| **Defining the task** | Create a specific output | "Please create 10-minute video summary for a lesson focusing on introduction to criminal law." |
| **Selecting a tone** | Use X tone of voice | Exam questions for PhD students, in a friendly tone. Also informal, playful, serious, persuasive. |
| **Using a style** | Write in a given style | A description written in Freud's style. Also academic, storytelling, jargon-filled. |
| **Limiting length** | Limit to X words | "Create an abstract for the attached article. Limit the abstract to 250 words." Also sentences, characters, paragraphs, lines. |
| **Comparative examples** | Take these examples into account | "Create a test based on the provided test examples that were used in a similar course." |

## 3. In-class exercise

Develop a marketing campaign for a product or service (or another domain of your choice) with AI tools, following the four steps:

1. **Problem.** Identify the product, do secondary research on its market, and set the campaign's objectives and target segment.
2. **AI tools.** Research tools for market research, content and visual design. Select the most suitable and justify what you chose and discarded.
3. **Interaction.** Generate content, give the AI feedback, experiment with prompts, assess the output critically and refine the campaign. Record which prompts worked and which missed.
4. **Reflection.** Write a report on what you learned and how you could improve your use of AI in marketing.
