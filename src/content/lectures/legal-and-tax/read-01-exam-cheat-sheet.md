## 1. [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|The Israeli Legal System]]

| Legal family | Features |
|---|---|
| **Common law** (England, US, Israel) | precedent-based; passive judge; flexible, dynamic |
| **Continental law** (France, Germany) | codified statutes; active judge; written law dominates |
| **Mixed jurisdiction** (Israel) | statutory law **plus** judicial precedent |

**Hierarchy of norms**

1. Basic Laws (quasi-constitutional, supreme)
2. Knesset statutes (primary legislation)
3. Secondary legislation (ministerial regulations, orders)
4. Administrative decisions and court judgments

- **Lex specialis:** the specific law overrides the general; a later law overrides an earlier one.

**Key principles**

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rule of law (formal) | publication, clarity, non-retroactivity, equal enforcement |
| Rule of law (substantive) | equality, protection of rights, justice |
| Separation of powers | legislature (Knesset), executive (government), independent judiciary |
| Non-retroactivity | laws do not generally apply backwards in time |
| *Erga omnes* | right valid against the whole world (property, IP) |
| *Pacta sunt servanda* | "agreements must be kept"; foundation of contract law |

> [!warning] Formal vs substantive rule of law: the sheet and Lec 1 disagree
> The sheet puts **publication, clarity and non-retroactivity under "formal"** and equality and rights under "substantive". [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 1]] assigns them the other way round (formal = enforcement and equality before the law; substantive = publication, clarity, non-retroactivity). Both agree on the list of ingredients; they differ only on the labels. Lec 1 matches the lecturer's own slide (Session 1, slide 38), so it's the sheet that has them swapped. If an exam asks for the label, use the Lec 1 wording.

**Sources of law**

| Source | Note |
|---|---|
| Legislation | Knesset laws and subsidiary regulations |
| Precedent | Supreme Court decisions bind lower courts |
| *Ratio decidendi* | the binding reasoning core of a judgment |
| *Obiter dictum* | non-binding remarks "in passing" |
| Legal custom | long-standing practice accepted as binding |

**Hard vs soft law**

| | Examples | Force |
|---|---|---|
| Hard law | statutes, regulations, court rulings, treaties | legally binding |
| Soft law | codes of conduct, contracts, guidelines, best practice | voluntary / agreed |

**Types of proceedings:** civil (private disputes; damages or injunction), criminal (state v individual; punishment), administrative (citizen challenges government authority).

> [!note] Beyond the lecture note
> **Legal custom as a source of law**, the **civil / criminal / administrative** split, and the sheet's 4-tier hierarchy wording ("Basic Laws" first) are **not in Lec 1**. Lec 1's hierarchy runs Constitution, Laws, Regulations, Orders, Private law. Lec 1 also treats **contracts as soft law**, and the sheet adds codes of conduct and guidelines.

## 2. [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Startup Lifecycle & Key Concepts]]

*(Lifecycle stages: [[Lec_01-Introduction & the Israeli Legal System|Lec 1]]; entity types: [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 3]].)*

**Development stages**

| Stage | Legal to-do |
|---|---|
| Pre-incorporation | founders' agreement, NDAs, protect IP, avoid employer conflicts |
| Incorporation | register company, Articles of Association, transfer IP to the company, set up ESOP and bank account |
| Investment rounds | term sheet, investment agreement, amend Articles, due diligence, regulatory compliance |
| Exit | M&A, secondary sale or IPO; tax planning; investor exit rights |

**Corporate structures**

| Structure | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Sole proprietor | 1 owner; pass-through tax; **no** separate legal personality; unlimited personal liability |
| Partnership | 2+ persons; joint and several liability; each partner is an agent; **max 20 partners**; fiduciary duty between partners |
| Private Ltd | most common for startups; separate legal personality; limited liability; can issue shares and options; flexible governance |
| Public corporation | shares traded after IPO; strict disclosure; external directors required |
| Association | non-profit; tax benefits under **Section 46** |
| Cooperative | democratic (**1 member, 1 vote**); profits by participation |
| Statutory corporation | created by statute; semi-autonomous; no private owner |

**Fundraising stages:** Bootstrapping (founders) → Pre-seed (incubators, accelerators, angels; idea stage) → Seed (MVP, market validation; angels and seed funds) → Series A (team, VC entry, product-market fit) → Series B (scale, new markets) → Series C+ (global expansion, institutions, pre-IPO) → IPO.

**Five key startup legal risks**

| Risk | Consequence |
|---|---|
| No founders' agreement | disputes over equity, roles, decisions |
| No NDA | loss of trade secrets and IP protection |
| Improper IP assignment | IP stays with the individual, not the company |
| Privacy violations | GDPR fines, reputational damage |
| Improper open-source use | forced disclosure of proprietary code |

> [!note] Beyond the lecture note
> "Fiduciary duty between partners" is **not stated in Lec 3**. The "max 20 partners" rule matches Lec 3 (which adds "except professional partnerships"). The sheet's five risks overlap Lec 2's five core risks on four points (founders' agreement, NDA, privacy, open source). Lec 2's fifth is **improper corporate structure**; the sheet instead lists **improper IP assignment** (which Lec 1 and Lec 2 do stress separately).

## 3. [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Key Business Terms & Agreements]]

**Agreements**

| Agreement | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Founders' agreement** | defines vision, values, roles, share distribution, decision-making, dispute resolution; protects IP, prevents unilateral action, sets exit mechanisms. Must include: share ownership, vesting, non-compete, voting rights, approval thresholds |
| **NDA** | confidentiality. Essential clauses: definition of confidential info, purpose limitation, disclosure restrictions, remedies. A **signed altered NDA** without review is legally binding |
| **Investment agreement** | valuation, share allocation, voting, veto, preemptive, exit and information rights |
| **Non-compete** | generally **unenforceable in Israel** except to protect genuine trade secrets or with special compensation |
| **SLA** | service levels, uptime (e.g. 99.9%), response times, remedies |
| **Licence agreement** | permission to use IP on defined conditions |
| **EULA** | end-user terms for software |
| **ToS / T&C** | **not** legally required, strongly recommended |
| **Privacy policy** | **legally required** under GDPR (what is collected, stored, used) |

**Key financial terms**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Vesting | rights granted gradually over time |
| Cliff | minimum period before **any** vesting (typically 1 year) |
| ESOP | employee equity plan aligning employee and company interests |
| Anti-dilution | protects investors when shares are issued at a lower price; adjusts the conversion price |
| Due diligence | investor review of finances, contracts, IP, clients |
| Angel | individual investing personal wealth early |
| VC | fund investing institutional money; active in management |
| Pre-money | value before the investment |
| Post-money | pre-money + investment |
| Cap table | ownership percentages of all shareholders |

**Valuation methods:** multiple-based (profit × industry multiple); comparison (similar companies); revenue-based (revenue × multiple; fast-growing startups); cost / entry barrier (cost to replicate); user-based (users × value per user).

**Cloud models:** **IaaS** (virtual servers, storage, networks; user controls OS and apps; AWS, GCP), **PaaS** (development platform, no server management; Google App Engine), **SaaS** (software by subscription, no installation; Asana, Google Workspace).

> [!note] Matches Lec 2
> Non-compete (*AES v. Saar*), the privacy policy / ToS required-vs-advisable split, the SLA 99.9% example, the five valuation methods and the investment-agreement clauses all match [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 2]]. Lec 2's own worked example gives "Ownership % = investment / post-money".

## 4. [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Contract Law]]

A contract is a legally binding agreement between 2+ parties; a promise or set of promises enforceable by law. Sources: Roman law (*pacta sunt servanda*), common law, Jewish law.

**Fundamental principles**

| Provision | Rule |
|---|---|
| **s.24** | freedom of contract: any content the parties choose; **not absolute** |
| **s.30** | contract **void** if illegal, immoral or contrary to public policy |
| **s.39** | good faith (see warning below) |
| Cogent provisions | cannot be contradicted by agreement (sanctity of life, access to courts) |

> [!warning] Good faith: s.39 versus s.12
> The sheet says s.39 good faith applies to **both negotiation and performance**. In [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 4]] the **negotiation** duty is **s.12** (liability for bad-faith negotiation before a contract forms), and s.39 is the good-faith gap-filler / performance provision. Cite s.12 for pre-contract conduct.

**Conditions for a valid contract**

| Condition | Rule |
|---|---|
| Offer and acceptance | offer = specific terms; acceptance = unconditional agreement |
| *Gmirat da'at* (serious intention) | both parties intend to be bound; **objective** test, assessed at the moment of signing |
| Legal capacity | not minors (under 18) or the mentally impaired (ss.3-4, s.8 Legal Capacity Law) |
| Legality of purpose | must not violate law or public policy |
| *Mesuyamut* (certainty) | specific enough; minimal essentials for real estate: **delivery date, price, nature of transaction** |
| Consideration | **NOT required** in Israeli law (unlike common law) |

**Contract types:** an **obligatory** transaction is future-facing and carries risk (e.g. buying an off-plan apartment); a **real** transaction is an immediate exchange with no risk (spot).

**Writing requirement:** real-estate transactions need writing (**s.8 Land Law**), otherwise invalid; this can be softened in exceptional circumstances by good faith (*Kalmer v Gai*).

**Interpretation (s.25)**

| Section | Rule |
|---|---|
| s.25(a) | interpret by the parties' mutual intent, from the contract and surrounding circumstances |
| s.25(b) | prefer the interpretation that **upholds** validity over one that voids it |
| s.25(b1) | drafter had an advantage: interpret **against the drafter** (*contra proferentem*) |
| s.25(c) | common terms by their customary meaning |

Method: circumstances → language → back to circumstances (**pendulum model**, *Apropim*).

> [!warning] s.25(a) is incomplete on the sheet
> After the **2011 amendment**, [[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 4]] adds that **if the intent is explicitly implied from the language, the contract is interpreted according to its language**. The sheet omits this proviso, which pulls back towards the text.

**Gap filling (s.26)**

- Missing terms are filled by custom between the parties; absent that, by common usage in similar contracts.
- Five mechanisms: (1) agreed mechanism, (2) dispositive legal provision (**s.46**: reasonable price), (3) optimal performance, (4) custom / usage, (5) good faith. This matches Lec 4.

**Market overt (s.34, Sale Law)**

| Sheet says (3 conditions) | Lec 4 says (4 conditions) |
|---|---|
| good faith (subjective) | good faith (subjective) |
| paid value | for value |
| bought from the seller in the ordinary course of their business | good faith at the time of transfer |
| | bought from someone who appeared to be the seller, in the ordinary course of that seller's business |

> [!warning] Market overt: 3 conditions on the sheet, 4 in Lec 4
> The sheet merges Lec 4's conditions. For an exam answer, give all four ([[Lec_04-Contract Law|Lec 4]], *Knaan* flea-market painting). The sheet's three are not wrong, just compressed.

## 5. [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Company Law]]

**Formation:** choose a type → prepare documents (application, Articles, director declarations, fee) → register with the Registrar of Companies.

**Articles of Association**

| Item | Section |
|---|---|
| Mandatory content: name, objectives, registered share capital, limitation of liability | s.18 |
| Optional content: number of directors, governance rules, share transfer conditions | s.19 |
| Articles are a **contract** between company and shareholders, and among shareholders | s.15 |

**Separate legal personality:** **s.4**, the company is a separate legal entity; the corporate veil separates company debts from personal assets. **Limited liability:** shareholders lose at most their investment.

**Piercing the corporate veil (s.6)** where: (1) the company is used for fraud or to oppress creditors; (2) harm to the company's purpose by unreasonable risk to creditors; (3) shareholder awareness or wilful blindness to improper conduct.

**Governance structure**

| Organ | Powers |
|---|---|
| **Shareholders** (general meeting, **s.57**) | appoint the board; approve interested-party transactions; increase or decrease share capital; appoint the auditor |
| **Board** (**s.92**) | strategy and supervision; appoint and dismiss the CEO; approve financial statements |
| **CEO** | day-to-day management, delegated by the board |

**Board powers that cannot be delegated:** appoint or dismiss CEO, approve financial statements, strategy, dividends, major capital changes, IPO.

**Agency problem (three types):** shareholders v management (perks, empire building); **majority v minority** (tunnelling, self-dealing); **shareholders v creditors** (excessive risk). Officers owe a **duty of loyalty** (avoid conflicts) and a **duty of care** (reasonable skill and diligence).

**Minority rights:** information, vote, derivative suit, petition for oppression, propose agenda items.

**Thin capitalisation / subordination:** a company financed mainly by shareholder loans may have that debt **subordinated** so shareholders are paid **last** (*Northern Chicks*, s.6(c)).

Credit hierarchy: (1) secured creditors → (2) priority creditors (employees, taxes) → (3) unsecured creditors → shareholders.

**Securities**

| Instrument | Key points |
|---|---|
| Shares | ownership; voting **s.188**; dividends **s.190** (only from sufficient profits and without endangering solvency); liquidation rights |
| Bonds | loan from investor; principal + interest; holder is a **creditor, not an owner**; tradable, convertible or collateral-backed |
| Options | right (not obligation) to buy or sell at a pre-agreed strike by expiry; a **derivative** |

> [!note] Section numbers checked
> I checked **s.4, 6, 15, 18, 19, 57, 92, 188, 190**, the non-delegable board powers, the three types of agency problem, the credit hierarchy and the veil-piercing grounds against [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 3]]. All match. Lec 3 additionally cites **s.192(a)** for officer duties and **ss.239-240** for external directors; the sheet gives no section for either.

## 6. [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Digital Regulation & Privacy]]

**GDPR overview**

- Adopted **April 2016**; in force **25 May 2018**.
- **Extraterritorial scope (Art. 3):** applies to all organisations in the EU **or** processing data of EU residents.

**Key roles**

| Role | Article | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Controller | 24 | determines purposes and means; responsible for compliance |
| Processor | 28 | processes on the controller's behalf, bound by instructions |
| DPO | 37-39 | mandatory in some organisations; monitors compliance; independent |

**Categories of PII:** basic (name, email, phone, address, government IDs); online (IP, cookies, browsing history, device, location); financial (bank accounts, cards, employment); **sensitive / special categories** (biometric, health, genetic, geolocation), which need higher protection.

**Article 5 principles:** purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality, accountability.

**Lawful bases (Art. 6), six:** consent; necessity for a contract; legal obligation; vital interests; public interest; legitimate interest.

**Privacy by design and default (Art. 25):** build privacy in from the start; the default setting must be the most privacy-protective.

**Data subject rights (Arts. 12-23):** access, rectification, erasure (right to be forgotten), data portability, object, restriction of processing.

> [!warning] Data subject rights: 6 on the sheet, 8 in Lec 7
> [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 7]] lists **eight** rights. The sheet omits the **right to be informed** and the **rights re. automated decision-making and profiling (Art. 22)**. If asked "list the data subject rights", use Lec 7's eight.

**International transfers:** SCC (Art. 46, pre-approved contract clauses), BCR (Art. 47, intra-group), **adequacy decision** (Art. 45).

> [!warning] Transfers: sheet is narrower and adds a detail
> Lec 7 lists **four** mechanisms (the three above plus **Codes of Conduct**, Arts. 40/46) and says **Israel itself relies on an adequacy decision**, with Amendment 13 to the Privacy Protection Law keeping Israel aligned. The sheet's example, the **EU-US Data Privacy Framework (July 2023)**, is **not in Lec 7**.

**Compliance tools:** DPIA (Art. 35, high-risk processing), RoPA (Art. 30), DSAR, cookie consent, breach notification, DPO appointment.

**EU regulatory framework**

| Law | Role |
|---|---|
| EU AI Act | risk-based: Unacceptable (banned) → High (strict requirements) → Limited (transparency) → Minimal (free use) |
| DSA | regulates online platforms; liability for illegal content |
| DMA | regulates "gatekeepers"; prohibits anti-competitive practices |

> [!warning] AI Act tiers: "Limited" is beyond Lec 7
> [[Lec_07-Digital Regulation|Lec 7]] describes the risk-based approach as **prohibited, high-risk, minimal** only. The **"Limited risk (transparency)" tier** on the sheet is not in the lecture note, so verify it before relying on it. The Lec 7 note also gives the AI Act timeline (proposal April 2021, published July 2024) and the Provider / Deployer roles, which the sheet omits.

> [!note] Article numbers checked
> Arts. 3, 5, 6, 12-23, 24, 25, 28, 30, 35, 37-39, 45, 46, 47 and the GDPR dates all match Lec 7.

## 7. [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Intellectual Property]]

**Property-rights foundation:** an **absolute right** (ownership) gives direct control and is valid against the world (*erga omnes*); a **limited right** (lease, lien, licence) is valid only within its scope. A **contractual right** binds only the parties; an **IP right** binds everyone.

| Regime | Protects | Registration | Duration | Conditions / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Copyright** | literary, musical, dramatic, artistic works (books, blogs, code, NFTs, UI/UX), software, games | **No**; automatic on creation | life of author + 70 years | rights: reproduction, distribution, public performance, derivative works, **moral rights** (attribution, integrity) |
| **Patent** | technological inventions with industrial application | **Yes** | **20 years from filing**; maintenance fees | (1) novelty, (2) non-obviousness / inventive step, (3) industrial applicability |
| **Trade secret** | secret information with commercial value | **No** | unlimited while secret | lost on disclosure; needs active measures (NDAs, access controls) |
| **Trademark** | names and signs identifying goods and services | **Yes** | (not stated on sheet) | (1) registrability: unique, not purely descriptive; (2) distinctiveness; (3) actual or intended use |
| **Industrial design** | unique visual / aesthetic features of products (furniture, jewellery, clothing, packaging) | **Yes** | (not stated) | appearance, **not** function |

**Trademark refusals:** misleading signs, similarity to existing marks, official symbols, purely descriptive terms (e.g. "Leather Shoes" for shoes). Registration gives exclusivity in the registered class and the right to sue infringers.

**IP in the AI era**

| Jurisdiction | Position |
|---|---|
| US | only natural persons hold copyright; AI works not automatically protected; "fair use" assessed for training data |
| EU | transparency about training data; risk-management approach (AI Act) |
| Israel | no ruling yet recognising copyright in AI-generated content |

Also: human creativity is required; **only natural persons can be inventors**, so AI cannot be a named inventor.

**IP licensing and startups:** an IP licence lets another use the IP while the owner keeps ownership; a software licence grants permission on conditions; an **open-source licence** gives free use with conditions and risks "contaminating" proprietary code; **founders must assign all IP to the company** by written agreement.

> [!warning] Copyright duration and moral rights are beyond Lec 5
> **"Life of author + 70 years"** and **moral rights** do not appear in [[Lec_05-Intellectual Property|Lec 5]], which says only "life + decades" for copyright and gives no trademark or design duration. The 70-year figure is the standard rule but is **not taught in the course notes**, so treat it as sheet-only. The patent (20 years from filing), the three patent conditions, the three trademark conditions, the trade-secret and the AI-era points all match Lec 5.

## 8. [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Tax Aspects]]

*(Entity-level taxation, i.e. pass-through v double taxation, is in [[Lec_03-Company Law & Corporate Structures|Lec 3]].)*

**Section 102, Income Tax Ordinance** (special regime for employee equity)

| Track | Tax on the employee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Capital Gain Track** (with trustee) | reduced capital-gains rate | most common for startups; **24-month holding period**; shares held by a trustee |
| **Ordinary Income Track** | taxed as regular income | less favourable for the employee |
| **Non-Trustee Track** | ordinary income | no trustee; less tax-advantaged |

**ESOP legal nature:** an option is a contractual right to **purchase** shares in future; not immediate ownership and not an obligation. Vesting is typically 4 years with a 1-year cliff (leave before the cliff = no equity). The **strike price** is pre-agreed; the company valuation at grant sets it and is critical for future tax exposure.

**Business structure and taxation**

| Structure | Tax |
|---|---|
| Sole proprietor | pass-through; taxed once at personal level |
| Corporation | **double taxation**: corporate tax, then dividend tax on distribution |
| Association | tax-exempt on qualifying activities; donations get a Section 46 deduction |

Double taxation can be mitigated by tax credits, dividend exemptions or choosing pass-through structures. **Equity v loan:** equity needs no repayment and brings dilution; a loan requires repayment of principal and interest, with no dilution, and the lender is a creditor.

> [!warning] Strike price: the sheet is inconsistent with itself
> Topic 8 says the strike price is "usually low at grant date", while topic 9 says it is "typically set at fair market value at grant date". [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 6]] says only that the exercise price is **fixed at the grant date** (and that valuation, e.g. a 409A-style valuation, matters). Use "fixed at grant, typically fair market value".

> [!note] Rate details live in the next section
> The sheet states the Capital Gain Track rate only in topic 9 (25%). Lec 6 adds what the sheet omits: **selling before 24 months** sends the gain to the marginal rate (up to about 59%), and **public v private** companies are treated differently under the Capital Gain Track.

## 9. [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)]]

**Why equity awards:** long-term incentive aligning investors, company and employees; alternative or supplement to cash for cash-constrained startups; attracts and retains talent by offering upside.

**Core definitions**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Option | right (not obligation) to buy a fixed number of shares at a fixed exercise price within a set period |
| ESOP | company-adopted framework with the general terms for all grants |
| Grant / option agreement | individual contract with one employee |
| Exercise / strike price | pre-agreed price per share paid on exercise |
| Share | ordinary share issued on exercise, equal to all regular shares including dividends |
| Vesting schedule | options become exercisable in tranches (typically 4 years) |
| Cliff | minimum period (usually 1 year) before any option vests |
| Expiration date | exercise deadline (typically **10 years** from grant); unexercised options expire worthless |

**Termination scenarios**

| Scenario | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Unvested options | expire immediately on any termination |
| Without cause | vested options exercisable within **3 months** |
| Death or disability | vested options exercisable within **12 months** |
| **For cause** (fraud, wilful misconduct) | **all** options expire immediately |

**Section 102 tax tracks**

| Track | Employee tax | Company deduction | Trustee |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capital Gain** (most common) | **25%** capital-gains rate; 24-month holding period | **No** | Yes |
| **Ordinary Income** | up to **59%** | **Yes** | Yes |
| **Non-Trustee** | ordinary income | **No** (rarely chosen) | No |

- Section 102 applies to **employees and officers only**, **not** controlling shareholders, consultants or service providers.
- **Section 3(i)** covers controlling shareholders, consultants and service providers: the tax event is **on exercise** and the gain is ordinary income, with no capital-gains benefit.

**Implementation timeline:** board adopts the ESOP → board approves the trustee → trustee submits to the tax authority. **Grants may start 30 days after submission; board approval deposited with the trustee within 45 days; signed agreements within 90 days.**

> [!note] Numbers checked against Lec 6
> **25%, up to about 59%, 24 months, 3 months, 12 months, 10 years, 30 / 45 / 90 days**, the track-by-track deduction pattern, and the s.102 v s.3(i) scope all **match** [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 6]]. Lec 6 states the 24 months run **from the grant date**, and adds that vested options must be exercised within about 90 days of leaving on good terms.

> [!warning] A trap in the sheet's wording
> The sheet says "Without cause: vested options exercisable within 3 months" and, separately, "For cause: ALL options expire", which could be read as covering only unvested options. Lec 6 is explicit that a for-cause dismissal wipes out **even vested options**.

## 10. [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Capitalization Tables]]

> [!info] Taught in the cap-table lecture
> This topic is covered by [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Lec 8]] (cap tables). [[Lec_06-Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP)|Lec 6]] only touches the cap table in passing (the option pool is part of it), and [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 2]] gives the pre/post-money definitions and the ownership formula.

**What a cap table is:** a spreadsheet of the company's full share-capital structure (who owns what, how much, at what price), updated for every round, option grant, conversion or transfer. **Ownership % = shares held / total outstanding shares.**

**Issued v fully diluted capital**

| Basis | Includes |
|---|---|
| Issued share capital | shares actually issued and held today: founders, investors, exercised employees |
| **Fully diluted** | issued shares **plus** unexercised options (granted and ungranted pool), warrants and convertible notes |

Investors and lawyers always negotiate on a **fully diluted** basis.

**Pre-money v post-money**

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Post-money | pre-money + investment amount |
| Price per share | pre-money / pre-round fully diluted shares |
| Investor ownership % | investment amount / post-money |

**Worked example from the sheet:** an investor offers **USD 2.5M for 25%** on a fully diluted basis.

- Post-money = 2.5M / 0.25 = **USD 10M**
- Pre-money = 10M − 2.5M = **USD 7.5M**
- Price per share = 7.5M / pre-round (fully diluted) shares

**Option pool:** a reserve of unissued shares for future employee grants; often created **pre-money** as an investor condition, so it dilutes the **founders, not the incoming investor**.

**Dilution:** when new shares are issued, existing holders' percentage falls even though their share count is unchanged. The aim is a smaller slice of a bigger pie, worth more. Every funding round, option grant or note conversion dilutes.

**Granting equity: the formula.** You cannot just take X% of the current shares, because the target percentage is of the **new, larger** total:

$$\text{New shares} = \frac{\text{Current shares} \times \text{Target}\%}{1 - \text{Target}\%}$$

The denominator $(1-\text{Target}\%)$ makes the grant a correct percentage of the post-issuance total.

**Anti-dilution protection** (for **down rounds**, i.e. new funding at a lower valuation than before):

| Type | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Full-ratchet** | most aggressive: the investor's price resets to the new (lowest) price |
| **Broad-based weighted average** | more founder-friendly: adjusts the conversion price using a weighted average across all shares |

> [!warning] Check against Lec 8
> The **full-ratchet / broad-based weighted-average** distinction, **warrants, convertible notes**, and the **post-issuance grant formula** are **not in Lec 1-7**; [[Lec_08-Capitalization Table Basics|Lec 8]] has the grant formula and the fully diluted count (options, warrants, convertible notes) and only names the two anti-dilution mechanisms. [[Lec_02-Legal Entrepreneurship — Fundamental Concepts|Lec 2]] defines anti-dilution only generally (it lowers the conversion price in a down round). Check these against the cap-table lecture and its practice questions. Also note Lec 2's own example rounds USD 1M into 10M + 1M to about 9% ("about 10% as framed in the slide"), while the sheet's example uses the exact post-money.
