## 📚 Course Context

> [!info] What this course is about
> This course gives a founder the **legal literacy** to navigate a venture from idea to exit. It is not about turning you into a lawyer — it is about knowing *which legal questions matter at each stage*, *when to bring in a lawyer*, and *how legal structure protects (or destroys) value*. The lens is consistently entrepreneurial: law as a tool for **managing risk**.

## 🚀 The Startup Lifecycle: Legal Considerations at Each Stage

The central organising idea of the session: a startup passes through distinct phases, and **each phase raises its own legal priorities**. The recurring question a founder should ask is *"what legal aspect is most important to address right now?"* Getting the legal work right early is cheap; fixing it later (during diligence or an exit) is expensive or impossible.

### The four stages

| Stage | Core legal priorities | Why it matters |
|-------|----------------------|----------------|
| **Pre-Incorporation** | Founders' Agreement (roles, shares, vesting, IP ownership); NDAs with anyone exposed to confidential info; protect IP and avoid conflicts with current employers | Sets the rules between founders *before* money or ego is at stake. ==Vesting== prevents a departing co-founder from walking away with dead equity. |
| **Incorporation** | Register the company; draft ==Articles of Association== (company bylaws); transfer all pre-existing IP into the company; set up contracts, bank account, and option plan (ESOP) | The company becomes a separate legal person. IP **must** sit inside the company, not with individuals, or investors won't fund it. |
| **Investment Rounds** | Negotiate ==Term Sheet== and Investment Agreement; amend Articles for investor rights (liquidation preference, anti-dilution); keep clean legal/financial documentation for due diligence; ensure sector compliance (fintech, medtech, AI, privacy) | Investors buy into a *clean* company. Messy cap tables, missing IP assignments, or weak compliance kill or reprice rounds. |
| **Exit** | M&A or IPO agreements, warranties, and indemnities; plan taxes for founders and investors; review IP, contracts, and compliance before closing | The exit is where every earlier shortcut surfaces. ==Warranties== and ==indemnities== allocate the risk of hidden problems between buyer and seller. |

> [!tip] The intuition: legal debt compounds
> Just like technical debt, **legal shortcuts accrue interest.** A founders' agreement skipped in week one becomes a lawsuit in year three; an IP assignment never signed becomes a deal-breaker in diligence. The lifecycle framing exists so you front-load the cheap, high-leverage legal work.

> [!warning] Common founder mistakes
> - Building the product *inside a current employer's time/resources* → the employer may own the IP.
> - Leaving IP in a founder's personal name after incorporation.
> - No vesting → a co-founder leaves after two months but keeps 33% forever.
> - Verbal-only founder arrangements → unenforceable when the relationship sours.

## 💰 Funding Stages — Glossary

Founders raise money in escalating rounds; each round has a characteristic purpose, investor type, and cheque size. Knowing the vocabulary is essential because **the legal documents change with the round**.

> [!info] The financing ladder
> | Stage | Purpose | Typical funders |
> |-------|---------|-----------------|
> | **Bootstrapping** | Founders fund the company from personal resources (and friends & family) | Founders, family, friends |
> | **Pre-Seed** | Early development of the idea and start of the product | Incubators, accelerators, angels, small VC |
> | **Seed** | Finance the MVP and obtain market/concept validation | Angels, seed funds (relatively small) |
> | **Round A** | Expand the team, develop the product, begin acquiring customers | Venture capital funds |
> | **Round B** | Scale operations, enter new markets | Larger VC rounds |
> | **Round C and beyond** | Major expansion, global entry, acquiring other companies | Large funds, institutional investors |
> | **IPO** | Offer shares to the public on a stock exchange | Public markets |

> [!tip] Why each round is bigger and "later money is more expensive to the buyer, cheaper to the founder"
> As the company de-risks (working product, real customers), each share is worth more, so founders sell **less equity per dollar raised**. Early money is the most dilutive because the risk is highest. The ==IPO== is both a fundraising event and a **liquidity event** — it lets early investors realise (cash out) their returns.

## ⚖️ Soft Law vs Hard Law

A foundational distinction for thinking about *where* legal risk comes from. The "practical-legal world" rests on two foundations:

| | **Soft Law** | **Hard Law** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Aspects we *choose* to regulate through legal tools | Aspects we are *obligated* to regulate by a binding framework |
| **Examples** | Contracts, founders' agreements, NDAs | Statutes, regulations, court rulings |
| **Source of force** | Mutual agreement between parties | The state / sovereign authority |
| **Founder's role** | We design it to create clear arrangements where we have an interest | We must comply; we cannot opt out |

> [!warning] Hard law always prevails over soft law
> No matter how carefully you draft a contract (soft law), a contractual term that conflicts with a statute or regulation (hard law) is **void to that extent**. You can contract *around* default rules, but never *against* mandatory ones. In the entrepreneurial world, risk must be managed against **both** foundations simultaneously.

## 🌐 The New Digital Age — Why Law Touches Everything

> [!info] How does all of this relate to founders?
> Modern ventures sit at the intersection of several legal layers at once: **regulation, human rights, contracts, corporations, intellectual property, and ethics**. A digital product can trigger privacy law, AI regulation, consumer-protection rules, and cybersecurity obligations — all before it has a single paying customer.

### What *is* law? The interdisciplinary view

Law is not a sealed-off technical field; it is a **layer that helps us interpret phenomena across other domains** — politics, economics, technology, and philosophy. This branch of study (the theory of what law *is* and where it comes from) is called ==jurisprudence==.

> [!tip] The single most useful principle in public law
> **A governmental authority may act only if the law *permits* it; a citizen is free to act unless the law *prohibits* it.**
> This asymmetry is the engine of the rule of law: the state is *boxed in* by positive authority, while private actors (including founders) enjoy residual freedom. It is why a founder can do anything not forbidden, but a regulator can only do what a statute empowers.

## 🏛️ The Israeli Legal System

### Hierarchy of norms

Israeli law is organised as a **hierarchy of legal sources**, each produced by a different authority and each subordinate to the one above it:

```
Constitution  ── Constituent authority (Knesset acting as constituent power)
   │
  Laws         ── Knesset (the legislature)
   │
Regulations    ── Executive authority (government ministries)
   │
  Orders        ── Executive / administrative bodies
   │
Private Law     ── The general public (contracts between private parties)
```

> [!info] Reading the hierarchy
> Higher norms **authorise and constrain** lower ones. Regulations must have a statutory anchor (a parent law that empowers the minister to make them); private contracts must sit within the bounds set by statute and regulation. This is the structural reason *hard law prevails over soft law*.

### A mixed legal system

> [!info] Israel = a "mixed jurisdiction"
> Israeli law is **not** purely one tradition. It draws from two great legal families and belongs fully to neither:
>
> - **Common Law (England):** inherited via §46 of the King's (Palestine) Order in Council — rules of evidence, agency, the adversarial procedure.
> - **Continental / Civil Law (Roman–Germanic):** the principle of **good faith** and the drive toward **codification**.
>
> Because it combines both, Israel is classed — alongside **Scotland** (English + French) and **Louisiana** (French + American) — as a **mixed jurisdiction**.

### Common Law vs Continental Law

The two families differ in *where legal authority lives* and *who drives the trial*. This is the central comparison of the session:

| Dimension | **Common Law** (England, USA) | **Continental / Civil Law** (France, Germany) |
|-----------|------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **Primary source of law** | Written law **+ judicial precedent** (case law) | A **written, codified** body of law (Civil Code, Criminal Code) |
| **Role of the judge** | Relatively **passive** — referee of the contest | **Active** — investigates the case; may use a special investigating judge in serious criminal matters |
| **Engine of the trial** | Witness examination & **cross-examination** by the attorneys | Written documents submitted in advance; attorney represents and files, does not investigate |
| **What decides the case** | **Persuasiveness** of arguments in court | Application of **pre-defined norms and procedures** |
| **Character** | Dynamic, flexible, precedent-driven | Predictable, systematic, code-driven |

> [!tip] One-line memory hook
> **Common Law trusts the *contest* (adversarial, judge-as-referee, precedent); Civil Law trusts the *code* (inquisitorial, judge-as-investigator, statute).** Israel borrows the adversarial machinery from the first and good-faith + codification from the second.

## 🧭 Fundamental Principles of Law

### Separation of Powers

Power is **divided between the branches of government** with **mutual oversight** so that no branch can dominate. The Israeli mechanisms of checks and balances include:

- The **State Comptroller** (audit of the executive)
- The **courts** (judicial review of the Knesset and government)
- The **media** — "the Fourth Estate"

> [!example] Montesquieu's justification
> *"Experience has always shown us that anyone who holds power is driven to abuse it… To prevent the misuse of power, it is necessary to arrange things so that **power checks power**."* — Montesquieu
> The separation of powers is not about efficiency; it is a deliberate, friction-creating design to **restrain** power.

### The Rule of Law

> [!info] Definition (Aharon Barak)
> *"All entities within the state — individuals, associations, and the branches of government — must act in accordance with the law. Any action contrary to the law must encounter the organised sanction of society."*
> The Rule of Law has a **dual meaning**: the **legality of government** (the state acts only by law) and the **supremacy of the law** (no one is above it). Former Chief Justice **Meir Shamgar** framed it as the *"protective wall against anarchy."*

The Rule of Law operates in two senses:

| Sense | Requirements | Idea |
|-------|-------------|------|
| **Formal** (the state) | Enforcement; equality before the law | The law is actually applied, and applied to everyone alike |
| **Substantive** | Publication; clarity; non-retroactivity | The law must be *knowable in advance* — published, clear, and not applied backwards in time |

**Operating principles of the Rule of Law:** basic law vs. regular law; specific law vs. general law; the principle of (non-)retroactive application; and the principle that **government acts only according to law**.

> [!warning] Why non-retroactivity matters to founders
> A regime that can criminalise yesterday's lawful conduct destroys the predictability businesses depend on. **Non-retroactivity** is what lets a founder rely on today's rules when making an investment decision.

## 📜 How Law Is Made: Legislation, Case Law & Precedent

Two channels produce binding Israeli law:

**1. Legislation (through bills):**
- **Governmental bills** — initiated by the executive
- **Private bills** — initiated by individual members of the Knesset

**2. Case Law (judge-made law):**
- Governed by the **principle of precedent** — the **Supreme Court** is the body that *creates* binding legal precedent.
- Within a judgment, two kinds of statement are distinguished:

| Term | Meaning | Binding? |
|------|---------|----------|
| ==Ratio decidendi== | The legal reasoning *essential* to the decision | **Yes** — this is the binding precedent |
| ==Obiter dictum== | Remarks said "in passing", not necessary to the outcome | **No** — persuasive only |

> [!tip] Why the ratio/obiter line is worth knowing
> When a lawyer cites a case, the *holding* (ratio) binds lower courts; the judge's side-comments (obiter) merely persuade. Reading a judgment as a founder or a law student means learning to **separate the binding rule from the commentary**.

## 📝 Summary

- A startup's legal needs evolve across four stages — **Pre-Incorporation → Incorporation → Investment Rounds → Exit** — and front-loading the cheap early work (founders' agreement, vesting, IP assignment, NDAs) prevents expensive failures later. ^startup-lifecycle
- Funding climbs a ladder — **Bootstrapping → Pre-Seed → Seed → A → B → C → IPO** — with cheque sizes rising and dilution falling as the company de-risks.
- Legal risk comes from two foundations: **soft law** (chosen, e.g. contracts) and **hard law** (mandatory, e.g. statutes). **Hard law always prevails.**
- The defining principle of public law: **government may act only when permitted; citizens are free unless prohibited.**
- Israel is a **mixed jurisdiction**, blending **Common Law** (adversarial procedure, evidence, agency) with **Continental Law** (good faith, codification); its norms form a hierarchy *Constitution → Laws → Regulations → Orders → private law*.
- The **Rule of Law** (formal: enforcement & equality; substantive: publication, clarity, non-retroactivity) and **Separation of Powers** (power checks power — comptroller, courts, media) restrain state power.
- Law is made by **legislation** (governmental/private bills) and **case law**; the **Supreme Court sets precedent**, and only the **ratio decidendi** binds — not **obiter dicta**.
