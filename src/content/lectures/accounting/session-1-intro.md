## 📚 2. Why Accounting Matters

> *"You have to understand accounting... it's the language of business and it's an imperfect language, but unless you are willing to put in the effort to learn accounting, how to read and interpret financial statements, you really shouldn't select stocks yourself."* (investor quote opening the deck)

Accounting is the central tool companies use to manage the business day to day. **Without recording, tracking and analysis of business activity, companies cannot exist.** Over time market efficiency would suffer and transaction costs would rise sharply.

> [!note] Lecturer's emphasis
> On the slide the lecturer circled **recording**, **tracking** and **analysis**: the three things accounting does.

### 2.1 The accounting "language": IFRS vs US GAAP

Like any language, accounting has rules, and different regions use different rule books. The two prominent ones:

| | **IFRS** | **US GAAP** |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | **Principles-based**: one standard per topic giving essential principles | **Rules-based**: extensive absolute quantitative thresholds |
| Management judgement | Leaves room for management to exercise judgement | Can still allow discretion (accounting policy) and differing opinions |
| Where | Most of the world (the deck's map shades most countries) | United States |

### 2.2 Users of financial information

Anyone who uses financial statements, budgets or costing models:

- Company **management and the board of directors**
- **Shareholders**
- **Lenders**
- **Potential investors**
- **Employees**
- **Authorities**: tax authorities, incentives
- **The public** (newspaper publications)

## 📚 3. The Financial Statements

Four reports together describe a company: the [[Balance Sheet]], the [[Income Statement]], the [[Cash Flows Statement]] and the [[Changes in Shareholders' Equity]] statement. (Session 2 adds the notes to the financial statements as a fifth component.)

| Statement | Timeframe | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| [[Balance Sheet]] | A **snapshot** at one day | What does the company own and owe? |
| [[Income Statement]] | A **period** | What was the profit or loss? |
| [[Changes in Shareholders' Equity]] | A specific day **and** a period | How did the owners' stake move? |
| [[Cash Flows Statement]] | A **period** | Where did cash come from and go? |

### 3.1 Balance Sheet

- Describes the financial position as a **snapshot on a particular day**.
- Three parts: **assets, liabilities and equity**, which together form the accounting equation.
- Built from **closing balances**, which are **cumulative from the company's inception**.

$$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$$

> [!note] Lecturer's handwriting
> On the scale picture he labelled the Assets side "Assets" and the other side **"Resources"** (right-hand label partly cut off; read as "Resources"). The idea: the left side is what the company has, the right side is who funded it (lenders as liabilities, owners as equity).

**Recording transactions on the balance sheet (the deck's example):**

| Date | Event | Assets | Liabilities | Equity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Jan 2025 | Shareholders invest \$10 million | Cash 10 | 0 | 10 |
| 1 Mar 2025 | Company takes a \$7 million bank loan | Cash 17 | Bank loan 7 | 10 |

Both sides rise by the same amount in each step, so the equation always balances.

### 3.2 Assets

> An **asset** is an economic resource controlled by the company as a result of past events, from which future economic benefits are expected to flow.

**Current assets** are cash or assets convertible to cash **within one year**:

| Item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cash and cash equivalents** | Cash on hand, bank balances and demand deposits, plus short-term highly liquid investments with insignificant risk of value change |
| **Marketable securities and deposits** | Short-term investments that can be sold quickly, and deposits that do not qualify as cash equivalents (typically redeemable after more than 3 months) |
| **Accounts receivable** | Amounts customers owe from credit sales not yet paid |
| **Inventory** | Goods made or bought for sale in the ordinary course of business |

> [!note] Lecturer's handwriting
> He added **"Prepaid expenses"** to the list of current assets (see [[Prepaid Expenses]]).

**Non-current assets** generate income for **longer than one year**:

- **Property, plant and equipment (PP&E)**: tangible items held for production, supply of goods or services, rental or administration and used for more than one period (land, factory, building, warehouse, machinery, equipment).
- **Intangible assets**: identifiable, non-monetary, no physical substance (patent, trademark, goodwill).

### 3.3 Liabilities

> A **liability** is a present obligation to an external party, arising from past events, whose settlement is expected to result in an outflow of economic benefits.

| Type | Items |
|---|---|
| **Current liabilities** (settled within one year) | **Accounts payable** (owed to suppliers on credit) · **Accrued expenses** (incurred but unpaid, see [[Accrued Expenses]]) · **Current portion of long-term debt** (the part due within the next year) · **Short-term loans** (maturity under one year) |
| **Non-current liabilities** (settled after more than one year) | **Long-term loans** (net of current maturities) · **Grants payable** (royalties owed on grants received in earlier years) |

### 3.4 Shareholders' equity

> The **residual interest** in the assets after deducting all liabilities.

| Group | Item | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Contributed capital** (invested by shareholders) | **Ordinary share capital** | Amount received for the **par value** of ordinary shares |
| | **Share premium** | Amount received above par (see also [[Additional Paid-in Capital]]) |
| | **Preferred shares** | Amounts received for shares with preferential rights, e.g. priority on dividends (see [[Preferred Stock]]) |
| | **Treasury shares** | The amount paid by the company to **repurchase its own shares** (see [[Treasury Shares]]) |
| **Earned capital** (earned by the company) | **Retained earnings** | Cumulative profits and losses **since inception**, less dividends distributed |
| | **AOCI** (accumulated other comprehensive income) | Accumulated gains and losses accounting has **not recognised in the income statement** |

> [!warning] Reconstructed from handwriting
> Next to share capital and share premium the lecturer scribbled **"\$1"**, **"999,999"** and what reads as **"100 @ 0.01 par"**. The most likely reading is a par-value example: 100 shares at \$0.01 par give \$1 of share capital, so if investors pay \$1,000,000 in total, **\$999,999** is share premium. This is my reconstruction of the scribble, not text on the slide.

### 3.5 Income Statement

- Covers a **period, not a day**.
- Lists all revenues and expenses recorded in that period. Its items are **not cumulative**: they describe only what happened in the period.
- Shown as a **waterfall**, from revenue down to profit or loss.

**Revenue/Sales, minus Cost of Sales, gives Gross profit; minus Operating expenses, gives Operating profit; minus Financing expenses and Taxes, gives Net income.**

| Key line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Revenue / Sales** | Revenue from sales or services recognised in the period |
| **Gross profit** | Revenue minus the cost of producing or purchasing the goods or providing the service (cost of goods sold) |
| **Operating profit (EBIT)** | Gross profit minus operating expenses not directly tied to sales. Excludes financing expenses and taxes |
| **Net profit (net income)** | Reflects the company's total business activity in the period |
| **Earnings per share (EPS)** | Profitability per share, to compare with other companies |

> [!note] Lecturer's handwriting
> Beside EPS he wrote **"P/E"**, linking EPS to the price-to-earnings ratio used to value shares.

### 3.6 Statement of Changes in Shareholders' Equity

- Reports both **a specific day and a period**.
- A **matrix**: it opens with the **opening balance** of each equity item, shows the events that moved it (share issues, dividends and the period's net income or loss), and closes with the **closing balance**.
- It is the **connection between the balance sheet and the income statement**: net income (an income statement item) appears as a movement in **retained earnings** (a balance sheet item).

### 3.7 Cash Flows Statement

- Covers a **period, not a day**.
- Describes the economic events that occurred **in cash**: the sources and uses of cash.
- Three groups of activities: **operating**, **investing** and **financing**. It also discloses material **non-cash transactions**.

> [!note] Lecturer's handwriting
> Next to "Operating activities" he wrote **"Indirect"**: operating cash flow is normally presented by the indirect method, starting from net income and adjusting for non-cash items and working capital (worked through in the mock exams, e.g. [[Sample Final D]]).

### 3.8 How the statements connect

The deck's closing diagram (annotated by the lecturer with **12/23** for the opening balance sheet and **12/24** for the closing one):

1. **Opening balance sheet**: cash, other assets, liabilities and equity at the start.
2. **Income statement**: revenues minus expenses give **net income**, which flows into retained earnings.
3. **Statement of changes in equity**: opening equity plus share issues and net income, less dividends, gives the change in equity.
4. **Cash flow statement**: operating + investing + financing = **change in cash during the period**.
5. **Closing balance sheet**: opening cash plus the change in cash is closing cash; opening equity plus the change in equity is closing equity.

| Opening balance sheet | + Movement | = Closing balance sheet |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | Cash flow statement (change in cash) | Cash |
| Equity | Statement of changes in equity (incl. net income) | Equity |

## 📚 4. Quick Reference

| Term | Plain-English definition |
|---|---|
| **IFRS** | Principles-based accounting standard used in most of the world |
| **US GAAP** | Rules-based US standard with quantitative thresholds |
| [[Balance Sheet]] | Snapshot of assets, liabilities and equity on one day |
| **Current vs non-current** | Converted or settled within one year, or beyond it |
| **Par value** | Nominal value per share; the amount above it is share premium |
| [[Treasury Shares]] | Company's own shares it bought back; reduces equity |
| **Retained earnings** | Cumulative profits kept in the company since inception |
| **AOCI** | Gains and losses not yet recognised in the income statement |
| **EBIT** | Operating profit: before interest and taxes |
| **EPS** | Net income per share |
