## Household consumption

Consumption depends on **disposable income** and the **interest rate**. Disposable income is split between consumption and saving: $Y^d = C + s_P$.

$$
MPC + MPS = 1
$$

The **marginal propensity to consume** is the share of each extra unit of disposable income that is consumed. The **marginal propensity to save** is the share that is saved.

$$
C = C_0 + c_1 Y^d - c_2 r,
\qquad
s_P = -C_0 + (1 - c_1) Y^d + c_2 r
$$

## Life-Cycle Hypothesis (Modigliani, 1954)

People plan over a lifetime and **smooth consumption** across periods of high and low income.

- Consumption depends on lifetime income, expected earnings and age.
- **Young people borrow, the middle-aged save and retirees dissave.**
- Pension systems and demographic shifts matter for national saving.

## Permanent Income Hypothesis (Friedman, 1957)

People base consumption on **permanent income**, the average they expect over the long term.

- Temporary income changes have small or no effects on consumption.
- Permanent income changes have larger effects.
- So **temporary tax cuts or transfers have limited impact**.

## The Malthusian trap

Malthus argued that population growth cancels gains in income:

1. Better technology raises output and income for a while.
2. Higher income raises population.
3. More people share the resources, so income per person falls.

This describes most of history before the Industrial Revolution, when sustained technological growth finally outpaced population growth.

## Investment

Gross investment is spending on physical assets for future production. Net investment subtracts depreciation. Drivers are current sales relative to capacity, expected future sales and the interest rate.

$$
I = I_0 + b_1 r + b_2 Y
$$

> [!warning] Sign on the slide
> The slide writes $+\,b_1 r$ while saying investment **falls** with the interest rate. For that to hold, $b_1$ must be negative.

## Government spending

Spending on goods and services used to provide public services. It excludes transfer payments and is treated as **autonomous**.

## Exchange rates

The **nominal exchange rate** $e$ is the price of one currency in another, for example 1 USD = 3.5 ILS. The **real exchange rate** adjusts for price levels:

$$
\text{Real exchange rate} = \frac{e \cdot P_{\text{foreign}}}{P_{\text{domestic}}}
$$

**Exports** rise with a real depreciation, because domestic goods become cheaper for foreigners. **Imports** rise with a real appreciation and with output.

$$
EX = X_0 + x_1 \cdot \frac{e P_f}{P_d},
\qquad
IM = IM_0 - m_1 \cdot \frac{e P_f}{P_d} + m_2 Y
$$
