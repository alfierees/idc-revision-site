## Government deficit and the interest rate

Assume household saving does not depend on the interest rate. The government raises spending and funds it with a deficit.

1. Public saving falls, so **supply shifts left** by the deficit.
2. Higher spending raises household income, and part is saved, so supply shifts **part of the way back**.
3. The rise in the deficit exceeds the rise in household saving, so total saving falls.
4. **The interest rate rises and investment falls.**

The slides also show the same result as a demand shift: the government's borrowing raises demand for funds, the rate rises, and private investment falls.

## Open economy

In an open economy, a gap between saving and investment is filled by net exports:

$$
S = I + NX
$$

- **World rate above the closed-economy rate:** $I$ falls, so $S > I$ and $NX > 0$.
- **World rate below the closed-economy rate:** $I$ rises, so $S < I$ and $NX < 0$.

## Interest rates and trade

When rates rise in a small economy, foreign investors bring money in. The local currency appreciates, so **imports rise and exports fall**. When rates fall, funds flow out, the currency depreciates, **imports fall and exports rise**.

## Balance of payments

The balance of payments summarises all economic activity with the rest of the world.

| Account | Item | Debit (outflow) | Credit (inflow) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Current** | Goods and services | Imports | Exports |
| **Current** | Unilateral transfers | Giving a gift | Receiving a gift |
| **Capital** | Capital movements | Lending and investing abroad | Foreign loans and investment |
| **Capital** | Foreign currency reserves | Increase in reserves | Decrease in reserves |

### Worked entries from the slides

| Transaction | Entry 1 | Entry 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Israel receives a USD 500M US grant | Credit, unilateral transfers | Debit, reserves increase |
| Consumers import USD 100M | Debit, goods and services | Credit, reserves decrease |
| A foreign resident invests USD 100M | Credit, capital movements | Debit, reserves increase |

Every transaction has two sides, so **the balance of payments always sums to zero**.
