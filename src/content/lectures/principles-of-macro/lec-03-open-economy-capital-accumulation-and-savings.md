## Value added in an open economy

Imported inputs are purchases from a firm, so they reduce value added.

- If the bread firm imports barley for 3, its value added falls by 3 and output falls from 57 to 54.
- If the wheat firm also exports wheat worth 4, output rises by 4.
- If the bread firm **switches** 3 of its domestic sales to exports, output does not change.

**Outsourcing does not change output.** If an agency supplies the wheat firm's workers for 7, the wheat firm's value added falls by 7 and the agency's value added rises by 7. Total output stays at 40.

**A price change between firms does not change output.** If the wheat price paid by the bread firm rises from 20 to 22, value added moves from bread to wheat and the total is still 40.

## The savings identity

Start from the national accounts identity:

$$
Y = C + I + G + NX
$$

Add and subtract net taxes $T$ and rearrange:

$$
\underbrace{(Y - T - C)}_{s_P} + \underbrace{(T - G)}_{s_G} = I + NX
$$

where disposable income is $Y^d = Y - T$, private savings are $s_P = Y^d - C$ and public savings are $s_G = T - G$.

$$
S = s_P + s_G = I + NX
$$

In a closed economy, $NX = 0$ and so $S = I$. Here $I$ is domestic investment and $NX$ is the economy's investment in foreign economies.

## Two corrections

**Net income from foreign factors.** NIFF is what a country's citizens produce abroad minus what foreign citizens produce domestically. If it is not zero:

$$
S = I + NX - NIFF
$$

> [!warning] Check the sign of NIFF
> This is the formula as printed on the slide. The slide's wording defines NIFF as income earned **abroad by citizens** minus income earned **here by foreigners**, and with that definition national saving would be $I + NX + NIFF$. The minus sign fits the opposite definition, net income **to foreign factors**, where $GNP = GDP - NIFF$. Check which one the lecturer intends.

**Undistributed profits.** Profit a firm keeps is not household income:

$$
Y^d = Y - T - \text{undistributed profits}
$$

Those retained profits are **business savings**, so private saving is household saving plus firm saving.

## Gross and net investment

$$
\text{Net investment} = \text{gross investment} - \text{depreciation}
$$

If 30 buildings are built and 5 old ones collapse, gross investment is 30 and net investment is 25.

## What GDP misses

GDP is strongly correlated with other measures of well-being, such as health, education, life expectancy, child mortality and access to clean water. It still leaves out or mismeasures **leisure, illegal activity, natural resources and quality**.
