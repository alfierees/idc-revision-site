## What is economic growth?

Economic growth is an **increase in the economy's production capacity**. It comes from better technology or more resources. Higher GDP per capita is associated with a higher standard of living.

Growth depends on:

1. The **quantity and quality of resources**: human capital, physical capital, natural resources.
2. The **quality of public policy and institutions**: property rights, political stability, trade policy, credit rating and investor trust.
3. **Security and economic resilience**.

## The production function

$$
Y = F(K, L)
$$

The model assumes one homogeneous type of capital, a homogeneous labour force and a homogeneous output. As capital rises with labour fixed, output rises **at a decreasing rate**.

## Marginal product

The marginal product of a factor is the extra output from one more unit of it, holding the other factor constant:

$$
MPL = \frac{\partial F(K,L)}{\partial L},
\qquad
MPK = \frac{\partial F(K,L)}{\partial K}
$$

For $F(K,L) = 3K^2L^4$, the slides give $\partial Y/\partial L = 12K^2L^3$ and $\partial Y/\partial K = 6KL^4$.

**Why poor countries can grow faster.** With little capital, a small investment raises productivity a lot. With a lot of capital, more investment adds only a little. That is diminishing returns.

## The labour market

In a perfectly competitive market, the real wage equals the marginal product of labour:

$$
\frac{W}{P} = MPL \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W = P \cdot MPL
$$

The MPL curve is the **labour demand curve**. Labour supply slopes up. Their intersection gives $L_0$, the real wage and output $Y = F(K_0, L_0)$.

The area under the MPL curve up to $L_0$ is total output. The rectangle below the wage is paid to workers, and the triangle above it goes to capital owners.

> [!note] Capital market
> It works the same way with one difference: in the short run **capital supply is fixed**, so it is a vertical line.

## Average product and factor shares

$$
APL = \frac{Y}{L},
\qquad
\frac{(W/P)\,L}{Y} = \frac{MPL}{APL},
\qquad
\frac{(R/P)\,K}{Y} = \frac{MPK}{APK}
$$

**Exogenous** variables are taken as given: the production function, capital $K_0$, labour supply. **Endogenous** variables are determined by the model: $L_0$, $Y_0$, the real wage and the real return to capital.

## Sources of growth in practice

- **Human capital:** education, skills, health. It has positive externalities, an opportunity cost and a risk of brain drain.
- **Physical capital:** domestic saving, plus foreign investment. [[Foreign Direct Investment]] is owned and operated by foreigners, while foreign portfolio investment is funded by foreigners and run by locals.
- **Natural resources:** a possible limit, though technology, recycling and new materials ease it.
- **R&D:** knowledge is a public good, non-rival and non-excludable, so the private sector underinvests. Governments respond with direct funding, grants, tax incentives and patents.
