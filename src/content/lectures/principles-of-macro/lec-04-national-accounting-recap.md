## Nominal and real GDP

GDP is measured at current prices. If prices rise, measured GDP rises even with no extra production.

$$
\pi = \frac{P_{t+s}}{P_t} - 1,
\qquad
RGDP = \frac{GDP}{1 + \pi}
$$

**Worked example from the slides.** The price index rises from 140 to 147, and GDP rises from 5,000 to 5,500.

$$
\pi = \frac{147}{140} - 1 = 5\%,
\qquad
RGDP_{2021} = \frac{5{,}500}{1.05} \approx 5{,}238
$$

Nominal growth is 10%, but real output grew by only about 4.8%.

## Value added and GDP: the recap table

| Entity | Output | Inputs from firms | Wages | Rent | Taxes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Firm A | 200 | 0 | 70 | 30 | 100 |
| Firm B | 150 | 50 | 60 | 25 | 15 |
| Government | | 20 | 80 | 20 | |

$$
VA_A = 200,\quad VA_B = 150 - 50 = 100,\quad VA_G = 80 + 20 = 100,\quad GDP = 400
$$

**Inventory check.** If Firm A's inventory rises by 20, its value added and GDP both rise by 20.

## Income side

$$
GDP = \text{wages} + \text{interest} + \text{rent} + \text{net profit} + \text{net taxes on production} + \text{dividends}
$$

With the table above, wages are 210, rent is 75, both firms' profit is 0 and taxes on production are 115. The total is $210 + 75 + 115 = 400$.

## From gross to net

$$
NDP = GDP - \text{depreciation}
$$

If GDP is 400 and NDP is 360, depreciation is 40, so the slide's "depreciation = 65" statement is false for this data.

> [!tip] Read every number carefully
> Recap questions often offer an answer that uses sales instead of value added, or that forgets government value added.
