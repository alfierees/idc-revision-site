The LM curve shows all combinations of $r$ and $Y$ at which the money market clears, $M/P = L(r, Y)$. It slopes up: higher income raises money demand, and with a fixed money supply $r$ must rise to restore equilibrium. Higher $M$ shifts it right (down); higher money demand shifts it left (up).

**LM\* (open economy, Lec 09).** In the [[Mundell-Fleming Model]] with $r = r^*$ the equation becomes $M/P = L(r^*, Y)$, which only one value of $Y$ satisfies whatever $e$ is. So LM\* is a vertical line in $(Y, e)$ space. It shifts right with higher $M$ and left with a higher $r^*$. Under a fixed exchange rate the central bank moves LM\* itself to defend the peg.

## When to use

In closed-economy questions LM carries monetary policy and money-demand shocks. In small-open-economy questions LM\* fixes $Y$ under floating rates, which is why fiscal policy is ineffective there and monetary policy works.
