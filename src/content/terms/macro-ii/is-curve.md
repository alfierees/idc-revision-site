The IS curve shows all combinations of the interest rate $r$ and output $Y$ at which the goods market clears, $Y = C(Y-T) + I(r) + G$. It slopes down: a lower $r$ raises investment and planned expenditure, so equilibrium $Y$ rises. Fiscal policy shifts it (higher $G$ or lower $T$ moves it right).

**IS\* (open economy, Lec 09).** In the [[Mundell-Fleming Model]] the interest rate is pinned at $r = r^*$, so the goods-market curve is drawn in $(Y, e)$ space: $Y = C(Y-T) + I(r^*) + G + NX(e)$. It also slopes down, because a higher $e$ (stronger domestic currency) lowers [[Net Exports]]. It shifts right with higher $G$, lower $T$ or an import restriction, and left when $r^*$ rises.

## When to use

In closed-economy questions use IS with LM to find $(Y^*, r^*)$. In small-open-economy questions use IS\* with LM\*: a fiscal expansion shifts IS\* right and the effect on $Y$ depends on the exchange-rate regime.
