Changes in the money supply affect only nominal variables (prices, nominal wages, nominal GDP) and not real variables (real GDP, real wages, employment). It follows from the quantity theory: $M$ rises, $P$ rises proportionally and $Y$ is unchanged.

## When to use

Use it for long-run classical questions on money. A one-time rise in $M$ gives a one-time price-level rise, not permanent inflation unless $M$ keeps outgrowing $Y$.
