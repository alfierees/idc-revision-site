Higher government spending raises the interest rate and reduces private investment, so income rises by less than the Keynesian multiplier. In the classical model, where $Y$ is fixed, the crowding out is one for one.

## When to use

Use it to explain why the IS-LM multiplier is smaller than $1/(1-\text{MPC})$, and in the Reagan-deficit case where lower saving raises $r$ and cuts $I$. In the open economy floating-rate case, the crowding out falls on net exports instead.
