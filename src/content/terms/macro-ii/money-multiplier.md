The amount by which the money supply expands per extra unit of monetary base, $m = (1+cr)/(cr+rr)$, so $M = m \cdot B$. In the simple case with no currency holdings it is $1/rr$: each deposit is re-lent and re-deposited.

## When to use

Use it for calculation questions such as a 100 rise in $B$ with $cr = 0.5$ and $rr = 0.1$ giving $m = 2.5$. It also explains why the Fed cannot control $M$ precisely: households set $cr$ and banks set $rr$ (Great Depression, QE).
