From $i = r + E\pi$: because the real rate is fixed by saving and investment, a one point rise in expected inflation raises the nominal interest rate by exactly one point and leaves $r$ unchanged.

## When to use

Use it to find $i$ from $r$ and inflation (for example $r = 4\%$, $\pi = 3\%$ gives $i = 7\%$). The long-run result contrasts with the short-run IS-LM result that higher $M$ lowers the interest rate.

See [[Lec_05-Inflation|Lec 05]].
