The nominal interest rate minus inflation, $r = i - \pi$. It is the real cost of borrowing, investment falls as it rises, and in the classical model it adjusts to equate national saving and investment.

## When to use

Use it with the Fisher equation $i = r + E\pi$: the real rate is set in the loanable funds market, so when expected inflation changes the nominal rate moves one for one.
