Keynes's theory that the interest rate adjusts to equate real money supply and real money demand. The central bank fixes $M/P$ (a vertical supply line) and demand falls with $r$, so an excess demand for money makes people sell bonds, which pushes $r$ up.

## When to use

Use it for the short-run effect of monetary policy: a rise in $M$ lowers $r$. This looks like a conflict with the long-run Fisher effect, but the two apply to sticky and flexible prices respectively (Volcker case).
