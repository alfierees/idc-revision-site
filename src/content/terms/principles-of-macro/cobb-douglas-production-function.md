The production function $Y = A K^{\alpha} L^{1-\alpha}$ with $0 < \alpha < 1$. The exponents sum to one, so it has constant returns to scale, and $\alpha$ is both capital's [[Output Elasticity]] and its income share. Seen in: [Macro-Economics II](/subjects/macro-ii), [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics).

## When to use

Differentiate to get $MPL = (1-\alpha)A(K/L)^{\alpha}$ and $MPK = \alpha A(L/K)^{1-\alpha}$. Scale all inputs by $t$ and add the exponents to read returns to scale, or scale one input by $(1+g)$ to find that output changes by $(1+g)$ raised to that input's exponent.

See [[Rec_05-Production at Full Employment — Cobb-Douglas, Marginal Products & Returns to Scale|Rec 05]]. Also in [[Rec_06-The Labor & Capital Markets, Factor Payments & Growth Accounting|Rec 06]], [[Read_01-Formula Sheet — Solow, Growth Accounting & the Open Economy|Read 01]].
