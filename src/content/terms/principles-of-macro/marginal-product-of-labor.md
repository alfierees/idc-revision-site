The marginal product of labor is the extra output from one more unit of labor, holding capital fixed: $MPL = \partial Y/\partial L$. With Cobb-Douglas it is $MPL = (1-\alpha) A (K/L)^{\alpha}$, which falls as labor rises and rises as capital rises. Seen in: [Macro-Economics](/subjects/macro-economics).

## When to use

Differentiate the production function to get it, or read its sign off the ratio $K/L$. In equilibrium it equals the [[Real Wage]], $W/P = MPL$, and its curve is [[Labor Demand]].
