Implicit conversion of a value to another type during an operation so both operands are compatible, for example an `int` converted to a `float` when the two are compared. Casting, such as `int(5.6)`, is the explicit version; see [[Type Casting]].

## When to use

It explains why comparing an [[Integer]] with a [[Float]] works. `True` and `False` also act as 1 and 0 in arithmetic, e.g. `True + False` gives 1.

See [[Lec_02-Numeric Values and Strings|Lec 02]].
