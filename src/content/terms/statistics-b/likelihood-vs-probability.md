Likelihoods attach to parameter values (hypotheses), not outcomes, and do not sum to 1; probabilities attach to outcomes and do sum to 1.

## When to use

In the two-bags tables, reading along a row (one bag, varying outcome) gives probabilities that sum to 1, while reading down a column (one outcome, varying bag) gives likelihoods that do not. A likelihood is not the probability that a hypothesis is true, so only comparisons between likelihoods are meaningful.
