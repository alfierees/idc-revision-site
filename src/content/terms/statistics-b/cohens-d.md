Cohen's $d$ is the gap between the true and null means measured in standard deviation units, $d=(\mu_{H_1}-\mu_{H_0})/SD$. Larger effects are easier to detect, so power rises with $d$.

## When to use

The standard deviation matches the test: $\sigma_X$ for a Z test, $S_X$ for a single sample t test, and $S_{pooled}$ for a two independent samples t test. Roughly 0.2 is small, 0.5 medium and 0.8 large, and the extra-credit slide asks for it next to the test result.
