The problem that arises when a person receives the benefit of a good but avoids paying for it.

## When to use

Explains why private markets under-provide [[Public Goods]]: if people cannot be excluded, everyone waits for someone else to pay and nobody does. Lec 13 lists it under both non-excludable cells (public goods and common resources). If the benefit exceeds the cost, the government should provide the good and pay for it with a tax, though measuring the benefit is hard.

See [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]].
