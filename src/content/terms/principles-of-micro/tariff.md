A tariff is a tax on goods produced abroad and sold domestically, such as imported steel. It raises the domestic price above the [[World Price]] by the amount of the tariff and imports fall.

## When to use

In the lecture's figure (world price 4, tariff 1) the price with the tariff is 5, and the rectangle E is tariff revenue (tariff per unit times imports). It moves the market back towards the no-trade equilibrium and creates a [[Deadweight Loss]].

See [[Lec_13-Public Goods and International Trade|Lec 13]].
