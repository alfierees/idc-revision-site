A rare non-luxury [[Inferior Good]] whose quantity demanded rises when its price rises, with income held constant. Poor households spend so much on the staple that a price rise eats their budget, so they cut dearer foods and buy more of the staple (potatoes and rice are the slide examples).

## When to use

One of two demand curves that break the [[Law of Demand]] (Lec 04). Do not confuse it with a [[Veblen Good]]: a Giffen good slopes upward because the buyer is poor, a Veblen good because the buyer wants status.

See [[Lec_04-Supply and Market Equilibrium|Lec 04]].
