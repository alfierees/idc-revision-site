A duty to act in an accepted manner and honestly. In contracts it applies at negotiation (s.12, which makes a party who negotiates in bad faith liable for the damage caused, even before a contract forms), at performance and gap-filling (s.39). Good faith is also one of the Continental (civil law) features of Israeli law.

## When to use

Use it when a party backs out of talks or relies on a missing formality: s.12 for bad-faith negotiation, s.39 as a completion mechanism for gaps, and Kalmer v Gai where good faith relaxes the writing requirement. It is also a condition of the market overt rule.
