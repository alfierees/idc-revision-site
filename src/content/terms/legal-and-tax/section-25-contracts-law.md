The statutory rule for interpreting contracts. Originally it said to read the contract's own language first and turn to the circumstances only if intent was unclear; the 2011 amendment reads language and circumstances together, but where intent is explicit in the language, the language governs. It also prefers a reading that upholds validity and, against a party with a drafting advantage, the reading against that party (contra proferentem).

## When to use

Use it in interpretation questions, to show the path from the original two-step rule through the [[Apropim Doctrine]] to the 2011 text, and how gaps are filled by custom (s.26) and good faith (s.39).
