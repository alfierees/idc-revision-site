CA 4263/04 asked whether thin capitalization alone is enough to subordinate a shareholder's debt to the company's other creditors. The court held that where thin capitalization combines with improper management, repayment of shareholder loans can be delayed until external creditors are paid in full (Section 6(c)), so the loans are treated as if they were equity.

## When to use

Use it for questions on shareholder loans versus creditors: it is the example of the subordination doctrine, a gentler alternative to piercing the veil that re-orders the queue of creditors without making shareholders personally liable.
