One of the conditions for a contract: both parties must mean the deal to be legally binding (CA 440/75 Zandbank v Danziger). Courts use a theoretical test (did the parties decide to create legal obligations, Levin) and a practical test that infers intent from the language and the circumstances together. Substance beats wording.

## When to use

Use it when a deal looks social, political or hedged with a disclaimer. Example in the lecture: two MKs agreeing to vote for a bill have consent but no intention to create legal relations, so no contract. Levin v Levin shows an express no-legal-effect clause does not defeat genuine intent.
