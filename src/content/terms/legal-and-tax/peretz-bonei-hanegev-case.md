CA 1932/90: Bochbot contracted to sell his apartment in three installments, then claimed he could not read or write and thought he was signing for 1,000 dollars of compensation, not a sale (he withdrew the illiteracy argument on appeal). The case tests whether the objective act of signing reflected true intention to sell, and the lecture groups it with Barashi to show the objective test bends for signers with impaired understanding.

## When to use

Use it, together with Barashi, in serious-intention (gmirot da'at) questions where one party says they did not understand what they signed.
