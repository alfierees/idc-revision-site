If bundle A is weakly preferred to B and B to C, then A is weakly preferred to C; it is what stops [[Indifference Curve]]s crossing.

## When to use

Use it to prove that indifference curves cannot cross, and to chain direct comparisons into indirect ones in revealed preference. Without it a consumer could cycle and there would be no most preferred bundle.

See [[Lec_02-Preferences|Lec 02]].
