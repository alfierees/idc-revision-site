The extra amount paid after one period for each unit saved or borrowed: saving one dollar today gives $1+r$ dollars tomorrow, so it sets the slope $-(1+r)$ of the intertemporal budget line.

## When to use

At the optimum the [[Marginal Rate of Substitution]] equals $1+r$. A higher rate rotates the budget line outward; the [[Substitution Effect]] raises saving and the [[Income Effect]] lowers it, so theory alone does not say which wins.
