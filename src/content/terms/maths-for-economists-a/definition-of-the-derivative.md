The limit $\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$ itself, used to differentiate from first principles. The limit is two-sided, so the quotient's limits as $h\to 0^-$ and $h\to 0^+$ must agree.

## When to use

Use it when asked to find a derivative "from the definition" (for $\sqrt{x}$ multiply by the conjugate to get $\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$) and to test differentiability at a join, such as $f'(2)$ for $\lvert x-2\rvert\cdot\sqrt[3]{x-2}$.

See [[Lec_07-The Derivative, Differentiability and Rules|Lec 07]].
