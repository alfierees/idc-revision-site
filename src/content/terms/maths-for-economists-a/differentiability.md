A function is differentiable at a point when the derivative exists as a finite number, so the one-sided slopes exist and agree. Differentiable implies continuous, so the failures are jumps, corners, cusps and vertical tangents.

## When to use

Run the routine at a point: domain, then continuity, then left and right derivatives. Matching slopes with a continuity failure is the trap, and a continuous function can still fail, as with a corner.
