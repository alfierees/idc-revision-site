An isoquant that bows inward: the midpoint of two bundles on the same isoquant lies on a higher isoquant, so a blend of inputs beats an extreme bundle. It follows from [[Diminishing RTS]].

## When to use

Use it to argue that a firm prefers diversity in inputs. Lecture example: with $f=l\cdot k$, the bundles $(4,2)$ and $(2,4)$ give 8 but their midpoint $(3,3)$ gives 9.

See [[PS_02-Isoquants, RTS & Properties of the Profit Function|PS 02]].
