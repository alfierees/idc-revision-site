An agent's utility that also counts part of the other agent's utility: $V_1(g) + y_1 + \alpha\,(V_2(g) + y_2)$ with $\alpha \le 1$. Because she internalises some of the other's benefit, equilibrium provision of a [[Public Goods|public good]] rises, and at $\alpha = 1$ it equals the social optimum.

## When to use

Appears in the public-goods problem set when asked to show that altruism raises provision. The lecture only states the results and leaves the proof to the problem set, so derive it there rather than quoting a formula.
