Differentiate $F(x,y) = 0$ with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a function of $x$ and putting a $y'$ on every $y$ term, then solve for $y'$.

## When to use

Use it when $y$ is not isolated and you need a slope or a [[Tangent Line]], for example on $x^2+y^2=25$ or on a level curve. Find the $y$ values at the given $x$ first, since the slope usually depends on both. Common slips are forgetting $y'$ ($\frac{d}{dx}y^2 = 2yy'$) and missing the product rule on terms like $xy^2$.

See [[Lec_04-Implicit Differentiation and Two-Variable Functions|Lec 04]]. Also in [[Lec_06-Partial Derivatives and the Differential|Lec 06]].
