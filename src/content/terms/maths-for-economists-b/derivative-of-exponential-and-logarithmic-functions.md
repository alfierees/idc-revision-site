$(a^x)' = a^x \ln a$ and $(\log_a x)' = \dfrac{1}{x \ln a}$, which reduce to $(e^x)' = e^x$ and $(\ln x)' = 1/x$.

## When to use

Needed whenever [[L'Hopital's Rule]] is applied to exponential or log limits, and for tangent-line questions. With the chain rule, $\frac{d}{dx}e^{g(x)} = g'(x)e^{g(x)}$ and $\frac{d}{dx}\ln g(x) = g'(x)/g(x)$. The lecture notes mark these rules as supplied because the slide area was blank.

See [[Lec_03-Exponential and Logarithmic Limits and Asymptotes|Lec 03]].
